What is the Banya Equation / What is the Banya Framework

The Banya Framework is an axiom-based science mining engine. It starts from 15 axioms, derives physical constants and laws in the forward direction, and judges the results against measured values and falsification conditions.

ItemContent
Input15 axioms, measured physical constants, library cards already judged
ProcessFive-step recursive substitution (Banya equation, norm substitution, constant substitution, domain conversion, finding)
OutputOne card. The verdict is one of discovery, hypothesis, prediction, retired (hypothesis library, unique predictions)
CriteriaError against measured values, and falsification conditions attached to values not yet measured
What it is notNot a Theory of Everything and not a replacement for existing physics. The one-line Banya equation by itself produces no numbers

The Banya Equation and the Banya Framework are different.

Banya Equation, a 4-axis orthogonal norm in one line

The Banya Equation comes from the Heart Sutra (Banya心經). "Everything changes except change itself" becomes δ (total variation), and "form is emptiness, emptiness is form" (色卽是空 空卽是色) is rewritten as the orthogonality of classical (time+space) and quantum (observer+superposition). The insight is transcribed directly into the language and formulas of computer science:

$$\delta^{2} = (time + space)^{2} + (observer + superposition)^{2}$$
$\delta$: change | time: time | space: space | observer: observation | superposition: superposition

The Banya equation is one line made of 4 words and 2 squares. It is a structural declaration that all change in the universe is the norm of 4 axes. Every derivation starts here. The Banya equation declares only topology (what is orthogonal to what); distance, sign and unit are fixed when constants are substituted (Axiom 1 Proposition).

Banya Framework, the engine that runs the equation

It is a tool system that takes the Banya Equation as its engine, fixes CAS (Read-Compare-Swap) as the sole atomic operator, and bounds resources by finite cost (≤+1, Axiom 4). It is closed by 15 axioms and propositions and imports 0 external equations.

Why CAS is the sole operator. The δ in the Banya Equation deals only with one change. Change is defined only when the pre-change state and the post-change state are compared at the same time. CAS is the only atomic operator that handles two states inside one operation, and therefore the only operator that brings forth order and change. Regardless of which domain (time, space, observer, superposition) CAS operates through, the atomic operator at the bottom is the same CAS.

Framework = Banya Equation + 1 CAS + description DOF 9 (fixed) + 1 kind of 1D ring (fixed) + cost

The equation declares the structure, and the framework substitutes constants into that structure to produce numbers.

Fitting versus Learning

How the axioms were made

The axioms were learned. The 15 axioms were completed by design before mining. While more than 1,700 physics equations were then mined, the axiom system was shaped like the weights of a trained model. The 9 rounds of shaping up to v1.9 were done by the hand of the axiom author, keeping the 15 axioms fixed and adding propositions.

Agreement with already known values, recovered deductively in the domain where they already lived, is therefore a necessary result of the learning, and the framework regards it as post-hoc agreement. By contrast, a relation or value that did not exist before, obtained by converting into an empty domain, is judged a discovery. That 15 fixed axioms cover that many equations is the achievement of the learning; the discriminating power lies in empty-domain discoveries and in predictions of unmeasured values made with the learned axioms (unique predictions).

Beyond wiring up what has been seen and deducing from it, the work of the axioms is to infer what was never seen. The learned axioms have staked more than 600 values not yet measured, each with falsification conditions. Every card carries its creation date and the public repository keeps its history, so the record proves these inferences were made before measurement. That 15 fixed axioms extend inference this far into unseen territory is where the value of the Banya Framework shows.

Grading, however, can only happen after measurement. Having inferred what was not seen does not mean every inference is right. Each prediction is judged by experiment one at a time, and wrong ones are kept with the reason for retirement attached. Some predictions have already been retired by the framework itself, through falsification or internal inconsistency.

It is announced in advance that AI will hereafter shape the weights of the axioms by itself, on the basis of mining results. At that stage the axioms are to be wrapped in the Lean theorem prover.

This learning differs from free-parameter fitting in 2 ways.

  1. The weights are integers. There are no adjustable continuous knobs. The weights are the integer lists registered in Axiom 9 (structural 1, 2, 3, 4, 7, 9, 16, 30, 128, 137 / cost 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 9, 13).
  2. The weights are shared. The same integers recur in cards from different domains: masses, mixing angles, cosmology, black holes. They are not tuned card by card, so changing one integer throws cards in several domains off together.

Common Misconceptions

Misconception 1. The Banya equation is a Theory of Everything (TOE)

Fact: the Banya equation is not a theory but a framework.

A theory explains "why" and predicts specific numbers. A framework defines "how far" and checks whether existing theories fit inside it.

Theory: E = mc² ==> 1 kg of mass gives 9×10^{16} J of energy (numerical prediction)
Framework: E = mc² lives in the classical bracket (time, space) (location check)

Example: the Pythagorean theorem declares a structure, "the square of the hypotenuse equals the sum of the squares of the other two sides." It does not say whether a side is 3 or 5. The Banya equation is the same.

The one-line Banya equation by itself produces no numbers. Numbers are obtained by substituting constants into the framework, so one says "derived with the Banya Framework," not "predicted by the Banya equation." Saying the equation predicts confuses it with a theory.

Misconception 2. The Banya equation replaces existing physics

Fact: existing physics equations work unchanged inside the Banya Framework.

Einstein's $E^2 = (mc^2)^2 + (pc)^2$ is an app in the classical bracket. The Schrödinger equation is an app in the quantum bracket. The framework does not replace the apps; it describes the OS they run on.

Before: relativity app and quantum app separate, incompatible
Banya Framework: both apps run on the same OS. The two brackets are orthogonal, so no conflict

Example: Excel did not disappear when Windows arrived. Excel runs on Windows.

Misconception 3. Four axes means four-dimensional spacetime

Fact: spacetime is the time, space side of the classical bracket. The other two axes (observer, superposition) are the quantum bracket and are not spacetime.

Classical bracket: time, space ==> spacetime (Einstein's domain)
Quantum bracket: observer, superposition ==> quantum state (Heisenberg's domain)
The two are orthogonal ==> no need to merge

The observed 3+1 dimensions come from the single space axis unfolding into three components. Axiom 2 Proposition text: "The space axis is unfolded into 3 components (x, y, z) by the action of the CAS 3-axis orthogonal locks (R_LOCK, C_LOCK, S_LOCK)." And the Wyler appendix of the axiom document: "The subframe (3,1) is observable spacetime."

Example: a car's speedometer and fuel gauge are independent dials. The fuel gauge does not change because the speedometer rises. Yet both describe the state of the car.

Misconception 4. δ is energy

Fact: energy is only one way of measuring δ. Seen from inside the FSM, δ appears as change; in Axiom 15 the identity of δ is firing.

Example: whether you measure distance in km or miles, the distance is the same. Whether you measure δ as energy or as probability, δ is the same.

Misconception 5. The + sign in the equation is addition

Fact: + is a structural declaration that two things are orthogonal. It does not mean adding numbers.

Axiom 1 Proposition text: "Therefore the + sign must not be read as addition. The + is a topological declaration that the two brackets are orthogonal, not a metric instruction to sum squared distances."

$$(\text{time} + \text{space})^{2} = \text{time}^{2} + \text{space}^{2}$$
Orthogonal, so there is no cross term

Example: going 3 km east and 4 km north on a map gives a straight-line distance of 5 km. Not 3+4=7 but √(9+16)=5. Orthogonal quantities combine by Pythagoras.

Orthogonal means independent, not merged. Trade-offs happen only among axes in the same bracket (time² + space² = c²); axes from different brackets (time and observer) are never operated on directly in one equation.

Misconception 6. Putting observer as an axis is unscientific

Axiom 1 text: "Without observer, there is nowhere to receive the result of CAS's Compare. Without superposition, there is nothing for CAS to reference. Both are structural necessities for the Banya Equation to operate, not figurative rhetoric borrowed from quantum mechanics."

Inventor's Reflection

I have never studied physics. I am a programmer who has not received formal education in mathematics. While building game engines, I picked up physics knowledge along the way, and I filled in the gaps through self-study.

And what I know applies 100% to physics.

In retrospect, this is unsurprising. Game engine development is the work of imitating nature, and the calculus, matrices, quaternions, and Clifford algebra I picked up along the way are tools built to describe nature's motion, rotation, and transformation. The mathematics learned to imitate nature and the mathematics used to describe nature cannot help but be the same toolset. The linear algebra, differentiation, probability, and information theory I picked up for AI development came from the same trunk. I arrived through a different path, but ended up inside the same toolbox.

I thought about why more deeply.

The starting point is this question: if I were to build the universe as a program, how would I design it? You need memory to store states, an operator to change states, an order of operations, and resources must be finite. If you allow infinite resources, the system diverges and nothing can stably exist. The atomic operation that reads, compares, and writes states on finite resources is CAS. As a programmer, I simply tried to design the most efficient system possible.

The Banya Equation alone, Axiom 1, was already sufficient to be compatible with existing physics equations. What was missing was the axioms needed to interpret that compatibility as a structural consequence, the "why it works that way." So CAS and the remaining axioms were added, and the single principle behind every addition was the principle of least action in logic.

The 15 axioms were completed by design. They were in place only after I had finished conceiving the universe as a computer, and full-scale mining began after that. The axioms were not made to fit physics equations; the design came first.

Mining is learning. The axioms are the weights, and the more than 1,700 physics equations are the pretraining data. Throughout mining the 15 axioms stayed fixed, and the axiom system was shaped by adding propositions. It is like fine-tuning with a small learning rate and a cooldown, and this is how the Banya Framework grows more precise. On the axiom system so shaped, 118 physics equations are compatible with FAIL 0, and E=mc², the uncertainty principle and black hole entropy emerge.

The learned axioms infer equations never seen. It is the same structure as a pretrained LLM (large language model) generating sentences that were not in its training data: data shapes the weights, and the shaped weights infer what was not seen. The record of those inferences, the grading principle, and the next step of automating the manual shaping are set out above (How the axioms were made).

This is profoundly interesting. A programmer designing "the most efficient system" produced a result that exactly matches the laws physicists discovered through 300 years of experiments.
