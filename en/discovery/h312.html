H-312: filter Compare false accumulation = running coupling

$$\alpha(N)=\alpha/(1-\alpha N/(3\pi))$$

Error 0.74%

When a CAS Compare returns false, a virtual pair is generated as a 1-loop. The accumulation of such events produces the running coupling. This is a reconfirmation of the result already obtained in D-109 with a 0.74% error.

Banya formula: $\alpha(N) = \alpha/(1 - \alpha N/(3\pi))$. The same formula as in H-270. One Compare false = one mismatch at the filter stage = generation of one virtual-pair (empty entity) loop.

Axiomatic basis: Axiom 4 (cost = +1 when a + boundary is crossed) defines the cost of Compare false. When Compare false occurs the pipeline restarts at filter, and this restart cost corresponds to virtual-pair creation.

Structural consequence: the larger the Compare-false accumulation $N$, the larger (QED) or smaller (QCD) the coupling constant becomes. The direction depends on whether the filter restart is screening or anti-screening.

Numbers: the deviation between the predicted $\alpha(M_Z)$ in D-109 and experiment is 0.74%. This is the result at the 1-loop approximation level. Including 2-loop and higher corrections shrinks the error.

Consistency: a reconfirmation of H-270 (QED running coupling). H-271 (QCD running) applies the same structure to the strong interaction. Cross-verified with D-109.

Physical correspondence: the running coupling constant. A central result of renormalization in quantum field theory. Virtual-pair creation = charge screening/anti-screening through vacuum polarization.

In conventional quantum field theory the running coupling emerges from handling the ultraviolet divergence of loop integrals. In Banya it arises from the discrete counting of Compare-false events, so divergences do not exist in principle.

5-Step Recursive Substitution: Deriving the running coupling from the Compare-false accumulation cost

Step 1. Banya Formula

$$\delta^2 = (\text{time} + \text{space})^2 + (\text{observer} + \text{superposition})^2$$

The running coupling is the accumulated cost the CAS Compare pays as it generates one loop of a virtual pair each time it returns false. One Compare-false is an irreversible cost +1 (Axiom 4: cost = + order crossing), and as this cost accumulates $N$ times the coupling strength changes.

Step 2. Norm Substitution

The norm being substituted is the Compare-false accumulation-cost norm. It is chosen because one Compare-false is one loop of a virtual pair whose cost is proportional to the $\alpha$ of D-01 and the CAS 3-step/$\pi$ phase. The coupling is therefore substituted by a function $\alpha(N)$ of the accumulation count $N$, diverging where the denominator reaches 1.

$\alpha = \dfrac{e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0\hbar c}$
$\alpha(N) = \dfrac{\alpha}{1 - \alpha N/(3\pi)}$
$\alpha(N)$: coupling after $N$ accumulations | $\alpha$: fine-structure constant (D-01) | $N$: Compare-false accumulation count | $3\pi$: CAS 3-step phase factor | $N=3\pi/\alpha$: divergence (saturation)
