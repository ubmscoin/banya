5-Step Recursive Substitution: Deriving the Band Gap $E_g$ from the Periodic-Potential Branching of the FSM Norm

Step 1. Banya Formula

$$\delta^2 = (\text{time} + \text{space})^2 + (\text{observer} + \text{superposition})^2$$

Bands are the result of the FSM norm (Axiom 14) splitting under a crystal's periodic potential (H-617). When the cost term forks into two values at the periodic boundary, the region between them becomes a forbidden gap where no state is usable. Bloch's periodic boundary condition is the periodicity of the FSM norm.

Step 2. Norm Substitution

(a) The norm being substituted is the FSM norm under a periodic potential. The FSM norm is energy (Axiom 14), and cost quantization (Axiom 4) splits the continuous energy into discrete bands. (b) This norm is chosen because the separation of allowed bands and forbidden gaps is the direct result of the norm forking into two branches at the periodic boundary. The gap is the distance between those two branches.

$E_n(\mathbf{k}) = \text{FSM norm}\big|_{\text{periodic potential}}$
$E_g = E_{n+1}(\mathbf{k}_0) - E_n(\mathbf{k}_0)$
$E_n(\mathbf{k})$: $n$-th band | $\mathbf{k}$: wavevector (Brillouin zone) | $E_g$: band gap | $\mathbf{k}_0$: zone boundary | gap $= 0$: conductor, gap $> 0$: insulator

Step 3. Constant Substitution

$E_{g}(Si)$ = 1.12 $\text{eV}$
$E_{g}(GaAs)$ = 1.42 $\text{eV}$
$E_{g}(C)$ = 5.5 $\text{eV}$ diamond

Step 4. Domain Transformation

(a) The transformation is into the condensed-matter (band theory) domain. (b) What it reveals is the size of the forbidden gap between the two bands forked at the periodic boundary. The width by which the FSM norm splits at the zone boundary $\mathbf{k}_0$ is measured directly as the band gap.

$\text{FSM norm branching}\big|_{\text{Si}} \quad\Rightarrow\quad E_g = 1.12\ \text{eV}$

Step 5. Discovery

Derived: $E_g = $ FSM norm branching width (positive)
Measured: Si $1.12\ \text{eV}$, GaAs $1.42\ \text{eV}$
Status: gap existence matches, size not derived
