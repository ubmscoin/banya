H-315: CPT symmetry = freedom of description direction in δ

$$CPT:\;\delta\text{ chooses description direction};\;C(7,k)=C(7,7{-}k)$$

CPT symmetry is derived as δ's freedom to choose the description direction. δ is outside the FSM, so it can freely choose forward or backward description, and the combination of the three reversals (C, P, T) leaves the equals sign (δ) invariant.

Banya formula: C = inversion $k \leftrightarrow (7-k)$ (the Pascal symmetry of H-311). P = inversion of the domain nibble bits ($0 \leftrightarrow 1$). T = reversal of the CAS order ($S \text{==>} C \text{==>} R$). The CPT combination = δ invariant.

Axiomatic basis: Axiom 14 (δ = outside the FSM) guarantees the freedom of description direction. Axiom 9 ($C(7,k) = C(7,7-k)$) supplies the C symmetry. Axiom 1 (4 domain axes) defines the P inversion, and Axiom 14 (FSM sequence) defines the T reversal.

Structural consequence: the CPT combination changes only the description direction, leaving the equals sign (δ) invariant, so physical laws are preserved. Individual C, P, T may break (weak interaction) but the CPT combination cannot break -- because δ is invariant.

Numbers: experimental upper bound on CPT violation: $|m_K - m_{\bar{K}}|/m_K < 6 \times 10^{-19}$ (CPLEAR). The invariance of δ guarantees this extreme precision.

Consistency: the C symmetry structure is confirmed in H-311 (128 = particle + antiparticle). H-314 (time symmetry) explains the T symmetry, and H-316 (arrow of time) explains the origin of T asymmetry.

In conventional QFT, the CPT theorem is proved from Lorentz invariance, locality, and unitarity. In Banya it follows from the single condition of δ invariance.

Verification: searches for CPT violation (ALPHA experiment with antihydrogen, CPLEAR with kaons, etc.) have produced no evidence of violation. An indirect confirmation of δ invariance.
