Step 2. Norm Substitution

The norm being substituted is the cost-gradient norm at the impact parameter $b$. This norm is chosen because the deflection angle is the integral of the transverse cost gradient accumulated along the path, and that gradient is stronger the closer it is, as $1/\ell^2$ (Axiom 11). The deflection angle is therefore substituted by $4GM/(c^2 b)$. The coefficient 4 is twice the Newtonian value 2 because both the time-component and the space-component cost gradients contribute to the bending. Why this norm: the gravitational-lensing deflection angle itself originates from the cost gradient outside the event horizon, so only a norm that carries that very cost structure maps onto it without slack. Why the substitution is valid: the standard physics form on the left and the Banya form on the right denote the same quantity, the latter merely rewriting it in CAS cost terms.
