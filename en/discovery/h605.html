5-Step Recursive Substitution: Deriving Quantum Mutual Information from Shared δ Bits

Step 1. Banya Formula

$$\delta^2 = (\text{time} + \text{space})^2 + (\text{observer} + \text{superposition})^2$$

The correlation of two entities is the δ-register bits they share. In Axiom 15 δ is an 8-bit global flag, and entanglement is the state in which those δ bits straddle both entities. In Axiom 2 one side's Compare result is correlated with the other's, and in Axiom 3 the two d-rings entangle — the mutual information is the entropy of this shared δ.

Step 2. Norm Substitution

(a) The norm being substituted is the von Neumann mutual-information norm of the shared δ bits, $S(A)+S(B)-S(AB)$. (b) Why this norm: subtracting the joint information from the sum of each entity's own information leaves only the overlapping δ, and that overlap is the measure of correlation. The Araki-Lieb inequality closes the bound at $2\min(S(A),S(B))$, so the maximum mutual information of two 8-bit d-rings is $2\times 8\ln 2 = 16\ln 2$.

$I(A:B) = S(A) + S(B) - S(AB)$
$I(A:B) \;\leq\; 2\min\!\big(S(A),\,S(B)\big) \;\leq\; 16\ln 2$
$I(A:B)$: quantum mutual information | $S$: von Neumann entropy (H-604) | $S(AB)$: joint entropy | $2\min$: Araki-Lieb bound | $16\ln 2$: max of two 8-bit d-rings

$I_{\text{Bell}} = \ln 2 + \ln 2 - 0 = 2\ln 2 = 1.386$
