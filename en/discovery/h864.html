5-Step Recursive Substitution: Deriving the DT Fusion Cost Gain from the FSM Norm Change

Step 1. Banya Formula

$$\delta^2 = (\text{time} + \text{space})^2 + (\text{observer} + \text{superposition})^2$$

The fusion gain is the norm that decreases when light FSMs (hydrogen isotopes) merge — the mass defect released as cost (Axiom 3). Since the FSM norm corresponds to binding energy, the norm difference before and after fusion is read as the cost of $\delta^2$. $Q>1$ is the condition that the released cost exceeds the cost spent on confinement.

Step 2. Norm Substitution

(a) The norm being substituted is the FSM mass norm — the difference in rest cost before and after the reaction. (b) The numerator of the cost gain $Q$ is the fusion energy released, and that energy closes as the mass defect $\Delta m\,c^2$ (Axiom 7 cost gain); hence the mass norm is chosen. The DT fusion energy is therefore substituted by the difference of the reactant and product mass sums.

$Q = \dfrac{P_{\text{fusion}}}{P_{\text{input}}} > 1$
$E_{\text{DT}} = \big[(m_D + m_T) - (m_{\text{He}} + m_n)\big]c^2$
$Q$: fusion cost gain | $E_{\text{DT}}$: DT reaction energy released | $m_D,m_T,m_{\text{He}},m_n$: D, T, He-4, neutron FSM masses | $c^2$: mass-cost conversion $931.494\;\text{MeV/u}$

Step 3. Constant Substitution

m_D  = 2.014102 u     AME2020
m_T  = 3.016049 u     AME2020
m_He = 4.002602 u     AME2020
m_n  = 1.008665 u     AME2020
c^2  = 931.494 MeV/u  CODATA

Step 4. Domain Transformation

(a) The transformation is from the dimensionless mass norm into the nuclear-energy domain (MeV). (b) What it reveals is the cost released by a single DT fusion — the energy released that becomes the numerator of the $Q>1$ gain. Multiplying the mass-sum difference by $c^2$ yields that value.

$E_{\text{DT}} = (5.030151 - 5.011267)\,\text{u} \times 931.494\;\text{MeV/u} = 0.018884 \times 931.494 = 17.59\;\text{MeV}$
