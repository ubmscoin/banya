$$E_\text{cr}=\frac{m_e^2 c^3}{e\hbar},\quad \text{prefactor correction}=\frac{4}{13}\quad(\text{FSM completes twice}=2\times13,\ \text{maintenance cost 4 remaining})$$

Prediction: a $4/13$ prefactor correction to the Schwinger pair-production rate. Test: ultra-intense lasers (ELI-NP, SEL, XCELS, 2030~2040).

In the Banya formula $\delta^2=(\text{time}+\text{space})^2+(\text{observer}+\text{superposition})^2$, Schwinger pair production (electron-positron pair creation in a strong electric field) occurs at the critical field $E_\text{cr}=m_e^2c^3/(e\hbar)$ and is described by an FSM cycle (Axiom 14).

Electron-positron pair production is a process where the FSM completes twice — particle 1 + antiparticle 1 = 2 FSMs, cost $2\times13$.

The total cost of one interaction is 13 (Axiom 4), and from the RLU remainder 9, a maintenance cost 4 remains (Axiom 6).

So a $4/13$ prefactor correction attaches to the pair-production rate ==> the ratio of maintenance cost 4 to total cost 13.

4 is the RLU maintenance cost (the cost of keeping the pair in the cache after creation), and 13 is the total cost of one FSM completion.

So this prediction is not "Schwinger pair production exists" but the quantitative claim "a $4/13$ correction in the prefactor."

$4/13$ is in the same total-cost-13 distribution family as the vacuum energy $4/13$ (P-130) and Casimir $9/13$ (P-126).

Since the CAS Swap is irreversible (Axiom 2), the created pair permanently resides on the screen.
