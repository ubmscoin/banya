In a microscopic system the CAS cost is not the same every cycle but fluctuates. Because the δ utterance (Axiom 15) is stochastic, the cost $+1$ can transiently reverse (entropy decrease). CAS irreversibility (Axiom 5) makes that bidirectional fluctuation asymmetric.

Step 2. Norm Substitution

The norm being substituted is the CAS irreversible-cost fluctuation norm. It is chosen because the probability ratio of forward to reverse fluctuations is set by the statistical property of the irreversible cost. Reading the CAS cost as an energy fluctuation (Axiom 4), that ratio closes as the exponential of the entropy production $\sigma$. Why this norm: the fluctuation-theorem symmetry relation itself originates from CAS cost fluctuation, so only a norm that carries that very cost structure maps onto it without slack. Why the substitution is valid: the standard physics form on the left and the Banya form on the right denote the same quantity, the latter merely rewriting it in CAS cost terms.
