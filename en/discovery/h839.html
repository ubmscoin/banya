H-839: Game Theory = Strategic Cost Exchange among Multiple CAS

$$u_i(s_i, s_{-i}) \;\leftrightarrow\; \text{cost of CAS}_i(\text{own strategy}, \text{others' strategies}) = \text{strategic cost exchange}$$

Grade: C

Game theory analyzes the strategic interaction of multiple decision-makers. In Banya, each player = the CAS operated by an observer (Axiom 15), and the payoff function = the CAS cost function. Nash equilibrium = a state in which the cost of every CAS cannot be unilaterally decreased.

Axiom 15 (observer = player), Axiom 2 (CAS = strategy), Axiom 4 (cost = payoff)

Axiom 15 (observer = decision-making subject), Axiom 2 (CAS = strategy execution), Axiom 4 (cost +1 = payoff). Nash equilibrium = $\forall i: \text{Cost}(CAS_i^*, CAS_{-i}^*) \leq \text{Cost}(CAS_i, CAS_{-i}^*)$. Prisoner's dilemma = individual CAS cost minimization ≠ collective cost minimization.

Pareto optimum = the minimum of the sum of all CAS costs. Nash equilibrium ≠ Pareto optimum = mismatch between individual CAS and collective CAS cost. Cooperation in repeated games = long-term minimization of CAS cost (using RLU decay). Altruism = short-term cost increase ==> long-term cost decrease.
