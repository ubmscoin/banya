Core Finding

P-103: δ Outside-FSM DOF: Bell-violation Partial Screening $=2\sqrt{2}(1-1/128)$

$$\text{벨 위반 부분 차폐}\leq2\sqrt{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{128}\right)\quad(\delta\ \text{부분 차폐 시 이산 감소})$$

Prediction: under δ partial screening, Bell violation discretely decreases to $\leq2\sqrt{2}(1-1/128)$. Test: decoherence-controlled Bell experiment (2030~).

In the Banya formula $\delta^2=(\text{time}+\text{space})^2+(\text{observer}+\text{superposition})^2$, $\delta$ applies globally from outside the FSM, so even when two entities CAS are independent, the equality (correlation) is shared (Axiom 15).

The Bell-violation bound $2\sqrt{2}$ (Tsirelson, P-65) is a structural consequence of 4-domain orthogonality, and δ nonlocality saturates it.

Partially screening δ weakens the global equality and decreases the violation ==> $\leq2\sqrt{2}\times(1-1/128)$.

The decrease unit $1/128$ is the weight of screening one mode of the 128-state space (P-51), meaning the screening is discrete.

So the Bell violation decreases not continuously with the degree of screening but in $1/128$-unit steps.

This shows that the Tsirelson bound (P-65) is saturated by δ nonlocality and that the saturation is discretely released by screening.

So this prediction is not "screening reduces violation" but the quantitative claim "the decrease is exactly $1/128$ units, bound $2\sqrt{2}(1-1/128)$."

This is in the same environment-DOF-screening family as the decoherence threshold 16 (P-56) and weak measurement 1/16 (P-101).

Test: if a discrete violation decrease near environment DOF 128 is confirmed in a decoherence-controlled Bell experiment (2030~), this prediction is supported.

Refutation: if the violation decrease under partial screening is unrelated to the $1/128$ scale, this prediction is discarded.

Re-substitution purpose: Test: decoherence-controlled Bell experiment (2030~). Refutation: partial-screening violation decrease unrelated to the $1/128$ scale. Source: Axiom 15 (δ = global), P-65 (Tsirelson bound = 4 axes), P-56 (decoherence threshold = 16).

Step 1. Banya Formula

$$\delta^2 = (\text{time} + \text{space})^2 + (\text{observer} + \text{superposition})^2$$

The Bell-inequality violation is the cost of $\delta$ applying globally outside the FSM (Axiom 15) so that two independent-CAS entities share the equality (correlation). This cost is read as the nonlocal correlation along the observer axis.

Step 2. Norm Substitution

The norm being substituted is the $\delta$ nonlocal-correlation norm. It is chosen because the Tsirelson bound $2\sqrt{2}$ is the structural consequence of 4-domain orthogonality (P-65) that $\delta$ nonlocality saturates, and partially shielding $\delta$ removes the violation one mode at a time in units of $1/128$ from the 128-state space (P-51). The violation bound is therefore substituted by the shielding factor $(1-1/128)$.

$S_{\max} = 2\sqrt{2}$
$S_{\text{shield}} \leq 2\sqrt{2}\left(1 - \dfrac{1}{128}\right)$
$S$: CHSH Bell correlation | $2\sqrt{2}$: Tsirelson bound (4-axis saturation, P-65) | $128 = 2^7$: environment state space (P-51) | $1/128$: single-mode shielding fraction

Step 4. Domain Transformation

$S_{\text{shield}} \leq 2.828427 \times 0.9921875 = 2.806327$
