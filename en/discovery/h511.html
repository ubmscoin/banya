The norm being substituted is the angular norm — the transverse Swap-chain cost divided by the longitudinal momentum. It is chosen because the jet width closes as the ratio of the transverse momentum $k_\perp$ one Swap adds to the total energy $E$. The jet cone half-angle is therefore substituted by the ratio of the transverse cost scale $\Lambda_{\text{QCD}}$ to the energy $E$. Why this norm: the Jet Cone Angle itself originates from the CAS Swap Chain Cost, so only a norm that carries that very cost structure maps onto it without slack. Why the substitution is valid: the standard physics form on the left and the Banya form on the right denote the same quantity, the latter merely rewriting it in CAS cost terms.
