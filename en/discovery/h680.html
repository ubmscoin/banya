Core Finding

H-680: Mass-Luminosity Relation = Power Law of FSM Norm vs Cost Emission

$$L \propto M^\alpha,\;\alpha \approx 3.5 \;\leftrightarrow\; \text{FSM norm}^{3.5} = \text{cost emission rate}$$

Grade: B

The mass-luminosity relation of main-sequence stars is $L \propto M^{3.5}$ (approximate), with massive stars vastly brighter. In Banya, the FSM norm (mass) sets the central temperature, and since the fusion rate depends strongly on temperature, cost emission grows as a power law of the norm.

Axiom 3 (FSM norm), Axiom 4 (cost emission), H-669 (main-sequence stars)

Axiom 3 (FSM norm = $M$ ==> central temperature $T_c \propto M/R$), Axiom 4 (cost emission = $L$, radiative transfer $L \propto M^3/\kappa$), Axiom 6 (RLU decay = radiative opacity $\kappa$). Detail: $\alpha \approx 4$ (intermediate mass), $\alpha \approx 3$ (high mass).

Main-sequence lifetime $t \propto M/L \propto M^{-2.5}$: a $10\;M_\odot$ star has $\sim 300\times$ shorter lifetime than the Sun. High-mass stars ($> 50\;M_\odot$): $L \to L_\text{Edd}$, $\alpha \to 1$. Low-mass stars ($< 0.43\;M_\odot$): fully convective ==> different relation.

$1\;M_\odot$: $L = L_\odot$. $10\;M_\odot$: $L \approx 3000\;L_\odot$. $0.1\;M_\odot$: $L \approx 10^{-3}\;L_\odot$.

Consistent with eclipsing-binary mass-luminosity data at $\alpha \approx 3.5$ (scatter $< 0.3$ dex).

Mass-luminosity relation, main sequence, opacity, Eddington limit.

Verified by precision mass and luminosity measurements of eclipsing binaries.

5-Step Recursive Substitution: Deriving the Mass-Luminosity Power Index from FSM Norm vs Radiative Cost Emission

Step 1. Banya Formula

$$\delta^2 = (\text{time} + \text{space})^2 + (\text{observer} + \text{superposition})^2$$

The mass-luminosity relation is the phenomenon in which a larger FSM norm raises the central cost so that radiative emission surges as a power law. Axiom 3 (FSM norm = mass) and Axiom 4 (cost emission = luminosity) read this power law.

Step 2. Norm Substitution

The norm being substituted is the central-cost norm fixed by hydrostatic equilibrium. The central temperature is proportional to the norm ($T_c \propto M/R$) and the radiative cost transfer scales as a power of that temperature (Axiom 6 RLU decay), so this norm is chosen. The luminosity is therefore substituted by a mass power law, with the index derived from the hydrostatic and radiative-transfer scaling.

$L \propto M^\alpha$
$\alpha = 3 + \dfrac{\Delta\kappa}{\Delta\ln M}$
$L$: luminosity = cost-emission rate | $M$: FSM norm | $\alpha$: mass-luminosity power index | $3$: baseline index of radiative transfer $L\propto M^3/\kappa$ | $\kappa$: radiative opacity (RLU decay)

Step 3. Constant Substitution

$\alpha_{base}$ = 3 baseline index of radiative transfer L $\sim$ $M^{3}/\kappa$
$d_\kappa$ = 0.5 mass-dependent opacity correction (intermediate mass)
$\alpha$ = 3 + 0.5 = 3.5
$\alpha_{obs}$ = 3.5 eclipsing-binary data (< 0.3 dex scatter)

Step 4. Domain Transformation

The transformation is into the stellar-structure domain. What it reveals is the main-sequence mass-luminosity power index $\alpha$. Adding the opacity correction to the radiative-transfer baseline index 3 yields that value.

$\alpha = 3 + 0.5 = 3.5$
$L(10\,M_\odot) \propto 10^{3.5} \approx 3.2\times10^{3}\,L_\odot$

Step 5. Discovery

Derived: $\alpha = 3.5$
Measured: $\alpha \approx 3.5$ (eclipsing binaries, $< 0.3$ dex scatter)
Error: $< 0.3$ dex

Verdict: Hypothesis. The baseline index 3 closes from radiative transfer, but the opacity correction $\Delta\kappa=0.5$ is external input, leaving the final forward-chain step for the power index $3.5$ incomplete, and $\alpha$ varies across mass ranges.
