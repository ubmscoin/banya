H-423: CAS cannot be measured by domain time

$$\text{domain time (bit 2)}\neq\text{CAS (bit 4,5,6) measurement tool}$$

(1) Domain time (bit 2) cannot measure CAS (bits 4, 5, 6). The domain is the target of CAS, not the measurement tool for CAS.

(2) Banya formula: domain time (bit 2) ≠ CAS (bits 4,5,6) measurement tool. You cannot measure the backend clock from the screen.

(3) By Axiom 3 (screen) domain time is the rendered output. By Axiom 15 (δ) CAS is a backend process ignited by δ. Measuring the process itself from its output is a category error.

(4) Structural consequence: in physics "measuring time" means reading the change in domain time. But this is the result (output) of CAS cycles, not the CAS cycle itself. The execution time of CAS cannot be measured by domain time.

(5) Numerics: minimum unit of domain time = Planck time t_P ≈ 5.39 × 10⁻⁴⁴ s. This is the rendered result of 1 CAS cycle, not the "elapsed time" of 1 CAS cycle.

(6) Consistency: the conventional wisdom that "times shorter than the Planck time are meaningless" is explained by this structure. Since the resolution of domain time is one CAS cycle, anything below is undefined.

(7) Physical correspondence: this is the minimum-time-unit problem in quantum gravity. In the Banya Framework it is a resolution limit of the measurement tool (domain), not a limit of system time.

(8) Analogy: you cannot measure the clock speed of a GPU (CAS) by the pixel count of the monitor (screen). The monitor is the output device of the GPU, not its measurement device.

(9) Verification: confirm that any attempt to measure CAS by domain time produces a logical contradiction (self-reference paradox, etc.).
