5-Step Recursive Substitution: Deriving the Nuclear Force Range 1.4 fm from the RLU Finite-Release Cost

Step 1. Banya Formula

$$\delta^2 = (\text{time} + \text{space})^2 + (\text{observer} + \text{superposition})^2$$

The nuclear force is the RLU release cost of exchanging a pion between nucleons. Because RLU residual release is finite (Axiom 6), cost accumulates along every propagation path (Axiom 4: cost $+1$/boundary), making the range finite.

Step 2. Norm Substitution

(a) The norm being substituted is the pion's FSM norm $m_\pi$ (Axiom 14). (b) It is chosen because the propagation cost along the RLU path accumulates exponentially with distance and is cut off at $\sim 1/m_\pi$. The nuclear potential is therefore substituted by the Yukawa exponential decay governed by the pion norm.

$V(r) = -\dfrac{g^2}{4\pi r}\,e^{-m_\pi r}$
$R = \dfrac{1}{m_\pi}$
$V(r)$: nuclear potential | $g^2/4\pi$: nuclear coupling | $m_\pi$: pion FSM norm (H-494) | $R$: RLU range | $e^{-m_\pi r}$: exponential accumulation of RLU path cost

Step 3. Constant Substitution

$m_\pi$ = 139.6 $\text{MeV}$ PDG 2024
hbar c = 197.3 $\text{MeV}$ $\text{fm}$
$g^{2}/(4\pi)$ = 14 nuclear coupling
$E_{d}$ = 2.224 $\text{MeV}$ deuteron binding energy

Step 4. Domain Transformation

(a) The transformation is into the nuclear-force (strong residual) range domain. (b) What it reveals is the nuclear range $R$ set by the finiteness of RLU release. Multiplying the inverse pion norm by $\hbar c$ yields that value.

$R = \dfrac{\hbar c}{m_\pi c^2} = \dfrac{197.3}{139.6} = 1.41\;\text{fm}$
