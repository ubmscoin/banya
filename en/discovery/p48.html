Prediction: leptoquarks do not exist (quark-lepton transition = internal CAS Swap). Test: LHC Run 3/4 (2026~).

In the Banya formula $\delta^2=(\text{time}+\text{space})^2+(\text{observer}+\text{superposition})^2$, quarks and leptons are different step-states of the same CAS operator — there is no separate particle linking them (Axiom 2).

The transition between quark and lepton occurs only through a Swap between CAS steps ==> changing the step is an internal CAS operation, not an external mediator.

A leptoquark is proposed as a new mediating boson directly linking quark and lepton. But the internal CAS Swap already plays that role, so an independent leptoquark is unnecessary.

Since CAS is the unique operator (Axiom 2), there is no room for a separate gauge DOF linking quark and lepton (P-29: gauge bosons complete at 12).

So this prediction is not "leptoquark not found yet" but the structural-absence claim "the quark-lepton transition is a CAS Swap, so there is no independent-mediator DOF."

Recent B-meson anomalies (R_K, etc.) fueled the leptoquark hypothesis, but updated measurements ease the tension with the Standard Model, trending consistent with this prediction.

Leptoquarks usually appear with grand unification or extended symmetry, but the Banya framework denies SUSY, extra dimensions, and new gauge bosons (P-29, P-30, P-31).

Observational strategy: at the LHC, leptoquarks are searched as quark+lepton co-production resonances.

Refutation: if a leptoquark is found at $5\sigma$, the internal-CAS-Swap structure is refuted and this prediction is discarded.
