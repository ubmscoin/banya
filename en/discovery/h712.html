H-712. Magneto-Optical Kerr Effect = Cost-Propagation Asymmetry of Lock Bits

Core Finding

H-712: Magneto-Optical Kerr Effect = cost-propagation asymmetry of lock bits

$$\theta_K \propto M \;\leftrightarrow\; \text{reflected-polarization rotation induced by surface lock-bit magnetization}$$

Grade: C

The Magneto-Optical Kerr Effect (MOKE) is the phenomenon in which the polarization of a CAS cost wave reflected from the surface of a magnetic material rotates in proportion to the magnetization ($M$). It is the reflection-side counterpart of the Faraday effect.

H-710 (Faraday rotation), H-627 (magnetism)

H-710 (magnetism-polarization coupling), H-627 (lock-bit alignment = magnetization), Axiom 4 (polarization asymmetry of reflected CAS cost)

Polar MOKE: $M \perp$ surface. Longitudinal MOKE: $M \parallel$ surface, within plane of incidence. Transverse MOKE: $M \parallel$ surface, orthogonal to plane of incidence. Kerr rotation $\theta_K \sim 0.01°\text{--}1°$. Magnetic-domain imaging.

Fe: $\theta_K \approx 0.6°$ ($633\;\text{nm}$). Co/Pt multilayers: $\theta_K \approx 0.3°$.

Consistent with MOKE measurements.

Magneto-Optical Kerr Effect (MOKE), magnetic-domain imaging, magneto-optical recording.

Verified by MOKE microscopy and magneto-optical disks.

Derivation of MOKE tensor components as a function of lock-bit magnetization direction.

The magneto-optical Kerr effect is the asymmetry by which, when surface lock bits align in one direction (magnetization), the left- and right-polarized costs of the reflected CAS cost wave differ. This rotation is read as the polarization asymmetry of the reflected CAS cost (Axiom 4), and lock-bit alignment (H-627) sets the sign of the asymmetry.

Step 2. Norm Substitution

The norm being substituted is the reflecting-surface lock-bit alignment norm. This norm is chosen because the magnetization $M$ splits the two cost modes of left- and right-circular polarization at first order, so the rotation angle is proportional to $M$. The reflected-polarization rotation is therefore substituted by the first-order magnetization term. This is the transmission-vs-reflection counterpart of the H-710 Faraday rotation. Why this norm: the Kerr rotation angle $\theta_K$ itself originates from lock-bit magnetization cost asymmetry, so only a norm that carries that very cost structure maps onto it without slack. Why the substitution is valid: the standard physics form on the left and the Banya form on the right denote the same quantity, the latter merely rewriting it in CAS cost terms.

$\theta_F \propto M\,\ell$
$\theta_K \propto M$
