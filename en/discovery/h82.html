Core Finding

H-82: CKM u-row Hamming distance monotone correspondence

$$|V_{ud}| > |V_{us}| > |V_{ub}|, \quad d_H(u,d) < d_H(u,s) < d_H(u,b)$$

Structural correspondence. CKM magnitude order matches the monotone decrease of Hamming distance.

We show that the magnitudes of the u-row entries of the CKM matrix are in monotone correspondence with Hamming distance. In the Banya formula, a quark transition is the CAS Read==>Compare==>Swap (Axiom 2), and bit comparison is performed at the Compare step. With the bit assignment of H-44 (3-bit octet), the Hamming distance is $d_H = 1$ for $u \to d$, $d_H = 2$ for $u \to s$, and $d_H = 3$ for $u \to b$, increasing monotonically. Structural consequence: the larger the Hamming distance, the higher the juim (juim) cost (Axiom 4, C+1 iteration) at Compare, so the transition probability decreases. The order $|V_{ud}| > |V_{us}| > |V_{ub}|$ exactly matches the bit-distance order. Numerics: $|V_{ud}| \approx 0.974$ ($d_H=1$), $|V_{us}| \approx 0.225$ ($d_H=2$), $|V_{ub}| \approx 0.004$ ($d_H=3$). Exponential decrease as the distance grows. Consistency: provides the structural basis for H-47 ($s_{13}$ CAS derivation). Consistent with D-07 (Cabibbo angle) and D-08 (Wolfenstein A). Physical correspondence: the near-diagonal dominance of the CKM matrix is interpreted as a preference for nearby transitions in bit space. In the existing Standard Model the CKM structure follows from Yukawa couplings, but in Banya it is reduced to a distance function in bit space. Verification: build a quantitative formula of the form $|V_{ij}| \propto \exp(-c \cdot d_H(i,j))$ and test whether the entire CKM matrix can be reproduced. 5-Step Recursive Substitution: Deriving the CKM u-row Hamming-Distance Monotone Correspondence from the Compare Bit-Comparison Cost

Step 1. Banya Formula

$$\delta^2 = (\text{time} + \text{space})^2 + (\text{observer} + \text{superposition})^2$$

A quark-flavor transition is the cost the Compare step of CAS Read ==> Compare ==> Swap (Axiom 2) pays to compare the bits of two states. The more bits there are to compare, the larger the cost and the more the transition is suppressed.

Step 2. Norm Substitution

(a) The norm being substituted is the Hamming-distance norm between the two quark bit strings. (b) In the 3-bit octet assignment of H-44, the $u$-row transitions move one bit further apart at a time, so this distance norm directly sets the Compare cost and forces the monotone ordering of the transition amplitudes — hence it is chosen.

$|V_{ij}| = f\big(d_H(i,j)\big),\quad f\ \text{monotone decreasing}$
$|V_{ud}| > |V_{us}| > |V_{ub}|,\quad d_H(u,d) < d_H(u,s) < d_H(u,b)$
$|V_{ij}|$: CKM transition amplitude | $d_H$: Hamming distance (H-44) | $f$: distance ==> amplitude monotone-decreasing function | $u,d,s,b$: quark flavors

Step 4. Domain Transformation

(a) The transformation is into the weak-mixing domain (CKM bit space). (b) What it reveals is whether bit distance sets the magnitude ordering of the transition amplitudes. Placing the Hamming-distance order alongside the amplitude order yields the monotone correspondence.
