5-Step Recursive Substitution: Deriving the BCS Gap $\Delta$ and Critical Temperature $T_c$ from the Swap Coupling of Two FSMs

Step 1. Banya Formula

$$\delta^2 = (\text{time} + \text{space})^2 + (\text{observer} + \text{superposition})^2$$

An FSM is a half-integer-norm fermion (Axiom 14). When two FSMs couple via Swap (Axiom 2) with opposite lock bits (spin), they become an integer-norm boson (Cooper pair). The amount by which the pair-coupling cost is below the solo cost (Axiom 4) is the energy gap, and the boson transfers cost without resistance, giving superconductivity.

Step 2. Norm Substitution

(a) The norm being substituted is the opposite-spin Swap coupling norm of two FSMs. (b) This norm is chosen because the cost difference by which pairing is cheaper than solo reads directly as the gap $\Delta$, and the RLU thermal energy (Axiom 6) limit that breaks that gap reads as the critical temperature $T_c$. The ratio of gap to $T_c$ closes as the BCS universal relation.

$\Delta = V \sum_k \langle c_{-k\downarrow}c_{k\uparrow}\rangle$
$2\Delta = 3.53\, k_B T_c$
$\Delta$: BCS energy gap | $V$: pair-coupling cost difference | $c_{k\uparrow}$: FSM (fermion) | $T_c$: critical temperature | $3.53$: BCS universal ratio

Step 3. Constant Substitution

$\Delta(Al)$ = 0.17 meV $T_{c}$ = 1.2 K
$\Delta(Nb)$ = 1.5 meV $T_{c}$ = 9.3 K
3.53 = 2 $\Delta$ / $k_{B}$ $T_{c}$ BCS 1957

Step 4. Domain Transformation

(a) The transformation is into the electromagnetic/condensed-matter (superconductivity) domain. (b) What it reveals is the proportionality between the gap $\Delta$ and the critical temperature $T_c$. Dividing the gap fixed by the pair-coupling cost difference by the BCS universal ratio $3.53$ gives the temperature $T_c$ at which RLU thermal energy breaks the pair.

$\dfrac{2\Delta_{\text{Al}}}{k_B T_c} = \dfrac{2 \times 0.17\ \text{meV}}{k_B \times 1.2\ \text{K}} \approx 3.5$

Step 5. Discovery

Derived: $2\Delta / k_B T_c \approx 3.5$ (bosonic coupling)
Measured: Al $3.5$, Nb $3.8$ (BCS 1957)
Status: low-T BCS matches, high-T superconductivity unsolved

Verdict: Hypothesis. The Swap coupling of two FSMs closes the bosonic gap and the BCS universal ratio, but the pairing mechanism of high-T superconductivity (YBCO) and the magnitude of $V$ are not derived from the axioms, so the round halted.
