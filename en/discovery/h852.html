Step 2. Norm Substitution

The norm being substituted is the cost-wave round-trip attenuation norm. The emitted cost wave travels to the target and back, attenuating as $1/R^2$ on each of the transmit and receive legs, so the signal weakens as $1/R^4$; the maximum detection range is fixed where this attenuation equals the minimum received power, so this norm is chosen. Why this norm: the Radar Detection Range itself originates from the CAS Cost-Wave Round-Trip Cost, so only a norm that carries that very cost structure maps onto it without slack. Why the substitution is valid: the standard physics form on the left and the Banya form on the right denote the same quantity, the latter merely rewriting it in CAS cost terms.

$P_r = \dfrac{P_t G^2 \lambda^2 \sigma}{(4\pi)^3 R^4}$
$R_{\max} = \left(\dfrac{P_t G^2 \lambda^2 \sigma}{(4\pi)^3 P_{\min}}\right)^{1/4}$
$R_{\max}$: maximum detection range | $P_t$: transmit power | $G$: antenna gain | $\lambda$: wavelength | $\sigma$: radar cross section | $P_{\min}$: minimum received power | $(4\pi)^3$: round-trip solid-angle factor
