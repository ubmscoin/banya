H-706. Rayleigh Scattering = Wavelength-Dependent Scattering of CAS Cost Waves

Core Finding

H-706: Rayleigh Scattering = Wavelength-Dependent Scattering of CAS Cost Waves

$$I \propto \frac{1}{\lambda^4} \;\leftrightarrow\; \text{Scattering intensity of CAS cost wave from small particles}$$

Grade: B

Rayleigh scattering is the phenomenon where a CAS cost wave, scattered by particles much smaller than its wavelength, has a scattering intensity proportional to $\lambda^{-4}$. Blue sky = strong scattering of short-wavelength cost waves.

Axiom 4 (CAS cost wave), Axiom 12 (ECS scatterer)

Axiom 4 (cost-wave scattering = cost re-emission), Axiom 12 (scatterer size in ECS $\ll \lambda$), Axiom 2 (CAS-induced dipole = cost-wave re-emission source)

Scattering cross section $\sigma \propto a^6/\lambda^4$ (particle radius $a$). Dominant blue scattering ==> blue sky. Red sunset = long-wavelength residue. Mie scattering = transition when particle $\sim \lambda$.

Atmospheric $\text{N}_2$ scattering cross section: $\sigma \approx 5 \times 10^{-31}\;\text{m}^2$ ($500\;\text{nm}$).

Consistent with atmospheric scattering observations.

Rayleigh scattering, sky color, sunset color, scattering cross section.

5-Step Recursive Substitution: Deriving the Rayleigh $1/\lambda^4$ Dependence from the CAS Cost-Wave Scattering Cost

Step 1. Banya Formula

Rayleigh scattering is the cost the CAS cost wave pays when it strikes a scatterer far smaller than its wavelength and re-emits the cost (Axiom 4 cost-wave re-emission). The scatterer is a small region of size $\ll\lambda$ inside the ECS (Axiom 7), and the re-emission strength is read through the scatterer's induced dipole (Axiom 2).

Step 2. Norm Substitution

(a) The norm being substituted is the frequency norm of the CAS cost wave. (b) Because the scatterer is far smaller than the wavelength and sees a uniform field over one period of the cost wave, the re-emission cost closes as the fourth power of the cost-wave frequency (the square of the dipole acceleration times two frequency factors). Since frequency is $1/\lambda$, the scattering-intensity norm is substituted by $\lambda^{-4}$. Why this norm: the Rayleigh $1/\lambda^4$ Dependence itself originates from the CAS Cost-Wave Scattering Cost, so only a norm that carries that very cost structure maps onto it without slack. Why the substitution is valid: the standard physics form on the left and the Banya form on the right denote the same quantity, the latter merely rewriting it in CAS cost terms.

$I \propto \dfrac{\sigma\,I_0}{r^2}$
$I \propto \dfrac{a^6}{\lambda^4}$
$I$: scattering intensity | $\lambda$: cost-wave wavelength | $a$: scatterer radius ($a\ll\lambda$) | $\sigma$: scattering cross-section | $a^6/\lambda^4$: dipole re-emission norm

This step substitutes concrete constant values for the symbols of the Banya relation above. Every value inserted is already fixed by a prior D/H/P card or axiom, not set anew in this card. Why these constants: they are exactly the inputs needed to evaluate the Rayleigh $1/\lambda^4$ Dependence numerically, with no hand-tuned free parameter beyond them. Step 4. Domain Transformation

(a) The transformation is into the atmospheric-optics domain (ECS scattering region). (b) What it reveals is how much more strongly the short-wavelength cost wave scatters than the long-wavelength one -- why the sky is blue and the sunset red. Substituting the blue and red wavelengths into the $\lambda^{-4}$ norm yields the scattering ratio. Why this domain: the Rayleigh $1/\lambda^4$ Dependence itself is the observable measured in this domain, so the cost structure must be projected here to be compared directly with experiment. Why the transformation closes the derivation: the constants substituted above fully numericalize the right-hand side, so the observable is fixed with no external input.

$\dfrac{I_{450}}{I_{650}} = \left(\dfrac{650}{450}\right)^4 = 4.35$
