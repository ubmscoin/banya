$$g_*=128\ \text{saturation}\ \Rightarrow\ \text{QGP phase transition},\quad g_*=128\ \text{at }T_c\sim150\ \text{MeV}\ (\text{CAS saturation point})$$

Prediction: at the QGP transition, effective DOF $g_*=128$ saturation is the phase transition, $T_c\sim150$ MeV. Test: RHIC/LHC heavy-ion (2027~).

In the Banya formula $\delta^2=(\text{time}+\text{space})^2+(\text{observer}+\text{superposition})^2$, the total state space is 128 = $2^7$ (P-51), and CAS saturates when all of it is activated in a region.

The quark-gluon plasma (QGP) transition is a phase transition in which strongly bound hadrons dissolve into free quarks and gluons.

This prediction holds that the phase transition occurs when the effective DOF $g_*$ reaches 128 at the QGP transition ==> 128 = the CAS saturation point.

At the transition temperature $T_c\sim150$ MeV, $g_*=128$, which is the structural basis of the QGP critical temperature seen by lattice QCD.

Since 128 = $2^7$ is the full index space, when a region DOF fills it no new state can be indexed, so it dissolves via a phase transition.

So this prediction is not "there is a QGP transition" but the structural quantitative claim "at the transition point $g_*$ saturates at exactly 128."

The key test is whether the standard lattice-QCD $g_*$ count (quark and gluon DOF) converges to 128 near $T_c$.

Observational strategy: measure the effective DOF and transition temperature of the QGP in RHIC and LHC heavy-ion collisions.

Test: if the effective DOF saturates at 128 at the QGP transition in RHIC/LHC (2027~), this prediction is supported.

Refutation: if the QGP transition is proven structurally unrelated to 128 saturation, this prediction is discarded.
