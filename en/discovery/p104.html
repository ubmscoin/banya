So in the delayed-choice quantum eraser, the "retrocausal"-looking correlation is δ global equality, but rendering it to the screen takes $\geq4\,t_P$.

So no retrocausal signal below $4\,t_P$ exists ==> however fast, a retrocausal correlation is bound by the 4-stage pipeline floor.

$4\,t_P\sim2.2\times10^{-43}$ s, the same pipeline-clock floor as the minimum event interval (P-61).

The δ equality itself is non-temporal (P-112), but for it to become an observable signal it goes through the screen-render delay $4\,t_P$.

So this prediction is not "retrocausality exists or not" but the quantitative claim "the floor of an observable retrocausal signal is $4\,t_P$."

This is in the same 4-stage-pipeline family as measurement delay (P-73) and minimum event interval (P-61).

Step 1. Banya Formula

$$\delta^2 = (\text{time} + \text{space})^2 + (\text{observer} + \text{superposition})^2$$

The "retrocausal" correlation of a delayed-choice quantum eraser is the cost of $\delta$ applying the equality outside causation (Axiom 15). But for that result to reach the screen (the classical record) it must pass through the pipeline on the time axis, so this cost is read as the number of pipeline stages (Axiom 3).

Step 2. Norm Substitution

The norm being substituted is the pipeline-stage norm. It is chosen because reaching the screen requires the 4 cost stages trigger·filter·update·render (P-52), and even though the $\delta$ equality is atemporal (P-112) it must pass these 4 stages to become an observable signal. The minimum delay is therefore substituted by the number of pipeline stages times the system-time period.

$\tau_{\min} = N_{\text{stage}} \cdot t_P$
$\tau_{\min} = 4\,t_P$
$\tau_{\min}$: minimum delay of an observable retrocausal signal | $N_{\text{stage}}$: number of pipeline stages $=4$ (P-52) | $t_P$: system-time period (Planck time) | $4\,t_P$: screen-render floor

Step 3. Constant Substitution

$N_{stage}$ = 4 pipeline trigger filter update render
$t_{P}$ = $5.39\times10^{-44}$ s system-time period
