Core Finding

P-124: Domain-4-bit Derivation of the Vacuum-birefringence Magnitude

$$\Delta n=\frac{4\alpha^2}{15}\cdot\frac{B^2}{B_\text{cr}^2}\cdot\frac{1}{2^4}\quad(\text{suppressed by }1/16\ \text{relative to QED, domain 4 bits}=2^4)$$

Prediction: vacuum birefringence is suppressed by $1/16$ relative to QED. Test: PVLAS-FE, BMV ($10^{-24}$, 2030~).

In the Banya formula $\delta^2=(\text{time}+\text{space})^2+(\text{observer}+\text{superposition})^2$, vacuum birefringence (the refractive-index anisotropy of the vacuum in a strong magnetic field) arises from the polarization selection among the orthogonal states of the 4 domain axes (Axiom 1, Axiom 5).

The birefringence magnitude is derived as $\Delta n=(4\alpha^2/15)(B^2/B_\text{cr}^2)(1/2^4)$.

$1/2^4=1/16$ is the full address space of the 4 domain bits (Axiom 5), the rate of selecting one polarization among 16 orthogonal states.

So vacuum birefringence is the standard QED value times a $1/16$ suppression factor ==> Compare conditional Swap (Axiom 7) passes only one polarization of 16.

16 is the domain 4 bits $2^4$, and since polarization selection occurs in that 16-state orthogonal space, the $1/16$ suppression arises.

So this prediction is not "vacuum birefringence exists" but the quantitative claim "exactly $1/16$ suppression relative to QED."

The $1/16$ suppression is in the same domain-4-bit family as the decoherence threshold 16 (P-56) and weak measurement 1/16 (P-101).

Standard QED predicts birefringence without a suppression factor, so the existence of the $1/16$ suppression is the discriminator.

Test: if next-generation PVLAS-FE and BMV (target sensitivity $10^{-24}$, 2030~) find vacuum birefringence suppressed by $1/16$ relative to QED, this prediction is supported.

Refutation: if vacuum birefringence matches QED exactly with no $1/16$ suppression, this prediction is discarded.

Step 1. Banya Formula

$$\delta^2 = (\text{time} + \text{space})^2 + (\text{observer} + \text{superposition})^2$$

Vacuum birefringence is the refractive-index anisotropy of the vacuum in a strong magnetic field, arising from the polarization selection among the orthogonal states of the 4 domain axes (Axiom 1). It is read through the domain 4-bit address space (Axiom 5).

Step 2. Norm Substitution

The norm being substituted is the domain 4-bit $2^4=16$ address norm. It is chosen because polarization selection is the rate of picking one of the 16 states of the full orthogonal address space of the 4 domain axes, and Compare conditional Swap (Axiom 7) passes only one polarization of 16. The birefringence is therefore the standard QED value times a $1/16$ suppression factor.

$\Delta n^\text{QED} = \dfrac{4\alpha^2}{15}\cdot\dfrac{B^2}{B_\text{cr}^2}$
$\Delta n = \Delta n^\text{QED}\cdot\dfrac{1}{2^4}$
$2^4=16$: domain 4-bit address space (Axiom 5) | $4\alpha^2/15$: Euler-Heisenberg coefficient | $B_\text{cr}$: critical magnetic field | polarization selection: Compare conditional Swap (Axiom 7)
