H-998. Mobius function = phase $(-1)^n$ of prime-axis bits: the fermionic primon gas and μ = 0 exclusion

Key finding

H-998: square-free integers are bit strings over mutually orthogonal prime axes; the Mobius function is the phase $(-1)^n$ after one + crossing per bit that is on; μ = 0 is the exclusion that one axis cannot hold its bit on twice

$$n = \prod_p p^{b_p},\; b_p\in\{0,1\} \;\Rightarrow\; \mu(n) = (-1)^{\sum_p b_p}, \qquad \exists\,p:\, p^2\mid n \;\Rightarrow\; \mu(n) = 0, \qquad \sum_{n}\frac{\mu(n)}{n^{s}} = \frac{1}{\zeta(s)}$$

Hypothesis -- a structural correspondence at the level of identities (no error concept). Recovers from axiom passages the same structure as the fermionic primon gas of the physics literature (Julia 1990, Spector 1990)

Every integer factors into primes in exactly one way, so an integer is an integer coordinate vector over prime axes, and the prime axes are mutually independent. The primon gas of the physics literature uses exactly this: one independent mode of energy $\log p$ per prime gives the partition function $\zeta(s) = \prod_p(1-p^{-s})^{-1}$; making the modes fermionic restricts occupation to 0 or 1, the states become square-free integers, the fermion-number parity $(-1)^F$ is exactly $\mu(n)$, and its sum (the Witten index) is $1/\zeta(s)$.

In Banya three passages give this structure directly. First, the Axiom 1 proposition (4-axis orthogonality = 4 bits): "orthogonal = independent; independent axes switch ON/OFF without interfering; independent ON/OFF without interference = a bit" -- a prime axis with occupation 0/1 is a bit. Second, the Axiom 1 proposition (unit-circle form of the Banya equation): "the phase after n + crossings = $(-1)^n$" -- reading each prime bit that is on as one + crossing, $\mu(n) = (-1)^{\omega(n)}$ is that phase. Third, a bit is either 0 or 1, so a state switching the same axis on twice ($p^2 \mid n$) cannot be written as a bit string, and its value there is 0. This is the same place as the exclusion readings of the existing cards H-135 (Pauli exclusion = Compare fail) and H-576 (Fermi-Dirac = Swap exclusion).

Re-substitution use: splits the open item of H-996 "orthogonal independence of μ" in two: independence across prime axes (multiplicative side) holds exactly by unique factorization; what is unproven is independence when the integers are lined up in size order (additive side). The mode energy $\log p$ is the same logarithm as the domain-time scale of H-995.

Why it stops at Hypothesis: a correspondence between mathematical identities and axiom passages; no new number is produced. Reading one prime as one Banya orthogonal axis is an interpretation; the axioms' axes close at 4 + 3 = 7, so the infinitely many prime axes must be read not as axiom axes but as coordinates of DATA-side multiplicity (Axiom 12 ECS entities). Formalizing that assignment is incomplete.

Five-step recursive substitution: reading the Mobius function as a bit phase

Step 1. Banya equation

$$\delta^2 = (\text{time} + \text{space})^2 + (\text{observer} + \text{superposition})^2$$

Orthogonal axes are independent, and the ON/OFF of an independent axis is a bit. In the unit-circle form one + crossing flips the phase from $+1$ to $-1$.

Step 2. Norm substitution

$n = \prod_p p^{b_p}$, $b_p \in \{0,1\}$ ==> $\mu(n) = (-1)^{\sum b_p}$; $\;b_p \ge 2$ ==> $\mu(n) = 0$
$\prod_p (1 - p^{-s}) = \sum_n \mu(n)\, n^{-s} = 1/\zeta(s)$ (bit 0 or 1 per prime axis; phase $-1$ when on)
$b_p$: bit of prime axis $p$ (Axiom 1 proposition 4-axis orthogonality = 4 bits) | $(-1)^n$: phase of n + crossings (Axiom 1 proposition unit-circle form) | product $\prod_p$: combination of unordered independent axes

Step 3. Constant substitution

$\mu(1)=1,\ \mu(2)=-1,\ \mu(6)=+1,\ \mu(30)=-1,\ \mu(4)=0,\ \mu(12)=0$
Square-free Dirichlet series $\sum_n |\mu(n)| n^{-s} = \zeta(s)/\zeta(2s)$; natural density $1/\zeta(2) = 6/\pi^2 = 0.6079$

Step 4. Domain conversion

Converted to statistical mechanics, $s$ is the inverse temperature, $\log p$ the mode energy, $\zeta(s)$ the bosonic partition function and $1/\zeta(s)$ the fermionic Witten index. The $6/\pi^2$ in the coefficient $\sqrt{6/\pi^2}$ of H-996 is identified in this domain as $1/\zeta(2)$, the density of states the fermionic gas allows.
