Axiom 8 (every-tick polling)

5-Step Recursive Substitution: Deriving the Minimum Tick Interval from the Polling Tick Cost

Step 1. Banya Formula

$$\delta^2 = (\text{time} + \text{space})^2 + (\text{observer} + \text{superposition})^2$$

Dynamics is the cost the CAS pays to poll each tick and run Swap when the condition holds. The accumulation of the left side $\delta^2$ of the Banya formula is time itself, and time is defined as the order of polls. The dynamics cost is thus read as the minimum interval of polling ticks (Axiom 8).

Step 2. Norm Substitution

(a) The norm being substituted is the time norm of the polling tick interval. (b) Without polling there is no Swap trigger and the state is static (time stops), while every-tick polling produces dynamics — hence this norm (Axiom 8). The minimum tick interval $\Delta t$ is therefore substituted by the least time one poll occupies = the Planck time.

$\forall\, t_n:\ \text{Poll}(t_n)\ \Rightarrow\ \text{CAS check}$
$\Delta t = t_P,\qquad \nu = 1/\Delta t$
$\Delta t$: minimum polling tick interval | $t_P$: Planck time | $\nu$: polling frequency | $t_n$: the $n$-th tick

Step 3. Constant Substitution

t_P = 5.391 x 10^-44 s    Planck time
nu  = 1/t_P
    = 1.855 x 10^43 Hz

Step 4. Domain Transformation

(a) The transformation is into the dynamics time-evolution domain. (b) What it reveals is the polling frequency $\nu$, the number of CAS checks per second. The reciprocal of the minimum tick interval yields that value.

$\nu = 1/t_P = 1/(5.391\times10^{-44}) \approx 1.855\times10^{43}\ \text{Hz}$

Step 5. Discovery

Derived: $\nu \approx 1.9\times10^{43}\ \text{Hz}$ ($\Delta t = t_P$)
Necessity: no polling means a static state (time stops)
Sufficiency: every-tick polling produces every-tick dynamics (time evolution closes)
