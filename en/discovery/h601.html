5-Step Recursive Substitution: Deriving the QKD Eavesdropping Threshold Error Rate from the CAS Compare Disturbance Cost

Step 1. Banya Formula

$$\delta^2 = (\text{time} + \text{space})^2 + (\text{observer} + \text{superposition})^2$$

Eavesdropping is a third Compare inserted between the two legitimate parties. In Axiom 2 a Compare reads the superposition and fixes the state, so an extra Compare pays an extra cost and collapses the superposition. In Axiom 4 that cost is irreversible and leaves a trace — this trace is the source of eavesdropping detection.

Step 2. Norm Substitution

(a) The norm being substituted is the d-ring 1-bit Compare norm that separates the two bases (rectilinear and diagonal). BB84 randomly alternates two CAS bases, and the Swap outcomes 0 and 1 of one bit are the two poles of the norm. (b) Why this norm: when the eavesdropper guesses the basis wrong, the Compare collapses the superposition along the wrong axis and injects errors into the legitimate measurement. With the eavesdropper intercepting on a random basis each time, each of the half-the-time basis mismatches errs with probability 1/2, so the upper-bound disturbance closes at $1/2 \times 1/2 = 1/4$.

$Q = \dfrac{1}{2}\cdot\dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{1}{4}$
$Q_{\text{th}} = 1 - 2H_2^{-1}\!\left(\tfrac12\right) \;\text{(BB84 threshold)}$
$Q$: max error rate injected by eavesdropper | $1/2$: basis-mismatch probability | $1/2$: Swap-error probability on mismatch | $Q_{\text{th}}$: threshold error rate for secure key extraction | $H_2$: binary entropy
