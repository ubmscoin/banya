H-815: Optical Theorem = Imaginary Part of CAS Total Cost in Forward Scattering

Grade: C

The optical theorem states that the total scattering cross-section is proportional to the imaginary part of the forward scattering amplitude. In Banya, the total cost of CAS (sum of costs across all channels) equals the imaginary cost component of the forward Compare (θ = 0). This is a consequence of unitarity (probability conservation).

Axiom 2 (CAS Compare), Axiom 4 (cost)

Axiom 2 (CAS Compare = scattering), Axiom 4 (cost +1 = cross-section contribution). CAS unitarity = total probability = 1 = completeness of FSM state transitions. Completeness ==> total cost = imaginary part of forward cost. This is the optical theorem.

The total cross-section cannot be negative (FSM norm ≥ 0). The energy dependence of the total cross-section is the energy scaling of CAS cost. The Froissart bound ($\sigma \lesssim \ln^2 s$) is derived from the causality constraint on CAS cost (H-811).
