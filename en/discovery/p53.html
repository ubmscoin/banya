P-53. $K^\pm$ Indexing Correction 27 MeV. Lattice QCD

Core Finding

P-53: $K^\pm$ Indexing Correction 27 MeV. Lattice QCD

$$\Delta m(K^\pm)\supset 27\ \text{MeV}=1\times3^3\quad(\text{CAS indexing cost quantum})$$

Prediction: the CAS indexing correction contributes 27 MeV to the $K^\pm$ mass. Test: Lattice QCD (2027~).

In the Banya formula $\delta^2=(\text{time}+\text{space})^2+(\text{observer}+\text{superposition})^2$, the mass splitting of charged mesons reflects the cost CAS pays to index quarks (Axiom 2, Axiom 4).

The base quantum of the indexing cost is 27 MeV = $1\times3^3$ — $3^3=27$ is an axiom integer from the three-generation structure.

This prediction states that one base quantum, i.e. 27 MeV, contributes to the mass splitting of $K^\pm$ (the strange meson).

$K$ corresponds to skipping one generation (u/d ==> s), so the indexing step is one unit ==> $1\times27=27$ MeV.

It grows as a staircase in heavier mesons: $D^\pm$ ~130 MeV (about 5 units, P-54), $B^\pm$ ~430 MeV (about 16 units, P-55), $B_c^\pm$ 81 MeV ($3\times27$, P-75).

So this prediction is not "K mass has a correction" but the quantitative claim "exactly 27 MeV = one $3^3$ quantum unit."

That this 27 MeV quantum appears universally across all charged mesons is the core of P-64 (universal formula).

Observational strategy: lattice QCD decomposes the electromagnetic and indexing components of the $K^\pm$ mass splitting and directly computes the 27 MeV contribution.

Test: if precise lattice-QCD calculations (2027~) confirm the $K^\pm$ indexing contribution at 27 MeV, this prediction is supported.

Refutation: if lattice-QCD results disagree with 27 MeV by more than $3\sigma$, this prediction is discarded.
