A quantum gate is the reversible transformation that changes state at the CAS Compare stage (Axiom 2). Because the total cost before and after must be conserved (Axiom 4), the gate is subject to the condition that the Banya norm $\delta^2$ is invariant under the transformation.

Step 2. Norm Substitution

(a) The norm being substituted is the CAS cost-conservation norm $\delta^2$. When a gate carries state $|\psi\rangle$ to $U|\psi\rangle$, cost conservation translates into norm conservation $\langle U\psi|U\psi\rangle = \langle\psi|\psi\rangle$. (b) This norm is chosen because the necessary and sufficient condition for norm invariance to hold for all $|\psi\rangle$ is $U^\dagger U = I$, the unitarity condition. Irreversible gates leak cost (violating Axiom 4) and are excluded. Why this norm: the quantum-gate unitarity condition itself originates from CAS cost conservation, so only a norm that carries that very cost structure maps onto it without slack. Why the substitution is valid: the standard physics form on the left and the Banya form on the right denote the same quantity, the latter merely rewriting it in CAS cost terms.
