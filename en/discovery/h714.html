Core Finding

H-714: Squeezed State = Asymmetric Reduction of CAS Cost Fluctuation

$$\Delta X_1 \cdot \Delta X_2 \geq \frac{1}{4},\quad \Delta X_1 < \frac{1}{2} \;\leftrightarrow\; \text{reduction of CAS cost fluctuation on one side}$$

Grade: B

A squeezed state is a state in which the quantum fluctuation of one of the two orthogonal components of the CAS cost ($X_1, X_2$) is reduced below the uncertainty-relation limit while the other component is enlarged.

Axiom 4 (CAS cost fluctuation), Axiom 9 (quantum uncertainty)

Axiom 4 (two orthogonal components of CAS cost), Axiom 9 (uncertainty relation = lower bound of cost fluctuation), H-700 (squeezing produced by nonlinear processes)

Squeezing produced by OPA (optical parametric amplification). Squeezing parameter $r$: $\Delta X_1 = e^{-r}/2$. LIGO quantum-noise reduction. Fundamental resource of Gaussian quantum information.

Maximum squeezing: $\sim 15\;\text{dB}$ (Vahlbruch 2016). LIGO: $\sim 6\;\text{dB}$ of squeezing applied.

Consistent with squeezed-state measurement (homodyne detection).

A squeezed state reduces the fluctuation of one of the two orthogonal CAS cost components $X_1, X_2$ below the standard limit and enlarges the other. This asymmetric reduction is read through the lower bound on cost fluctuation (Axiom 9 uncertainty relation) and the two orthogonal components (Axiom 4).

Step 2. Norm Substitution

The norm being substituted is the product lower-bound norm of cost fluctuation. This norm is chosen because the product $\Delta X_1\,\Delta X_2$ is bound at a minimum, yet reducing a single component alone has no lower bound, so the squeezing parameter $r$ can exponentially compress one side. The asymmetric reduction is therefore substituted by an exponential function of $r$. Why this norm: the squeezing parameter $r$ itself originates from the CAS cost-fluctuation lower bound, so only a norm that carries that very cost structure maps onto it without slack. Why the substitution is valid: the standard physics form on the left and the Banya form on the right denote the same quantity, the latter merely rewriting it in CAS cost terms.

$\Delta X_1\,\Delta X_2 \geq \dfrac{1}{4}$
$\Delta X_1 = \dfrac{e^{-r}}{2}, \quad \Delta X_2 = \dfrac{e^{+r}}{2}$
$\Delta X_1,\Delta X_2$: two orthogonal cost fluctuations | $1/4$: product lower bound (Axiom 9) | $r$: squeezing parameter | product stays at the bound, one side compresses as $e^{-r}$
