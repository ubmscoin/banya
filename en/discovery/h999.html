H-999. Two thresholds of the sound horizon: the side released first has the shorter accumulated length ($r_* < r_\text{drag}$)

Key finding

H-999: the release threshold is set by the write-maintenance cost. Since decay is continuous and the threshold is discrete, two grips with different thresholds are released at different moments on the same continuous decay. Recovery continues between the two releases, so the accumulated length measured on the side released first is shorter than that measured on the side released later.

$$r_* < r_\text{drag} = 3\times7^2 = 147\ \text{Mpc},\qquad \frac{r_*}{r_\text{drag}} \overset{?}{=} \frac{56}{57} = 0.98246\ \ (\text{numerical basis incomplete})$$

Hypothesis -- the direction ($r_* < r_\text{drag}$) follows from axiom passages. The ratio 56/57 was chosen after seeing the measurement. Observed: $r_* = 144.39\pm0.30$, $r_\text{drag} = 147.09\pm0.26$ Mpc (Planck 2018), ratio 0.98164

Axiom 6 states that the total cost incurred when CAS makes a grip is recorded in the RLU, and that the RLU recovers the residual cost continuously. In the same place it states that decay is continuous and the threshold is discrete (Axiom 2 proposition). The value of the threshold is set by the write-threshold proposition of Axiom 2: threshold = write-maintenance cost / total CAS cost = 4/13; when the grip strength falls below this value the three-axis grip releases and the ball is discretely freed from DATA. Separately, in Axiom 7, when Compare is false the Swap is not executed and superposition is maintained.

Combining these passages, two grips with different thresholds follow the same continuous decay and are released at different moments. After release the length of that grip no longer accumulates, so the accumulated length measured on the grip released first is shorter than that measured on the grip released later. What follows from the axioms is this order and the inequality.

Mining correspondence: the side released first is recombination (photon decoupling) and the side released later is drag (baryon release). The 147 Mpc of D-63 is compared with the Planck $r_\text{drag}$ measurement 147.09, so it is the drag-epoch horizon. Hence the recombination horizon $r_*$ must be shorter than 147, and the observed 144.39 agrees with this direction.

Numbers: taking the ratio as $56/57 = 1 - 1/57$ gives $r_* = 147\times56/57 = 144.42$ Mpc, 0.02% from the observed 144.39. The ratio, however, does not follow from the axioms. 57 is not a registered number of Axiom 9, and reading 57 as a count of RLU slots conflicts with the Axiom 13 proposition that "the RLU does not use logical addresses (slot numbers)". The ratio was chosen after seeing the measurement, so chance agreement from multiple comparisons cannot be excluded.

Re-substitution use: grounds for correcting the label of D-63 from $r_s$ to $r_\text{drag}$. P-527 is rewritten to predict $r_\text{drag} = 147$ directly, and this card supplies the direction that $r_*$ is shorter. The acoustic angles of P-241 and D-136 must use $r_*$ and therefore depend on this card.

Why it stops at Hypothesis: the axioms contain only one write-maintenance cost (4) and one threshold (4/13). The value of a second threshold is not fixed, so the ratio of the two horizons cannot be computed from the axioms. The direction follows from the axioms; the number is incomplete.
