Photoelectric emission is the cost of a single photon activating the CAS Compare and triggering a Swap. The Swap is written only if Compare is true (Axiom 7: Swap if Compare true, superposition held if false), and since the CAS is atomic, partial activation is impossible (Axiom 2), so the threshold — that the photon energy must exceed the activation minimum cost $W$ — is read as a $\delta^2$ cost boundary.

Step 2. Norm Substitution

(a) The norm being substituted is the Compare-activation cost norm, where the work function $W$ is its minimum boundary count (Axiom 4: minimum boundary count). (b) This norm is chosen because, by CAS atomicity, it is the frequency of a single photon rather than light intensity (photon count) that solely sets whether activation occurs — if $h\nu < W$ then Compare is false, no Swap, no emission. The kinetic energy of the emitted electron is therefore substituted by the remainder of the photon energy after subtracting the activation cost. Why this norm: the Photoelectric Threshold $E_k = h\nu - W$ itself originates from the CAS Compare Activation Cost, so only a norm that carries that very cost structure maps onto it without slack. Why the substitution is valid: the standard physics form on the left and the Banya form on the right denote the same quantity, the latter merely rewriting it in CAS cost terms.

$E_k = E_{\text{photon}} - E_{\text{activation}}$
$E_k = h\nu - W$
