H-509. EMC Effect = CAS Cost Modification Inside the Nucleus

Core Finding

H-509: EMC effect = CAS cost modification inside the nucleus

$$R_{\text{EMC}} = \frac{F_2^A}{A \cdot F_2^N} \neq 1,\quad \text{CAS cost}(A) \neq A \cdot \text{CAS cost}(N)$$

Grade: C

The EMC effect is the phenomenon in which the structure function of a nucleon inside a nucleus differs from that of a free nucleon. In the Banya Framework this is because the nuclear environment modifies the CAS cost. When many FSM-closed regions (nucleons) are densely packed, RLU paths superpose and the cost structure changes. Fermi motion of the nucleons, binding energy, and nucleon swelling are all unified as CAS cost modifications.

Axiom 4 (CAS cost modification), Axiom 6 (RLU path superposition), H-495 (structure function)

Axiom 4 (cost $+1$/boundary: changes in boundary density inside the nucleus ==> cost modification), Axiom 6 (RLU shared across multiple nucleons ==> path interference), Axiom 12 (superposition of FSM-closure regions ==> partial release), Axiom 11 (interaction $\propto 1/\ell^2$: $\ell$ varies inside the nucleus)

Small $x$ shadowing: RLU path superposition ==> cost decreases. Intermediate $x$ suppression (EMC region): nucleon swelling ==> FSM norm redistribution. Large $x$ Fermi motion: high-momentum component added. Anti-shadowing: RLU compensation effect.

$R_{\text{EMC}} \approx 0.85$ ($x \sim 0.6$, Fe). Shadowing $R \approx 0.8$ ($x < 0.05$). Anti-shadowing $R \approx 1.05$ ($x \sim 0.1$).

Qualitative agreement with EMC/NMC/E139 data. Quantitative $A$-dependence agreement to within $\sim 20\%$.

The EMC ratio is therefore substituted by the in-nucleus cost ratio relative to the free nucleon.
