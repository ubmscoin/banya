P-100: 2-nibble Orthogonality = The Quantum-Classical Boundary is Cost

$$\text{quantum-classical boundary}=\text{nibble 0(DATA)}\times\text{nibble 1(OPERATOR)}\ \text{crossing cost},\ N_\text{env}=16=2^4\ \text{: decoherence minimal}$$

Prediction: the quantum-classical boundary = 2-nibble crossing cost; decoherence time is minimal at $N_\text{env}=16=2^4$. Test: decoherence vs environment DOF (2029~).

In the Banya formula $\delta^2=(\text{time}+\text{space})^2+(\text{observer}+\text{superposition})^2$, the 8 bits split orthogonally into nibble 0 (DATA, 4 domain bits) and nibble 1 (OPERATOR, CAS 3 + δ) (Axiom 1 2 nibbles).

The quantum-classical boundary (decoherence) is the cost incurred as the two nibbles cross (Axiom 7 bracket crossing).

So the transition from quantum to classical is not a mysterious collapse but the cost accounting of the DATA-nibble and OPERATOR-nibble crossing.

When the environment DOF reaches $N_\text{env}=16=2^4$ (the entire domain nibble), the decoherence time is minimal ==> the fastest classicalization.

16 is the full address space of the 4 domain bits, so when the environment fills all 16 modes, quantum coherence disappears fastest (P-56 threshold 16).

So this prediction is not "the environment causes decoherence" but the structural quantitative claim "the boundary = 2-nibble crossing cost, decoherence minimal at $N_\text{env}=16$."

This generalizes P-56 (decoherence threshold 16) as a quantum-classical boundary mechanism — at 16 the decoherence time shows a singular transition.

The key is that the quantum-classical boundary is set not by an absolute size (e.g. particle number) but by the environment-DOF-16 cost.
