In the Banya formula $\delta^2=(\text{time}+\text{space})^2+(\text{observer}+\text{superposition})^2$, the strong force manifests as the atomicity of CAS -- the three bits 111 of CAS are held as one block and cannot be split (Axiom 2, Axiom 14 FSM declaration).

This atomicity confining quarks inside the FSM is the confinement of the strong force. A quark cannot escape alone.

Proton decay $p\to e^+\pi^0$ proceeds via a dimension-6 operator, converting one of three quarks into a lepton and breaking baryon number.

CAS atomicity extremely suppresses this channel that would decompose the 111 block ==> because the cost of splitting an atomic unit is astronomically large.

The quantitative result of the suppression is the proton lifetime $\tau_p>10^{36}$ yr (P-02), well above the current Super-Kamiokande lower bound of $10^{34.4}$ yr.

So this prediction goes beyond "the proton is stable" to a quantitative lower bound "lifetime above $10^{36}$ yr," arising from the suppression by CAS atomicity.

Grand-unified theories (SU(5), etc.) predict short lifetimes of $10^{31}$-$10^{35}$ yr and are largely excluded -- the Banya framework predicts a longer lifetime.
