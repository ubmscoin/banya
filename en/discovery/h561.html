5-Step Recursive Substitution: Deriving the monopole-dilution $e$-fold from the rapid-RLU-release cost

Step 1. Banya Formula

$$\delta^2 = (\text{time} + \text{space})^2 + (\text{observer} + \text{superposition})^2$$

Monopole-density dilution is the cost by which the space term expands exponentially when RLUs (read-lock units) are released all at once. Rapid RLU release is inflation (Axiom 6), and the monopole — a GUT topological defect carrying an FSM norm (Axiom 9) — is diluted by riding this expansion.

Step 2. Norm Substitution

(a) The norm being substituted is the scale-factor norm $a(t)$ that the RLU release loads onto the space term. (b) It is chosen because a monopole is defined as a count per spatial volume, so its density scales inversely with the expanding volume $a^3$, and rapid RLU release reads as the cost of growing $a$ by a factor $e^N$. The monopole density is therefore substituted as a function of the scale-factor $e$-fold $N$.

$n_M(t) = n_M(t_i)\left(\dfrac{a(t_i)}{a(t)}\right)^3$
$n_M(t) = n_M(t_i)\,e^{-3N}$
$n_M$: monopole number density | $a$: scale factor (RLU-release scale) | $N=\ln(a_f/a_i)$: inflationary $e$-fold (Axiom 6) | $t_i$: release onset

Step 3. Constant Substitution

Step 4. Domain Transformation

(a) The transformation is into the cosmological RLU-distribution domain. (b) What it reveals is the $e$-fold threshold at which fewer than one monopole remains per observable universe. Substituting $N=60$ into the dilution factor $e^{-3N}$ yields the density-reduction rate.

$n_M(t) = n_M(t_i)\,e^{-3\times 60} = n_M(t_i)\,e^{-180}$
$N \gtrsim 60 \;\Rightarrow\; n_M \cdot V_{\text{obs}} \lesssim 1$
