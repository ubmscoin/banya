A Weyl semimetal is a cost structure in which the FSM norm (Axiom 14) vanishes at a point. Where the norm is zero the mass term disappears and the cost grows linearly with the momentum deviation. That zero is topologically protected (Axiom 11), so it appears only in pairs and is not easily gapped out.

Step 2. Norm Substitution

The norm being substituted is the FSM norm evaluated at the band-crossing point (H-618). The norm itself, rather than the static gap term of the dispersion, is substituted because the gap-closing condition is exactly the norm zero, and that zero defines the Weyl point. The first-order expansion of the norm makes the dispersion linear.

$E(\mathbf{k}) = \pm\sqrt{(\hbar v_F)^2|\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{k}_W|^2 + m^2}$
At the norm zero: $E(\mathbf{k}) = \pm\hbar v_F|\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{k}_W|$
$E$: band energy | $\mathbf{k}_W$: Weyl-point momentum | $v_F$: Fermi velocity | $m$: mass term = FSM norm | $\lVert\text{FSM}\rVert=0$: norm zero (Axiom 14)
