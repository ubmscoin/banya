The state in the Banya formula $\delta^2=(\text{time}+\text{space})^2+(\text{observer}+\text{superposition})^2$ rides on an 8-bit d-ring buffer (Axiom 15 proposition).

Entropy is the upper bound on the information one unit can hold. Since the d-ring is exactly 8 bits, the maximum entanglement entropy one d-ring unit can carry is 8 bits.

In nat units, $S_\text{max}=8\ln 2$ — $\ln 2$ nat per bit times 8 bits.

8 = 4 domain bits (nibble 0) + 3 CAS bits + 1 firing bit $\delta$ (nibble 1) (Axiom 1, Axiom 2).

So even at maximal entanglement with the environment, one unit cannot hold more than 8 bits ==> this bound follows directly from the d-ring bit count.

This is in the same spirit as the area law of black-hole entropy — the discrete limit of the information container sets the entropy bound.

So this prediction is not "entropy has a bound" but the quantitative claim "exactly $8\ln 2$."
