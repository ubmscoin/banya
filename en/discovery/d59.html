$$\frac{\tau_\tau}{\tau_\mu} \approx \frac{\alpha^3}{3}$$

Error 2.0%. * Grade B

The most compact CAS approximation of the tau/muon lifetime ratio: $\alpha^3/3$. Each of the 3 CAS stages (Read, Compare, Swap) accumulates one bracket cost (Axiom 4) of $\alpha$. $\alpha^3$ is the product of the costs of the 3 stages, and the denominator 3 normalizes by the CAS stage count. This approximation is a compact version of D-50 ($BR \times (m_\mu/m_\tau)^5$) and D-53 ($(2\pi/9)^5 \alpha^{5/2} \cdots$). If one approximately absorbs $(2\pi/9)^5$, $(1+\alpha/\pi)^{-5}$, and $BR$ from D-53, $\alpha^3/3$ remains. From the d-ring viewpoint, the CAS cost of one juim is $\alpha$, and this is incurred across 3 stages. Dividing by 3 takes the average over the 3 CAS stages, i.e. the mean cost density at the ring seam. This is the minimum-cost model of the juida operation, and is the intuitive summary of the mass-ratio model of D-50 and the pure-CAS model of D-53. From the workbench viewpoint, this is the simplest occupancy pattern with $\alpha$ cost placed on each of 3 workbench slots. With error 2.0%, it is coarser than D-50 (0.23%) or D-53 (0.6%), but it exposes the essence of the CAS structure most directly.
