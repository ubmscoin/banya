P-109: Retrocausality: Post-selection Probability Floor $=1/128$

$$P(\text{post-selection success})\geq\frac{1}{128}=\frac{1}{2^7}\quad(\text{7-bit effective state space is the bound})$$

Prediction: the success probability of any post-selection $\geq1/128$. Test: multi-photon post-selection (2028~).

In the Banya formula $\delta^2=(\text{time}+\text{space})^2+(\text{observer}+\text{superposition})^2$, $\delta$ permits backward description (post-selection) from outside the FSM (Axiom 15), but the 7-bit effective state space (128) imposes a bound.

Post-selection picks out only a specific result after measurement, and its success probability must be at least the weight of one mode of the 128-state space.

For any post-selection condition, the success probability is $\geq1/128=1/2^7$ ==> the minimum probability of picking one of 128.

A protocol requiring a post-selection probability lower than this has no place in the 128-state space and is structurally impossible.

So post-selection cannot pick out arbitrarily rare events — the floor is blocked at $1/128$.

This shows that δ permits backward description from outside the FSM, but the effective state space 128 (P-51) limits that freedom.

So this prediction is not "post-selection is possible" but the quantitative claim "the success probability floor is exactly $1/128$, below which is impossible."

$1/128$ is the probability version of the same 128 structure as inflation transition (P-60), ringdown (P-80), and scattering angle (P-98).
