H-521: Hawking radiation = CAS pair production cost separation near the event horizon

$$T_H = \frac{\hbar c^3}{8\pi G M k_B},\quad \text{CAS pair production: cost separation} \to \text{thermal radiation}$$

Grade: B

Hawking radiation is the phenomenon in which the cost of CAS pair production is split by the event horizon. When one member of a CAS pair ($q\bar{q}$, $e^+e^-$, etc.) created by a vacuum fluctuation falls inside the horizon and the other escapes outside, the cost separation of the FSM norm yields actual particle production.

Axiom 4 (cost separation), H-520 (event horizon = cost threshold), Axiom 8 (fluctuation per tick)

Axiom 4 (cost $+1$/boundary: the horizon plays the boundary role ==> cost separation of the pair), Axiom 8 (vacuum fluctuation every tick = CAS pair production), Axiom 14 (FSM norm ==> energy of the pair), Axiom 6 (RLU release ==> physical energy near the horizon)

Black-hole temperature $T_H \propto 1/M$: the smaller it is, the hotter. Black-hole evaporation: $dM/dt \propto -1/M^2$. Final-stage explosion. Information paradox (H-523): does thermal radiation lose pure-state information?

Solar-mass BH: $T_H \approx 6 \times 10^{-8}\;\text{K}$. Evaporation time $\sim 10^{67}$ yr. BH of $M \sim 10^{12}\;\text{kg}$: $T_H \sim 100\;\text{GeV}$, evaporation time $\sim$ age of the universe.

Direct observation impossible (temperature far too low). Analogue experiments (acoustic black holes) confirm the thermal spectrum.

5-Step Recursive Substitution: Deriving the Hawking temperature from the cost separation at the event-horizon boundary

Step 1. Banya Formula

$$\delta^2 = (\text{time} + \text{space})^2 + (\text{observer} + \text{superposition})^2$$

Hawking radiation is the cost separation paid when, of a CAS pair born from a per-tick vacuum fluctuation (Axiom 8), one falls inside the event-horizon boundary and the other escapes outside. Because the horizon acts as a cost-$+1$ boundary (Axiom 4), the two costs of the pair split and the escaping one becomes a real particle.

Step 2. Norm Substitution

The norm being substituted is the FSM norm $M$ (Axiom 14). This norm is chosen because the pair-production cost-separation rate is proportional to the horizon surface gravity, which in turn is inversely proportional to the FSM norm $M$. The Hawking temperature is therefore substituted by the cost of the surface gravity $\kappa = c^4/(4GM)$.

$T_H = \dfrac{\hbar\kappa}{2\pi c k_B}$
$T_H = \dfrac{\hbar c^3}{8\pi G M k_B}$
$T_H$: Hawking temperature | $\kappa$: surface gravity | $M$: FSM norm | $8\pi$: horizon-boundary solid angle $\times 2$ | $k_B$: cost-to-temperature conversion

Step 3. Constant Substitution

Step 4. Domain Transformation

The transformation is into the cosmology RLU-cache cost domain. What it reveals is the temperature $T_H$ of the thermal radiation a solar-mass black hole emits at its boundary. Converting the surface-gravity cost into a temperature yields that value.

$T_H = \dfrac{\hbar c^3}{8\pi G M_\odot k_B} = 6.17\times 10^{-8}\ \text{K}$
