H-996. Critical line 1/2 = the norm exponent of independent orthogonal units: $M(x) \sim \sqrt{x}$

Key finding

H-996: the exponent 1/2 of the Riemann hypothesis occupies the same place as the norm exponent $\sqrt{x} = x^{1/2}$ of $x$ mutually independent unit components combined orthogonally

$$M(x) = \sum_{n\le x}\mu(n), \qquad \text{RH} \iff M(x) = O\!\left(x^{1/2+\varepsilon}\right), \qquad \Big\|\underbrace{(\pm1,\ldots,\pm1)}_{x\text{ orthogonal units}}\Big\| = \sqrt{x}$$

Hypothesis -- a structural correspondence for the exponent 1/2. Littlewood's equivalence (1912) and Denjoy's independent-coin intuition (1931) rewritten through Banya's orthogonal = independent proposition

The Mobius function $\mu(n) \in \{-1, 0, +1\}$ is defined only through prime factorization, i.e. remainder operations, and the Mertens function $M(x)$ is its sum. Both are operations Axiom 3 allows on DATA (mod $N$, $\Sigma$), so $M(x)$ is a pure DATA quantity. The Riemann hypothesis is equivalent to $M(x) = O(x^{1/2+\varepsilon})$ (Littlewood 1912).

In Banya orthogonality is independence: Axiom 1 proposition (4-axis orthogonality = 4 bits) "orthogonal = independent; independent axes switch ON/OFF without interfering with one another". The norm of $N$ orthogonal axes is the square root of the axis count: Axiom 2 proposition "the norm is uniquely $\sqrt1, \sqrt2, \sqrt3$ according to the number of active axes", and the Axiom 1 proposition (recursion principle) "any $N$-axis orthogonal group defined by the axioms has the unit $N$-sphere form by the same pattern". So if the $\mu$ values of $x$ integers are mutually orthogonal independent units, the size scale of their sum is $\sqrt{x}$, and the exponent 1/2 is the exponent of the norm operation $\sqrt{\cdot}$. The Riemann hypothesis reads as "the prime-side fluctuations behave like independent orthogonal units that do not interfere".

Re-substitution use: H-995 handles the phase ($\gamma\log x$) and this card the amplitude ($x^{1/2}$), together covering the oscillatory term $x^\rho$ of the explicit formula. Basis of P-640 (prediction that the Riemann hypothesis holds).

Why it stops at Hypothesis: $\mu(n)$ is deterministic and multiplicatively correlated. That its values behave as orthogonal independent units is the content of the Riemann hypothesis itself; this card does not prove it but rewrites it in axiom vocabulary. Reading each integer as an independent entity (Axiom 12: each observer-CAS pair runs its own FSM, no synchronization) is at the interpretation level.

Why this verdict

Verdict: Hypothesis (structural correspondence for the exponent 1/2; independence unproven)

Reason: $M(x)$ defined only by DATA-allowed operations / orthogonal = independent (Axiom 1 proposition) / norm of $N$ axes = $\sqrt N$ (Axiom 2 proposition, Axiom 1 recursion principle) / independence of $\mu$ = RH itself, unproven

Location: Category: h structure. Domain: DATA integer staircase ==> norm of orthogonal units. Recursive substitution path: H-995, D-162

Five-step recursive substitution: reading the critical exponent 1/2 from the orthogonal norm

Step 1. Banya equation

$$\delta^2 = (\text{time} + \text{space})^2 + (\text{observer} + \text{superposition})^2$$

The Banya equation is a sum of squared norms of orthogonal axes. With orthogonal axes there are no cross terms, so the squared norm is the sum of squared components and the norm is its square root. This card applies that structure to $x$ integer unit components.

Step 2. Norm substitution

$\|M\|^2 = \sum_{n\le x}\mu(n)^2 = \#\{n \le x : n \text{ square-free}\} \approx \tfrac{6}{\pi^2}x$ (assuming zero cross terms)
$\|M\| \approx \sqrt{6/\pi^2}\,\sqrt{x} \;\Rightarrow\;$ exponent $1/2$
$\mu(n)$: Mobius function (mod operation, Axiom 3) | zero cross terms: orthogonal = independent (Axiom 1 proposition) | $\sqrt{\cdot}$: norm operation (Axiom 2 proposition); norm-derived values are not listed degrees of freedom and are used here only as an operation (Axiom 9) | $6/\pi^2$: density of square-free integers

Step 3. Constant substitution

$M(10^3) = 2,\; M(10^4) = -23,\; M(10^5) = -48,\; M(10^6) = 212$ (direct computation)
$|M(x)|/\sqrt{x}$: $0.063,\; 0.230,\; 0.152,\; 0.212$ -- all of the same order, below $\sqrt{6/\pi^2} = 0.780$

Step 4. Domain conversion

Converted to the prime-counting function the Riemann hypothesis is $\pi(x) - \mathrm{Li}(x) = O(\sqrt{x}\log x)$; in the explicit formula of H-995 it says all oscillatory terms share the amplitude $x^{1/2}$. All three forms share the exponent 1/2, the exponent of the orthogonal norm.

Step 5. Finding

Verdict: Hypothesis. Falsification: if a nontrivial zero with real part other than 1/2 is found, the "norm of independent orthogonal units" reading is rejected (P-640). A norm is a size scale, not an upper bound, so the disproof of the Mertens conjecture $|M(x)| \lt \sqrt{x}$ (Odlyzko-te Riele 1985) does not contradict this reading.

Byproducts

The fluctuation envelope of a sum of independent units exceeds the norm $\sqrt{x}$ by an iterated-logarithm factor (order $\sqrt{x\log\log x}$). The failure of the Mertens conjecture is thus read as mistaking a norm for an upper bound. The distinction between norm (scale) and bound (envelope) is a rule to keep whenever another card uses $\sqrt{N}$.
