Core Finding

H-321: Quantum tunneling = δ bypasses the CAS cost barrier

$$\delta\text{ fire cost}=0\;\Rightarrow\;\delta\text{ bypasses Swap cost barrier}$$

Quantum tunneling is derived as the utterance bit δ bypassing the CAS cost barrier. Because δ utterance cost is 0, it can skip past Swap cost barriers inside the FSM.

Banya formula: since δ fire cost = 0, δ bypasses the Swap cost barrier. In the transmission probability $T \propto e^{-2\kappa L}$, $\kappa L$ equals the number of CAS stages within the barrier.

Axiomatic basis: Axiom 15 (δ = outside FSM, cost 0) permits barrier bypass. From Axiom 4 (cost = +1 when crossing +), Swap inside the FSM has cost > 0, but δ does not go through cost computation.

Structural consequence: the reason transmission probability decays exponentially with barrier width $L$ and height $V_0 - E$ is that the number of CAS stages inside the barrier is proportional to $L$, and the probability that Swap proceeds without δ utterance at each stage drops exponentially.

Numerics: in alpha decay, transmission probability $T \sim 10^{-30}$ to $10^{-40}$. In semiconductor tunnel diodes, $T \sim 10^{-1}$. The value varies greatly with barrier structure.

Consistency: emerges from the same principle (δ cost 0) as H-317 (teleportation = δ distance-independent) and H-318 (Bell violation = δ globality). In H-274 (δ duty cycle) the δ utterance probability is constrained.

Physical correspondence: quantum tunneling. The fundamental mechanism behind alpha decay (Gamow, 1928), scanning tunneling microscopy (STM), tunnel diodes, nuclear fusion, etc.

In conventional quantum mechanics, tunneling is explained by exponential decay of the wavefunction. In Banya, δ's cost-0 utterance establishes the equals sign "beyond" the barrier. Wavefunction decay corresponds to the exponential decrease in the number of CAS stages.

Verification: the distance dependence of the tunnel current in STM, $I \propto e^{-2\kappa d}$, is precisely confirmed. The correspondence between the number of CAS stages and $\kappa d$ can be quantitatively verified.

5-Step Recursive Substitution: Deriving the Quantum-Tunneling Transmission Probability from the δ Fire Cost of 0

Step 1. Banya Formula

$$\delta^2 = (\text{time} + \text{space})^2 + (\text{observer} + \text{superposition})^2$$

Quantum tunneling is the event in which the fire bit $\delta$ skips over the Swap cost barrier inside the FSM. The $\delta$ fire cost is 0 (Axiom 15: $\delta$ is outside the FSM), so it can establish the equality beyond the barrier without paying the Swap cost imposed by Axiom 4 (cost = $+1$ when crossing a boundary).

Step 2. Norm Substitution

The norm being substituted is the norm of the number of CAS steps inside the barrier. The wider the barrier width $L$, the more Swap steps must proceed within it in proportion, and the probability that each step passes without a $\delta$ fire multiplies per step, so the transmission probability decays exponentially. This cost norm is chosen because it sets the transmission probability, and the standard quantum-mechanical exponential-decay form is substituted into a CAS-step-count form.

$T = e^{-2\kappa L}$
$T = e^{-2\,n_{\text{CAS}}}$
$T$: transmission probability | $\kappa$: decay constant inside the barrier | $L$: barrier width | $n_{\text{CAS}}=\kappa L$: number of CAS steps inside the barrier | $\delta$: fire bit, cost 0

Step 3. Constant Substitution

$\kappa$ = sqrt(2 m (V0 - E)) / hbar
$T_\alpha$ = $10^{-30}$ $\sim$ $10^{-40}$ $\alpha$ decay (Gamow 1928)
$T_{diode}$ = $10^{-1}$ tunnel diode
$I_{STM}$ $\sim$ $e^(-2$ $\kappa$ d) scanning tunneling microscope

Step 4. Domain Transformation

The transformation is into the quantum/information domain (establishing the equality beyond the barrier). What it reveals is the transmission probability $T$ that one particle bypasses the barrier by a $\delta$ fire. Putting the CAS step count $n_{\text{CAS}}$ produced by the barrier width into the exponent yields that value.

$T = e^{-2\,n_{\text{CAS}}},\quad n_{\text{CAS}}=\kappa L$
