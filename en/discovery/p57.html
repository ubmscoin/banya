P-57: $C(7,2)=21$ = Meson State Ceiling

$$\binom{7}{2}=21=\text{combinatorial ceiling of meson states}\quad(\text{quark-antiquark combinations of the 7-bit index})$$

Prediction: the combinatorial ceiling of independent meson states is $\binom{7}{2}=21$. Test: LHCb/Belle II spectroscopy (2028~).

In the Banya formula $\delta^2=(\text{time}+\text{space})^2+(\text{observer}+\text{superposition})^2$, a meson is a CAS indexing state of a quark-antiquark pair, and the index is 7 bits (full-description DOF, Axiom 9).

A meson chooses two slots (quark and antiquark) from the 7-bit index ==> the number of independent combinations is $\binom{7}{2}=21$.

So this prediction states that the combinatorial ceiling of independent meson states (basis states within the same quantum-number family) is 21.

21 is the first term in the decomposition 57 = 21+35+1 of P-24/P-50 ($C(7,2)$), a core integer of the CAS combinatorial structure.

So independent meson basis states beyond 21 have no place in the 7-bit index space and are structurally impossible.

So this prediction is not "there are many mesons" but the combinatorial quantitative claim "independent basis states are bounded at exactly 21."

Exotic mesons (tetraquarks, etc.) must also be explained within this index combination and do not require new basis DOF beyond 21.

Observational strategy: count the number of independent basis states in LHCb/Belle II meson spectroscopy.

Test: if independent meson states do not exceed 21 in LHCb/Belle II (2028~), this prediction is supported.

Refutation: if an independent meson basis state exceeding 21 is found at $5\sigma$, this prediction is discarded.
