H-683: Tidal Force = Gradient of Axiom 11 Interaction

$$\Delta a \approx \frac{2GMr}{d^3} \;\leftrightarrow\; \text{Axiom 11 cost gradient}$$

Grade: B

The tidal force is a differential force that arises from the non-uniformity (gradient) of the gravitational field. In Banya, the cost transfer of Axiom 11 decays with distance as $1/r^2$, so a cost difference appears between the near and far sides of an extended body = tidal force.

Axiom 11 (gravitational cost transfer), Axiom 4 (cost gradient)

Axiom 11 (gravitational cost = $GM/r^2$, gradient = $-2GM/r^3$), Axiom 4 (cost difference = tidal acceleration $\Delta a = 2GMr/d^3$, $r$ = body size, $d$ = center-to-center distance). Tidal torque ==> spin-orbit synchronization (Moon). Tidal dissipation ==> orbital change.

Lunar tides: ocean tides $\sim 1\;\text{m}$. Io tidal heating: energy source of its volcanism. Tidal locking: Moon-Earth, Pluto-Charon. Tidal lag ==> lunar recession ($3.8\;\text{cm/yr}$). Roche limit (H-684).

5-Step Recursive Substitution: Deriving the Tidal Acceleration from the Axiom 11 Cost Gradient

Step 1. Banya Formula

$$\delta^2 = (\text{time} + \text{space})^2 + (\text{observer} + \text{superposition})^2$$

The tidal force is the differential force arising when the gravitational cost (Axiom 11) is transmitted non-uniformly with distance. The difference in cost (Axiom 4 cost difference) felt by the near and far faces of an extended body is the tidal acceleration.

Step 2. Norm Substitution

The norm being substituted is the distance-gradient norm of the gravitational cost. It is chosen because as the cost $GM/r^2$ falls off as $1/r^2$, its gradient $-2GM/r^3$ produces a differential force across the body's size. The tidal acceleration is therefore substituted by the cost gradient times the body radius.

$g(r) = \dfrac{GM}{r^2},\quad \dfrac{dg}{dr} = -\dfrac{2GM}{r^3}$
$\Delta a \approx \dfrac{2GMr}{d^3}$
$\Delta a$: tidal acceleration | $M$: central body mass | $r$: body size | $d$: central distance | $2$: cost-gradient integer (Axiom 11)

Step 3. Constant Substitution

tidal accel = 2GMr/d^3         Axiom 11 gradient
Moon tide   = Δa/g ~ 1e-7      observed
Moon recession = 3.82 cm/yr    LLR
Io heating  = 1e14 W           observed

Step 4. Domain Transformation

The transformation is into the distance-gradient domain of the gravitational cost. What it reveals is the cost difference across the two ends of an extended body — the tidal acceleration $\Delta a$. Multiplying the cost gradient $-2GM/r^3$ by the body radius $r$ yields that value.

$\Delta a \approx \dfrac{2GMr}{d^3},\quad \text{Moon tide}\;\;\Delta a/g \sim 10^{-7}$
