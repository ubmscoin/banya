5-Step Recursive Substitution: Deriving the antibunching $g^{(2)}(0)$ from FSM norm-0 single emission

Step 1. Banya Formula

$$\delta^2 = (\text{time} + \text{space})^2 + (\text{observer} + \text{superposition})^2$$

Photon antibunching is the nonclassical correlation by which two photons from a single-photon source never arrive simultaneously. The photon is an FSM norm-0 entity (Axiom 3), and this anticorrelation is read through the cost structure in which a single CAS cost quantum is emitted only one at a time (Axiom 4).

Step 2. Norm Substitution

The norm being substituted is the single-emission second-order correlation norm. This norm is chosen because, when a source emits only one cost quantum at a time, the simultaneous (zero-delay) detection probability drops, giving $g^{(2)}(0)<1$. The antibunching is therefore substituted by the zero-delay normalized correlation.

$g^{(2)}(0) = \dfrac{\langle n(n-1)\rangle}{\langle n\rangle^2}$
$g^{(2)}_{\text{single}}(0) = 1 - \dfrac{1}{N}$
$g^{(2)}(0)$: zero-delay second-order correlation | $n$: detected quantum count | $N$: number of entities that can emit simultaneously | $N=1$ gives $g^{(2)}(0)=0$ (perfect single)

Step 3. Constant Substitution

thermal g2(0) = 2 bunching (Bose statistics)
laser g2(0) = 1 Poisson
QD single g2(0) < 0.01 quantum-dot measurement
NV center g2(0) = 0.1 NV-center measurement

Step 4. Domain Transformation

The transformation is into the electromagnetic-coupling domain. What it reveals is the zero-delay correlation of a single ($N=1$) emitter — the antibunching limit of a perfect single photon. Substituting $N=1$ into the formula above yields that value.

$g^{(2)}_{\text{single}}(0) = 1 - \dfrac{1}{1} = 0$
