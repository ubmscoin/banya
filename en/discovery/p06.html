P-06: Photon Dispersion

P-06 prediction (predictions.html#r5-photon)

In the Banya Framework, the Compare stage of CAS incurs a cost of $\alpha$. This cost grows as energy approaches the Planck energy.

Standard quantum-gravity models predict $\Delta v / c \sim (E/E_P)$ or $(E/E_P)^2$. The Banya Framework multiplies this by $\alpha$, because the Compare cost imparts a faint energy dependence even to the speed of light. The attachment of the concrete coefficient $\alpha$ is the Banya Framework's signature.

$$\delta^2 = (\text{time} + \text{space})^2 + (\text{observer} + \text{superposition})^2$$

Photon propagation rides on the cost the CAS Compare pays to select one candidate. That Compare cost is the fine-structure constant $\alpha$ (Axiom 2 proposition: data type 137), and as energy approaches the Planck energy $E_P$ the cost accumulates, imparting a faint energy dependence even to the speed of light.

Step 2. Norm Substitution

The norm being substituted is the Compare-cost norm $\alpha$. Photon dispersion is propagation that crosses the time and space brackets, and each crossing triggers one Compare, so the dimensionless ratio $(E/E_P)$ of standard quantum gravity carries a forced coefficient of the Compare cost $\alpha$. This $\alpha$ coefficient is the Banya Framework's signature. Why this norm: Photon Dispersion itself originates from the Compare Cost $\alpha$, so only a norm that carries that very cost structure maps onto it without slack. Why the substitution is valid: the standard physics form on the left and the Banya form on the right denote the same quantity, the latter merely rewriting it in CAS cost terms.

$\dfrac{\Delta v}{c} \sim \left(\dfrac{E}{E_P}\right)$
$\dfrac{\Delta v}{c} \sim \alpha\left(\dfrac{E}{E_P}\right)$
$\Delta v/c$: speed dispersion ratio | $\alpha$: Compare cost = fine-structure constant | $E$: photon energy | $E_P$: Planck energy

The transformation is into the photon-propagation domain. What it reveals is the magnitude of the dispersion coefficient by which a high-energy photon lags a low-energy one.
