5-Step Recursive Substitution: Deriving the Compton Shift $\tfrac{h}{m_e c}(1-\cos\theta)$ from the Read-Compare Cost Exchange

Step 1. Banya Formula

$$\delta^2 = (\text{time} + \text{space})^2 + (\text{observer} + \text{superposition})^2$$

Compton scattering is the process in which a photon (CAS cost packet) and an electron (FSM norm) elastically exchange cost over one Read-Compare-Swap cycle. The CAS proceeds in the order R then C then S (Axiom 2), and the electron mass is the FSM norm (Axiom 14). Since the scattering angle $\theta$ is the inner product of the input/output axes (Axiom 1), the exchanged cost is read on $\delta^2$.

Step 2. Norm Substitution

(a) The norm being substituted is the FSM norm (electron mass $m_e$), and the minimum exchanged cost unit is the CAS cost packet $h$. (b) This norm is chosen because the wavelength shift is fixed by the Compton wavelength $\lambda_C = h/(m_e c)$ — the CAS minimum cost divided by the FSM norm. The wavelength shift is therefore substituted by the product of $\lambda_C$ and the angular factor $(1-\cos\theta)$.

$\lambda_C = \dfrac{h}{m_e c}$
$\lambda' - \lambda = \dfrac{h}{m_e c}(1-\cos\theta)$
$\lambda'-\lambda$: wavelength shift | $h$: CAS minimum cost packet | $m_e$: electron FSM norm (Axiom 14) | $\cos\theta$: axis inner product (Axiom 1) | $\lambda_C$: Compton wavelength

Step 3. Constant Substitution

h = $6.626\times10^{-34}$ $J\times$ s
$m_{e}$ = $9.109\times10^{-31}$ $\text{kg}$
c = $2.998\times10^{8}$ m/s
$\lambda_{C}$ = $h/(m_{e}$ c) = $2.426\times10^{-12}$ m

Step 4. Domain Transformation

(a) The transformation is into the Read-Compare cost-exchange domain. (b) What it reveals is the angle-to-wavelength-shift relation. At $\theta=0$ the axis inner product is 1, so cost exchange is 0; at $\theta=\pi$, $(1-\cos\theta)=2$ gives maximum transfer. Multiplying $\lambda_C$ by the angular factor yields the value.

$\lambda' - \lambda = 2.426\times10^{-12}\,(1-\cos\theta)\ \text{m}$
