P-87. Max Simultaneous Inter-nibble Bit-AND Lock = 3

Core Finding

P-87: Max Simultaneous Inter-nibble Bit-AND Lock = 3

$$\max(\text{simultaneous nibble lock})=\min(4,3)=3\ \Rightarrow\ \text{observer bit always free (always observable)}$$

Prediction: maximum simultaneous inter-nibble lock = 3; the observer bit is always free. Test: quantum-information experiments (2028~).

In the Banya formula $\delta^2=(\text{time}+\text{space})^2+(\text{observer}+\text{superposition})^2$, the 8 bits split into nibble 0 (4 domain bits) and nibble 1 (CAS 3 + δ), and a CAS operation applies a simultaneous lock via a bit-AND between the two nibbles (Axiom 5).

The number of bits that can be simultaneously locked is limited by the smaller of the two nibbles -- $\min(4,3)=3$.

Since CAS is 3 bits (Read, Compare, Swap), it can lock at most 3 domain bits at once ==> the 4th domain bit is always free.

That 4th free bit is the observer (Axiom 10: observer = filter). The observer bit is never locked by CAS, so observation is always possible.

So during any CAS operation the observer can filter freely ==> the structural basis that observation is always possible in principle.

This is why measurement is always possible and the observer can exercise will (P-105) -- the observer bit is exempt from the lock.

So this prediction is not "observation is possible" but the structural claim "maximum simultaneous lock 3, the observer bit is lock-exempt."

3 = min(4,3) is a direct consequence of the CAS 3 steps and the 4 domain axes, leaving 4−3=1 bit (observer) always free.

Test: if the observer DOF (measurement accessibility) is maintained during any operation in quantum-information experiments (2028~), this prediction is supported.

Refutation: if a case is found where the observer bit is locked by CAS and observation is blocked, this prediction is discarded.
