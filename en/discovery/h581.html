5-Step Recursive Substitution: Deriving the Wien Displacement Constant $b$ from the CAS Cost Peak of d-ring Modes

Step 1. Banya Formula

The blackbody spectrum is the thermal-equilibrium distribution of d-ring discrete modes (Axiom 2) exchanging the CAS cost $+1$ (Axiom 4). Each mode's energy is quantized into $h\nu$ quanta (Axiom 3), and the wavelength where the product of mode density and occupation probability is maximal sets the peak.

Step 2. Norm Substitution

(a) The norm being substituted is the product of d-ring mode density and CAS-cost occupation — the Planck distribution norm $u_\lambda$. (b) The peak is the mode with the most active cost exchange, so the wavelength-derivative zero of this norm is sought. That zero is the root $x_{\max}$ of the dimensionless equation $x=5(1-e^{-x})$, and the Wien constant closes as $b=hc/(k_B x_{\max})$.

$\lambda_{\max} T = b = \dfrac{hc}{k_B\,x_{\max}}$
$x_{\max}=\dfrac{hc}{\lambda_{\max} k_B T}=5\bigl(1-e^{-x_{\max}}\bigr)$
$b$: Wien displacement constant | $x_{\max}$: zero of the Planck-distribution derivative | $h$: Planck constant (DATA quantum, Axiom 3) | $k_B$: cost-temperature conversion | $5$: exponent of the d-ring mode density $\lambda^{-5}$

Step 4. Domain Transformation

(a) The transformation is into the dimensionless mode domain ($x=hc/\lambda k_B T$). (b) What it reveals is the wavelength-temperature product of the mode with maximal cost exchange — the Wien displacement constant $b$. Substituting $x_{\max}$ into $hc/k_B$ yields that value.

$b=\dfrac{hc}{k_B\,x_{\max}}=\dfrac{(6.626\times10^{-34})(2.998\times10^{8})}{(1.381\times10^{-23})(4.9651)}=2.898\times10^{-3}\ \text{m K}$
