P-08: Discrete Cost Ceiling in Extreme-density Regions (no singularity)

$$\ell_{\min}=1,\qquad \max\frac{1}{\ell^2}=\frac{1}{1^2}=1,\qquad \text{dense } N\ \Rightarrow\ \text{cost}\propto N^2\ (\text{finite})$$

Prediction: per-pair cost ≤ 1/1²=1, no divergence ==> energy cutoff in high-density regions. Verify with ngEHT / X-ray (2030~)

In the Banya formula $\delta^2=(\text{time}+\text{space})^2+(\text{observer}+\text{superposition})^2$, DATA (the classical bracket) is discrete (Axiom 3). Spacetime is not continuous but an integer-celled d-ring.

The distance $\ell$ between two entities (ECS, Axiom 12) is an integer. In a discrete system its minimum is not 0 but $\ell=1$ -- the indivisible minimum unit (Axiom 3: there is no half a grasp).

The inter-entity interaction strength is $C(1-\ell/N)/(4\pi\ell^2)$ (Axiom 13 proposition). The maximum of the inverse-square term $1/\ell^2$ is $1/1^2=1$ at $\ell=1$.

So a single pair has a finite ceiling of 1. Since $\ell=0$ does not exist, the divergence $1/\ell^2\to\infty$ is structurally blocked (Axiom 13: no singularity).

When $N$ grasps pack at $\ell=1$, the cost over $N(N-1)/2$ pairs accumulates as $N^2$, but since each pair is finite the total is finite.

A black hole is not an "infinite-density singularity" but a finite-cost $N^2$ accumulation. Because CAS is a discrete operator (Axiom 2), energy has a finite maximum.

The singularities of physics are artifacts of assuming continuous spacetime and taking $1/r^2$ to $r\to0$ -- in the Banya structure $r\to0$ itself does not exist.

Hence below the Planck scale the energy spectrum must not diverge but show a finite cutoff (ceiling).

This follows deductively from Axiom 3 (discreteness) and Axiom 13 ($\ell\geq1$) with no added axiom.
