The nuclear pair effect is the phenomenon whereby like nucleons (proton-proton or neutron-neutron) form pairs and increase the binding energy. In Banya, FSM even-number coupling reduces cost via lock-bit (Axiom 9) anti-alignment (spin 0).

Axiom 9 (lock bit), Axiom 3 (FSM coupling), H-652 (shell model)

Even-even nuclei: $I^\pi = 0^+$ ground state (100% without exception). Even-odd nuclei: the unpaired nucleon determines the spin. Superfluid gap = nuclear version of BCS theory. Pair-breaking energy $\sim 2\Delta$.

Sn-120: $\Delta \approx 1.2\;\text{MeV}$. Pb-208: $\Delta \approx 0.7\;\text{MeV}$. Formula: $\Delta \approx 12/\sqrt{A}$.

$\Delta$ extracted from even-odd mass difference, $< 20\%$ consistency.

The pair effect appears when two like-nucleon FSMs bind through lock-bit (Axiom 9) anti-alignment, lowering the binding cost. CAS Swap exclusion (Axiom 5) forces a time-reversed orbital pair, and FSM even-number coupling (Axiom 3) closes that cost advantage as the pair gap $\Delta$.

Step 2. Norm Substitution

(a) The norm being substituted is the lock-bit anti-alignment cost norm over the FSM norm $A$ (nucleon number). (b) Because even-even nuclei have $0^+$ ground states without exception — lock-bit anti-alignment (spin 0) being the cost minimum — the pair gap is read through this norm, which decays as the inverse square root of the FSM norm. The Weizsacker pairing term is substituted into FSM-norm form.

$\delta_\text{pair} = \dfrac{a_P}{\sqrt{A}}$
$\Delta(A) = \dfrac{12}{\sqrt{A}}\;\text{MeV}$
$\Delta$: pair gap | $A$: FSM norm = nucleon number (Axiom 3) | $a_P=12\;\text{MeV}$: pairing-term coefficient | $\sqrt{A}$: inverse-square-root norm decay of lock-bit anti-alignment

Step 3. Constant Substitution

a_P     = 12 MeV            Weizsacker pairing-term coefficient
A(Sn)   = 120              Sn-120 nucleon number
A(Pb)   = 208              Pb-208 nucleon number
Delta_exp(Sn-120) = 1.2 MeV

Step 4. Domain Transformation

(a) The transformation is into the nuclear binding-energy domain (FSM norm $A$). (b) What it reveals is the pair gap $\Delta(A)$ of even-even nuclei. Dividing the pairing-term coefficient by the square root of the FSM norm yields the gap for both nuclei.

$\Delta(\text{Sn-}120) = \dfrac{12}{\sqrt{120}} = 1.10\;\text{MeV}$
$\Delta(\text{Pb-}208) = \dfrac{12}{\sqrt{208}} = 0.83\;\text{MeV}$
