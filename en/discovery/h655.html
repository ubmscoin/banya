H-655: Isotope Stability = Cost Minimization of FSM Norm Ratio

$$\frac{\partial B}{\partial Z}\bigg|_A = 0 \;\rightarrow\; Z_\text{stable}(A) \;\leftrightarrow\; \text{FSM minimum cost ratio}$$

Grade: B

Isotope stability means that, at a given mass number $A$, the cost (binding energy) is minimized when the proton number $Z$ takes a specific value. In Banya, the point where the proton-neutron ratio of the FSM norm minimizes cost = the stable nucleus.

Axiom 4 (cost minimum), H-643 (binding energy), H-648 (magic numbers)

Axiom 4 (cost minimization ==> stable configuration), Axiom 5 (CAS electromagnetic cost = Coulomb ==> protons disfavored), Axiom 3 (FSM exclusion = asymmetric energy ==> balance). Valley of stability: $Z_\text{stable} \approx A/(2 + 0.0154 A^{2/3})$.

Valley of stability: for light nuclei $N \approx Z$, for heavy nuclei $N > Z$. Beta decay = cost relaxation toward the valley of stability. Drip line = boundary where FSM binding cost = 0. About 290 stable isotopes.

$^{56}\text{Fe}$: $Z/A = 0.464$. $^{208}\text{Pb}$: $Z/A = 0.394$. Stable isotopes: $\sim 290$.

For a given mass number $A$, which nucleus is stable is the question of which configuration costs least. In Banya, cost minimization (Axiom 4) sets the stable configuration. The Coulomb cost (Axiom 5) penalizes protons, while the FSM-exclusion asymmetry cost (Axiom 3) pulls toward proton-neutron balance.

Step 2. Norm Substitution

(a) The norm being substituted is the FSM norm ratio that minimizes the binding cost with respect to $Z$. The sum of the Coulomb cost (Axiom 5) and the asymmetry cost (Axiom 3) sets the stable $Z$ at $\partial B/\partial Z=0$. (b) This norm is chosen because the competition between the two costs traces the valley of stability.

$\dfrac{\partial B}{\partial Z}\bigg|_A = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; Z_\text{stable}(A)$
$Z_\text{stable} = \dfrac{A}{2 + 0.0154\,A^{2/3}}$
$B$: binding cost | $Z$: proton number | $A$: mass number | $2$: asymmetry-cost balance ($N=Z$) | $0.0154\,A^{2/3}$: Coulomb-cost proton penalty
