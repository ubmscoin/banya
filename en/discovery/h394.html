Core Finding

H-394: δ duty cycle = Fermi-Dirac occupancy

$$P(\delta=1,\text{Swap})=\frac{1}{1+e^{n_{\text{Swap}}E_P/(k_B T)}}$$

(1) The δ duty cycle (the fraction with δ=1 that also reaches Swap) follows the Fermi-Dirac occupancy function form.

(2) Banya formula: P(δ=1, Swap) = 1/(1 + e^(n_Swap E_P/(k_B T))). When Compare is false, Swap is unreachable, so the statistical occupancy takes Fermi-Dirac form.

(3) By Axiom 4 (cost) and Axiom 5 (cost conservation), the probability of reaching Swap depends on energy (cost). n_Swap = cumulative Swap count, E_P = Planck energy.

(4) Structural consequence: the Fermi-Dirac distribution emerges naturally from the pass-through probability of the CAS pipeline. The "one state, one occupant" exclusion principle stems from the atomicity of CAS Swap (only one entity may write at a time).

(5) Numerics: at chemical potential μ = 0, P = 1/(1 + e^(E/kT)). For E = 0, P = 1/2 (half-occupied). For E ≫ kT, P ==> 0 (unoccupied). For E ≪ -kT, P ==> 1 (fully occupied).

(6) Consistency: since the Fermi-Dirac distribution is derived from CAS structure, the foundational distribution of quantum statistical mechanics becomes a physical consequence.

(7) Physical correspondence: the energy-level occupation of electrons follows this distribution. The filling of all states below the Fermi energy at absolute zero stems from the atomicity of CAS Swap (simultaneous writes are impossible).

(8) The Bose-Einstein distribution is P = 1/(e^(E/kT) - 1). The sign difference in the denominator stems from the presence or absence of CAS exclusivity. Bosons allow multiple Swaps to the same state; fermions do not.

(9) Verification: rigorously derive the Fermi-Dirac distribution from the atomicity of CAS Swap, and confirm that the Bose-Einstein distribution emerges from non-atomic Swap.
