H-538: Weak Gravity Conjecture = CAS Confinement Cost > Cumulative Cost Always

$$F_{\text{gauge}} \geq F_{\text{gravity}},\quad \text{CAS boundary cost} \geq \text{CAS cumulative cost}\;\forall\;\text{state}$$

Grade: B

The Weak Gravity Conjecture (gravity is always weaker than every other force) is the structural inequality stating that the CAS boundary-crossing cost (gauge force) is always greater than the CAS cumulative cost (gravity). The boundary cost is a first-order effect (Axiom 4: $+1$ per boundary) while the cumulative cost is a second-order effect (H-517), so this inequality is axiomatically guaranteed.

Axiom 4 (boundary cost $+1$ = first-order > cumulative = second-order), H-517 (gravity = cumulative), H-529 (G small)

Axiom 4 (cost $+1$ per boundary: the fundamental unit of a gauge force = first-order), Axiom 4 (cumulative: cost of cost = second-order ==> always smaller than first-order), Axiom 14 (FSM norm: gauge coupling $\sim O(1)$ vs gravity coupling $\sim m/m_P \ll 1$), Axiom 11 (in the interaction formula, gauge $C > G$)

$m_P = \sqrt{\hbar c/G}$: the gauge-gravity boundary. Proton: electromagnetic repulsion / gravitational attraction $\sim 10^{36}$. Electron: $e^2/(Gm_e^2) \sim 10^{42}$. The hierarchy problem $m_W/m_P \sim 10^{-17}$ is also an expression of this inequality.

$G m_p^2/(\hbar c) \sim 10^{-39}$. $\alpha_{\text{EM}} \sim 10^{-2}$. Ratio $\sim 10^{37}$. Holds for every known particle.

Holds for every known particle and force. No counterexample found.
