A gamma-ray burst is the event in which the CAS-chain cost accumulated during a wholesale FSM collapse is released at once through a narrow jet. Axiom 4 (cost is released irreversibly) and Axiom 5 (CAS chain) read this extreme release.

Step 2. Norm Substitution

The norm being substituted is the rest cost bound to the collapsed FSM norm (Axiom 3). The isotropic-equivalent energy $E_\text{iso}$ is that entire cost concentrated into the jet solid angle, and because the cost is released irreversibly (Axiom 4) this norm is chosen. The isotropic energy is therefore substituted by the product of the collapse mass $M$, efficiency $\eta$, and beaming solid-angle fraction $f_b$. Why this norm: the Gamma-Ray Burst Isotropic Energy itself originates from the Extreme CAS-Chain Collapse Cost, so only a norm that carries that very cost structure maps onto it without slack. Why the substitution is valid: the standard physics form on the left and the Banya form on the right denote the same quantity, the latter merely rewriting it in CAS cost terms.

$E_\text{iso} = \dfrac{\eta\,M c^2}{f_b}$
$f_b = 1 - \cos\theta_j,\quad \Gamma \gtrsim \theta_j^{-1}$
$E_\text{iso}$: isotropic-equivalent energy | $\eta$: cost-release efficiency | $M c^2$: collapsed-FSM rest cost | $f_b$: beaming solid-angle fraction | $\theta_j$: jet half-angle | $\Gamma$: Lorentz factor

Step 4. Domain Transformation

The transformation is into the cosmological high-energy-emission domain. What it reveals is the isotropic-equivalent energy $E_\text{iso}$ produced by beaming. Multiplying the collapse rest cost by the efficiency and dividing by the beaming fraction yields that value. Why this domain: the Gamma-Ray Burst Isotropic Energy itself is the observable measured in this domain, so the cost structure must be projected here to be compared directly with experiment.
