H-633: Mott Insulator = CAS Cost Interaction Overwhelms Bandon

$$U > W \;\text{==>}\; \text{band half-filling insulation} \;\text{<==>}\; \text{CAS cost interaction } > \text{ bandwidth}$$

Grade: C

A Mott insulator is a state in which the CAS cost interaction ($U$) exceeds the bandwidth ($W$); by bandon theory (H-619) it should be a conductor, yet in reality it is an insulator. The double-occupancy cost $U$ = cost of placing two FSMs on the same d-ring.

H-619 (bandon), Axiom 4 (interaction cost)

H-619 (band gap = 0 nominally), Axiom 4 (CAS cost interaction $U > W$), Axiom 14 (FSM fermion = Pauli exclusion ==> double-occupancy cost)

Hubbard model $H = -t\sum c^\dagger c + U\sum n_\uparrow n_\downarrow$ = CAS hopping + double occupancy. Mott-Hubbard gap = $U - W$. Insulator at half filling.

NiO: Mott insulator, $U \approx 8\;\text{eV}$, $W \approx 3\;\text{eV}$.

Consistent with experiments on Mott insulators (NiO, V₂O₃, etc.).

Mott insulator (Mott 1949), Hubbard model, strongly correlated electron systems

Verified by Mott-transition experiments (pressure / doping).

Material-specific quantitative derivation of the CAS cost $U$.

A Mott insulator is the phenomenon where the double-occupancy cost $U$ of placing two FSMs on the same d-ring (Axiom 14 Pauli exclusion) exceeds the bandwidth $W$ (H-619), so a half-filled state that should conduct by band theory closes into an insulator. This cost interaction (Axiom 4) is read as the insulating gap.

Step 2. Norm Substitution

(a) The norm being substituted is the CAS double-occupancy cost norm. (b) It is chosen because once the cost $U$ of putting two FSMs on the same d-ring exceeds the bandwidth $W$ that hopping opens, the cost cannot flow and is trapped. The hopping and double-occupancy terms of the Hubbard Hamiltonian are therefore substituted by $W$ and $U$, and the Mott-Hubbard gap is read as $U-W$. Why this norm: the NiO Mott-Hubbard gap $U-W \approx 5\;\text{eV}$ itself originates from the CAS double-occupancy cost, so only a norm that carries that very cost structure maps onto it without slack. Why the substitution is valid: the standard physics form on the left and the Banya form on the right denote the same quantity, the latter merely rewriting it in CAS cost terms.

$H = -t\sum c^\dagger c + U\sum n_\uparrow n_\downarrow$
$E_{gap} = U - W \quad (U > W \;\Rightarrow\; \text{half-filled insulation})$
$U$: CAS double-occupancy cost | $W$: bandwidth (H-619) | $t$: d-ring hopping | $n_\uparrow n_\downarrow$: same-ring double occupancy | $E_{gap}$: Mott-Hubbard gap
