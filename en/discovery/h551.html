5-Step Recursive Substitution: Deriving the Absence of a SUSY Partner Slot from the CAS Bit-Slot Count

Step 1. Banya Formula

$$\delta^2 = (\text{time} + \text{space})^2 + (\text{observer} + \text{superposition})^2$$

The cost one particle occupies is the slot held by the CAS 3 bits (R·C·S = 1·2·4, Axiom 4). FSM states (Axiom 9) are the finite list of these bit combinations, and SUSY demands an extra boson-fermion partner slot for each. Whether such extra bits exist in the Banya formula is read as a slot count.

Step 2. Norm Substitution

The norm being substituted is the count norm over CAS 3-bit combinations. A particle slot is a nonzero bit combination, and for a SUSY partner to exist this count would have to double, so the total-slot count norm decides whether a SUSY slot exists. Because the FSM norm (Axiom 11) supplies mass, no separate supersymmetric mass spectrum is needed either.

$N_{\text{SUSY}} = 2 N_{\text{SM}}$
$N_{\text{slot}} = 2^3 - 1 = 7$
$N_{\text{slot}}$: CAS nonzero bit-combination count | $2^3$: all R·C·S 3-bit states | $-1$: excludes the 0 combination (empty slot) | $N_{\text{SUSY}}$: slot count SUSY would require

Step 3. Constant Substitution

R, C, S = 1, 2, 4            Axiom 4 CAS 3 bits
2^3     = 8                 all bit combinations
0 combo = 1                 empty slot (no particle)
N_slot  = 8 - 1 = 7         nonzero combos = all particle slots

Step 4. Domain Transformation

The transformation is into the particle-classification domain. What it reveals is whether any spare bit slot remains to hold a SUSY partner. Since the 7 nonzero combinations of the 3 bits are already all the particle slots, the count shows no new bit exists for a partner slot.

$N_{\text{slot}} = 7, \quad N_{\text{partner}} = 2^3 - 1 - 7 = 0$

Step 5. Discovery

Derived: partner slot count $N_{\text{partner}} = 0$
Measured: no SUSY particle detected in LHC Run 1-3
Consistency: qualitative match (quantitative mass bound pending)
