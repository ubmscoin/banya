H-456: Born Rule |ψ|² = Self-Reference Normalization of Compare Probability

$$P(k) = |c_k|^2 = |\langle k|\psi\rangle|^2 \;\Longleftrightarrow\; P(\text{Compare true for }|k\rangle) = \frac{\|d_k\|^2}{\sum_i \|d_i\|^2}$$

Grade: A

The Born rule is a postulate of quantum mechanics whose origin remains unexplained. Banya framework: in the superposition $|\psi\rangle = \sum c_i |i\rangle$, the coefficient $c_i$ is the d-ring occupancy weight $d_i$ of the ECS index $|i\rangle$ (Axiom 13). The probability that Compare returns true on $|k\rangle$ is the $|k\rangle$ occupancy ratio in the d-ring, equal to $\|d_k\|^2 / \sum \|d_i\|^2$. Reason the square appears: CAS is a self-reference loop (Axiom 10), and Read(amplitude) × Compare(amplitude) = amplitude².

Axiom 10 (δ==>observer==>Compare==>DATA==>δ self-reference), Axiom 13 (superposition = ECS index)

Axiom 10 (self-reference loop) ==> Axiom 13 (ECS index weight $d_i$) ==> Axiom 2 (Read × Compare = 2nd order) ==> Axiom 7 (Compare-true probability = normalized ratio)

The Born rule is not a postulate but a consequence of CAS self-reference structure. Read reads amplitude $d_k$ and Compare compares with the same amplitude, so the probability is proportional to $|d_k|^2$. Normalization $\sum P(k) = 1$ is conservation of total d-ring occupancy. CAS translation of Gleason's theorem: on ECS of dimension $\geq 3$, the unique additive measure is $|c_k|^2$.

The statistical distribution of every quantum measurement follows $|c_k|^2$. Double slit: $P(x) = |\psi_1(x) + \psi_2(x)|^2$. Stern-Gerlach: $P(\pm) = |c_\pm|^2$.
