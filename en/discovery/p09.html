P-09: Casimir Discrete Staircase (not continuous $1/d^4$)

$$F_{\text{Casimir}}(d)\ \text{= discrete staircase},\qquad \Delta d = 1\ \text{cell shift},\quad \text{not continuous } 1/d^4$$

Prediction: sub-nm Casimir force is a discrete staircase, not continuous 1/d⁴. Verify with AFM (2028~)

In the Banya structure, when an empty entity (a never-written cell) enters the contraction region of nearby grasps, a distortion arises where it is misread as written, due to fixed datatype reads (Axiom 11 proposition: empty-entity distortion).

This distortion is the Banya identity of the Casimir effect -- the "vacuum" between plates is actually the contraction overlap of empty entities.

But since DATA is discrete (Axiom 3), this distortion changes only in integer-cell units. There is no half-cell distortion.

Hence the Casimir force at sub-nanometer scales must show a staircase (step) structure, not a smooth continuous $1/d^4$.

Each step corresponds to a 1-cell shift of the d-ring (Axiom 15 proposition: ring buffer). Each time distance d drops by one cell, the force jumps discontinuously.

Step height is set by the inter-entity strength $C(1-d/N)/(4\pi d^2)$ (Axiom 13 proposition) -- closed by axiom integers with no external free parameter.

Standard QED Casimir gives $1/d^4$ via continuous integration, but that is the macroscopic approximation when discrete cells are numerous.

Going down to sub-nm (few cells) exposes discreteness and the staircase appears -- the decisive difference between the continuous and discrete assumptions.

This discrete staircase is a unique signature of the Banya framework; it cannot arise in a continuous-spacetime theory.

Verification: AFM-based sub-nanometer Casimir precision measurement (2028~). Refutation: observing perfect continuous $1/d^4$ at all scales refutes the discrete-spacetime hypothesis.
