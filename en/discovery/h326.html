Core Finding

H-326: Special-relativity time dilation = domain time consumption-rate difference

$$v/c=\text{shift rate on d-ring};\;\gamma=\Delta t_{\text{system}}/\Delta t_{\text{domain}}$$

Special-relativity time dilation is derived as a difference in domain-time consumption rate. The faster the shift rate on the d-ring, the less domain time is consumed per unit system time.

Banya formula: $v/c = $ shift rate on the d-ring. $\gamma = \Delta t_{\text{system}}/\Delta t_{\text{domain}}$. The cost spent on shifting reduces the budget available for rendering, so domain time slows.

Axiomatic basis: Axiom 4 (cost = +1 when crossing +) defines the shift cost. Axiom 15 (δ = system time) provides the reference. Axiom 8 (screen = domain time) defines the observed time.

Structural consequence: the total cost budget per system tick is fixed. Spending more cost on shifts (motion) leaves less cost for renders (time elapsing). This is the structural reason behind "moving makes time slow down."

Numerics: at $v/c = 0.99$, $\gamma \approx 7.1$. Muon lifetime demonstration: 2.2 $\mu$s at rest ==> atmospheric muons $\sim 15\;\mu$s. GPS satellites $v/c \sim 10^{-5}$, $\gamma - 1 \sim 10^{-10}$.

Consistency: a concrete consequence of H-323 (Lorentz invariance = mapping preservation). Coupled with H-324 (gravitational time dilation), it yields the total GPS correction ($\sim 38\;\mu$s/day). Also resolves the twin paradox.

Physical correspondence: special-relativity time dilation. The phenomenon that moving clocks run slow. Predicted by Einstein's 1905 special relativity. Confirmed by muon lifetimes and particle accelerators.

In conventional special relativity, time dilation arises from the geometry of Minkowski spacetime. In Banya it arises from an economic structure: the allocation of the cost budget (shift vs. render). Twin paradox: acceleration (direction change = Swap) incurs cost and creates the asymmetry.

Verification: precisely confirmed by muon lifetime demonstrations, the Hafele-Keating experiment (1971), GPS corrections, etc. Verification of the cost-budget-allocation interpretation depends on whether the system/domain time distinction can be distinguished.
