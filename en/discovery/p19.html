P-19. Quantum-computer Error Floor = $\alpha$

Core Finding

P-19: Quantum-computer Error Floor = $\alpha$

$$\text{single-gate error-rate floor}\ \geq\ \alpha=\frac{1}{137}\approx0.73\%\qquad(\alpha=\text{CAS Compare selection probability, }T(16){+}1)$$

Prediction: the physical floor of single-gate quantum error rate is $\alpha=1/137\approx0.73\%$. Test: QEC experiments (2026~).

In the Banya formula $\delta^2=(\text{time}+\text{space})^2+(\text{observer}+\text{superposition})^2$, $\alpha=1/137$ is the probability that CAS Compare selects 1 of 137 candidates — the candidate count being the triangular number $T(16)+1=137$ (Axiom 2 proposition, D-01 derivation demo 1).

This selection probability matches the volume ratio $D_5$ of the 5-sphere to within 0.00006% (D-01). So $\alpha$ is not a fitted value but the geometric cost of the CAS selection structure.

A quantum gate operation is essentially a physical implementation of CAS — it reads, compares, and swaps a state. Every time a gate fires, the Compare selection cost is necessarily incurred.

The cost of Compare exceeding + to pick one candidate cannot be zero (Axiom 4: a cost beyond + exists). This minimum cost is the lower bound of the error probability.

Hence the single-gate error rate cannot physically drop below this selection probability $\alpha\approx0.73\%$ ==> a discrete cost floor unbreakable no matter how technology advances.

The key is that this is not a technical limit like noise or control precision, but a limit of the CAS cost structure itself. In a continuous theory the error rate should in principle reach 0, but in discrete CAS that is impossible.

Concretely, while error correction (QEC) can lower the logical-qubit error rate, the single-operation error rate at the physical-gate level must stall at the $\alpha$ scale.

Today's best single-gate quantum error rates lie in roughly the 0.1–1% range. Observing whether improvement systematically stalls near 0.73% tests this prediction.

Test: in QEC and gate-benchmarking experiments (2026~), measure whether the lower-bound distribution of physical-gate error rates clusters at the $\alpha$ scale.

Refutation: if single physical-gate error rates are achieved persistently and reproducibly below $0.1\%$, the discrete cost floor is broken and this prediction is discarded.
