P-90. Max Simultaneous Entangled Pairs $=C(7,2)=21$

Core Finding

P-90: Max Simultaneous Entangled Pairs $=C(7,2)=21$

$$\text{max simultaneous entangled pairs}=\binom{7}{2}=21\quad(\text{beyond it, natural decoherence})$$

Prediction: maximum simultaneous entangled pairs per entity = $\binom{7}{2}=21$; beyond it, natural decoherence. Test: many-body entanglement (2029~).

In the Banya formula $\delta^2=(\text{time}+\text{space})^2+(\text{observer}+\text{superposition})^2$, one entity is described by a 7-bit index (full-description DOF, Axiom 9), and entanglement is a pair linking two index slots.

The number of independent entangled pairs one entity can simultaneously maintain is the combination of choosing two slots from 7 bits, $\binom{7}{2}=21$.

So the maximum simultaneous entangled-partner pairs of a single entity is 21 ==> beyond 21, index slots run out and natural decoherence occurs.

This is the same combinatorial integer as the meson-state ceiling 21 (P-57) — both arise from $C(7,2)$, choosing two slots from 7 bits.

Maintaining more than 21 entanglements would require indices outside the 7 bits, but the full-description DOF closes at 7, so it is impossible.

So this prediction is not "entanglement has a limit" but the combinatorial quantitative claim "at most 21 simultaneous pairs, decoherence beyond it."

This is in the same information-capacity family as the decoherence threshold 16 (P-56) and the entropy bound $8\ln2$ (P-44).

Observational strategy: measure the maximum number of entangled partners one qubit simultaneously maintains in many-body entanglement experiments.

Test: if single-entity simultaneous entanglement does not exceed 21 pairs in many-body experiments (2029~), this prediction is supported.

Refutation: if simultaneous entanglement beyond 21 pairs is stably maintained, the $C(7,2)$ ceiling is refuted and this prediction is discarded.
