Core Finding

H-362: Cost classification of the 16 nibble-crossing items

$$16=4(\text{cost 0})+4(\text{branch point})+4(\text{observation cost})+4(\text{render cost})$$

(1) The 16 nibble-crossing items are the 4×4=16 items formed by crossing the quantum/classical distinction of the domain nibble (4-bit) with the R, C, S, δ distinction of the CAS nibble.

(2) Banya formula: 16 = 4(cost 0) + 4(branch point) + 4(observation cost) + 4(render cost). Quantum × (R, C) = cost 0, quantum × (S, δ) = branch, classical × (R, C) = observation, classical × (S, δ) = render.

(3) By Axiom 1 (4 axes), the domain nibble splits into the quantum bracket (observer, superposition) and the classical bracket (time, space). The CAS nibble has the 4 stages R, C, S, δ.

(4) Structural consequence: in the quantum domain, Read and Compare do not disturb the state, so their cost is 0 (superposition preserved). In the quantum domain, Swap and δ fix the state and are therefore branch points (collapse). In the classical domain, Read and Compare incur observation cost, while Swap and δ incur rendering cost.

(5) Numerical values: 4 items per category × 4 categories = 16 total. cost 0 (4) + branch (4) + observation (4) + render (4) = 16. Cost-incurring items = 12, cost-0 items = 4.

(6) Consistency: in quantum mechanics, the distinction between superposition preservation before observation (cost 0) and collapse after observation (branch) matches the nibble-crossing classification.

(7) Physical correspondence: cost-0 items correspond to quantum non-demolition measurements (QND), branch points to wavefunction collapse, observation cost to classical measurements, and render cost to thermodynamically irreversible processes.

(8) The 16-item classification provides a structural definition of the quantum-classical boundary. The quantum region is cost 0 + branch (8 items), and the classical region is observation + render (8 items). The boundary is the ON/OFF state of the upper 2 bits (classical bracket) of the domain nibble.

(9) Verification: assign a concrete physical process (e.g., photon detection, spin measurement, heat emission) to each of the 16 crossing items and confirm that the cost classification matches experiment.
