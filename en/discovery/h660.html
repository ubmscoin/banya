Core Finding

H-660: Nuclear Quadrupole Moment = Non-Spherical Distribution of Domain Bits

$$Q = \frac{1}{e}\int \rho(r)(3z^2 - r^2)\,d^3r \;\leftrightarrow\; \text{non-spherical distribution of domain bits}$$

Grade: C

The nuclear quadrupole moment represents the degree of non-spherical deformation of the nuclear charge distribution. In Banya, when domain bits (Axiom 1) are distributed anisotropically across the 3 axes, the quadrupole moment is $Q \neq 0$, reflecting the non-spherical cost distribution of the FSM ensemble.

Axiom 1 (4-axis domain), Axiom 14 (FSM distribution), H-653 (collective motion)

Axiom 1 (3 spatial domain axes ==> anisotropic distribution possible), Axiom 3 (FSM norm distribution = charge density $\rho(r)$), Axiom 4 (cost of non-spherical deformation = deformation energy). Deformation parameter $\beta_2$: prolate ($Q > 0$), oblate ($Q < 0$).

Spherical nuclei (magic numbers): $Q \approx 0$. Deformed nuclei (rare earths, actinides): $|Q| \sim \text{several}\;\text{barn}$. Closed shell ± a few nucleons: single-particle $Q$ ≪ collective $Q$. Quadrupole deformation = origin of rotational bands.

Lu-176: $Q = 8.0\;\text{b}$. Adjacent to Pb-208: $Q \approx 0$. $^{166}\text{Er}$: $\beta_2 \approx 0.34$.

Muonic X-rays and electron scattering measure $Q$ with $< 5\%$ consistency.
