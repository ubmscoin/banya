A main-sequence star is the state in which the FSM fusion cost gain (H-647) and the RLU emission cost loss (Axiom 6) are exactly balanced. The cost equilibrium of generation equal to loss (Axiom 4) enforces hydrostatic and thermal equilibrium at once, and that equilibrium sets the lifetime the star spends on the band.

Step 2. Norm Substitution

The norm being substituted is the FSM-fusion = RLU-emission cost-equilibrium norm. While equilibrium holds, the fuel norm is consumed in proportion to the luminosity, and that consumption rate sets the lifetime, so this norm is chosen. The fuel norm scales with mass $M$ and the consumption rate with luminosity $L \propto M^{3.5}$, so the lifetime falls out as their ratio.

$\epsilon_\text{nuc}(r) = \epsilon_\text{rad}(r) + \epsilon_\text{conv}(r)$
$t_\text{MS} \propto \dfrac{M}{L} \propto M^{-2.5}$
$\epsilon_\text{nuc}$: fusion generation rate | $\epsilon_\text{rad}, \epsilon_\text{conv}$: radiative/convective loss | $t_\text{MS}$: main-sequence lifetime | $M$: fuel norm = mass | $L \propto M^{3.5}$: mass-luminosity

Step 4. Domain Transformation

The transformation is into the macroscopic-stellar time domain (residence time on the equilibrium band). What it reveals is the duration over which cost equilibrium holds — the main-sequence lifetime $t_\text{MS}$. Dividing the fuel norm $M$ by the luminosity $L \propto M^{3.5}$ yields $t_\text{MS} \propto M^{-2.5}$, giving about 10 Gyr for the Sun, about 3 Myr for O type, and over 100 Gyr for M type.
