5-Step Recursive Substitution: Deriving the Fission Threshold $Z^2/A>47$ from Coulomb Cost Exceedance

Step 1. Banya Formula

Nuclear fission is the cost threshold where a heavy FSM can no longer confine its Coulomb cost by surface binding and splits in two. The surface cost of holding the FSM norm in a closed interval (Axiom 3) and the CAS electromagnetic cost (Axiom 5) are read through the cost comparison of Axiom 4.

Step 2. Norm Substitution

(a) The norm being substituted is the fissility norm $x=E_C/(2E_S)$, measuring the ratio of surface-binding cost to Coulomb cost. (b) Fission is the event of a closed FSM interval (Axiom 3) breaking, and whether it breaks is set by whether the Coulomb cost exceeds the surface cost (Axiom 4 cost comparison) — hence this norm. In the liquid-drop cost the Coulomb term $E_C\propto Z^2/A^{1/3}$ over the surface term $E_S\propto A^{2/3}$ is proportional to $Z^2/A$, so the spontaneous-fission threshold closes as the integer comparison $Z^2/A>47$.

$x = \dfrac{E_C}{2E_S}$
$x \ge 1 \;\text{when}\; \dfrac{Z^2}{A} > 47$
$x$: fissility parameter | $E_C$: Coulomb cost (Axiom 5) | $E_S$: surface-binding cost (Axiom 3) | $Z^2/A$: fissility index | $47$: spontaneous-fission threshold integer

Step 3. Constant Substitution

$Z^{2}/A_{crit}$ = 47 spontaneous fission threshold
$E_{f}(U-235)$ = 6 $\text{MeV}$ fission barrier
$E_{event}$ = 200 $\text{MeV}$ released per U-235 fission

Step 4. Domain Transformation

(a) The transformation is into the nucleon-binding cost domain (FSM surface versus Coulomb). (b) What it reveals is the threshold at which a nucleus splits by spontaneous fission. Solving the fissility condition $x=1$ for $Z^2/A$ yields the threshold integer.

$\dfrac{Z^2}{A}\bigg|_\text{crit} = 47, \qquad E_f(\text{U-235}) = 6\;\text{MeV}$

Derived: $Z^2/A > 47$ (spontaneous fission), barrier $6\;\text{MeV}$
