5-Step Recursive Substitution: Deriving the Gravitational-Wave Strain from the Cost Transfer of FSM Asymmetric Acceleration

Step 1. Banya Formula

$$\delta^2 = (\text{time} + \text{space})^2 + (\text{observer} + \text{superposition})^2$$

A gravitational wave is the phenomenon in which the cost fluctuation arising when two FSM norms accelerate asymmetrically propagates as space curvature. Axiom 11 (cost transfer = gravity) and Axiom 3 (FSM norm = mass) read this fluctuation.

Step 2. Norm Substitution

The norm being substituted is the quadrupole norm $I_{ij}$ of the mass distribution. The monopole and dipole emit nothing by cost conservation (Axiom 4), and the second time-derivative of the quadrupole is the first to transfer cost into space (Axiom 11), so this norm is chosen. The strain is therefore substituted by the second derivative of the quadrupole.

$h = \dfrac{2G}{c^4 r}\,\ddot I_{ij}$
$\ddot I_{ij} \sim 2\mu(\pi f_\text{GW})^2 d^2$
$h$: strain | $G$: cost-transfer constant | $c$: signal-propagation limit | $r$: source distance | $I_{ij}$: mass quadrupole | $\mu$: reduced norm | $f_\text{GW}$: wave frequency | $d$: binary separation

Step 3. Constant Substitution

G = $6.674\times10^{-8}$ $\text{cm}^{3}$ $g^{-1}$ $s^{-2}$
c = $2.998\times10^{10}$ $\text{cm}/s$
$\mu$ = 16 $M_{sun}$ = $3.18\times10^{34}$ g GW150914 reduced norm
d = 350 $\text{km}$ = $3.5\times10^{7}$ $\text{cm}$ separation just before merger
$f_{GW}$ = 150 $\text{Hz}$ merger band
r = 410 Mpc = $1.265\times10^{27}$ $\text{cm}$ GW150914 distance

Step 4. Domain Transformation

The transformation is into the cosmological cost-transfer domain. What it reveals is the strain $h$ arriving at the detector. Inserting the second derivative of the quadrupole into the cost-transfer factor $2G/c^4 r$ yields that value.

$\ddot I_{ij} \sim 2 \times 3.18\times10^{34} \times (\pi\times150)^2 \times (3.5\times10^{7})^2 \approx 1.7\times10^{49}\;\text{g}\cdot\text{cm}^2/\text{s}^2$
$h = \dfrac{2 \times 6.674\times10^{-8}}{(2.998\times10^{10})^4 \times 1.265\times10^{27}} \times 1.7\times10^{49} \approx 1.4\times10^{-21}$
