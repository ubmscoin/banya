H-400: Casimir Effect: Boundary Constraints Among 16 Domain Patterns

$$\text{mode density}\propto 16-(\text{boundary constraint});\;2\text{ plates}=2\text{ bits fixed}$$

(1) The Casimir effect is the attractive force between two conducting plates arising from a vacuum energy difference. In the Banya Framework it is the phenomenon where some patterns among the 16 domain patterns are forbidden by boundary constraints.

(2) Banya formula: mode density ∝ 16 - (boundary constraint); 2 plates = 2 bits fixed. The boundary fixes domain bits, so the number of allowed patterns decreases.

(3) By Axiom 1 (4 axes) 16 patterns exist. By Axiom 15 (δ) the boundary condition fixes a domain bit (forcing ON or OFF).

(4) Structural consequence: when the boundary fixes one space axis (bit 3), only the remaining three axes' 2³ = 8 patterns are allowed. The energy-density difference between outside the boundary (16 patterns) and inside (8 patterns) is the Casimir force.

(5) Numerics: Casimir pressure F/A = -π²ℏc/(240 d⁴). d = plate separation. The 16 ==> 8 pattern reduction (factor 2) contributes to the π²/240 coefficient.

(6) Consistency: the Casimir effect was measured to 1% precision by Lamoreaux in 1997. The domain-pattern constraint interpretation must reproduce this precision.

(7) Physical correspondence: conducting plates = boundary condition = space domain fixed. Between the plates long-wavelength modes (those with large self-induction between balls) are forbidden. In the Banya Framework this means bit 3 (space) is fixed and self-induction is reduced.

(8) Under different boundary conditions (e.g., electrical vs. geometric) different domain bits are fixed. The Casimir force in each case depends on the number of fixed bits.
