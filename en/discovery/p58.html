P-58: $C(7,3)=35$ = Baryon State Ceiling

$$\binom{7}{3}=35=\text{combinatorial ceiling of baryon states}\quad(\text{3-quark combinations of the 7-bit index})$$

Prediction: the combinatorial ceiling of independent baryon states is $\binom{7}{3}=35$. Test: LHCb/BESIII spectroscopy (2028~).

In the Banya formula $\delta^2=(\text{time}+\text{space})^2+(\text{observer}+\text{superposition})^2$, a baryon is a CAS indexing state of three quarks, and the index is 7 bits (full-description DOF, Axiom 9).

A baryon chooses three slots (three quarks) from the 7-bit index ==> the number of independent combinations is $\binom{7}{3}=35$.

So this prediction states that the combinatorial ceiling of independent baryon states (basis states within the same quantum-number family) is 35.

35 is the second term in the decomposition 57 = 21+35+1 ($C(7,3)$), the CAS combinatorial integer paired with the meson 21 (P-57).

The three-quark combination is consistent with CAS holding the 3-bit 111 as one block via strong confinement (Axiom 14 FSM).

So independent baryon basis states beyond 35 have no place in the 7-bit index space and are structurally impossible.

So this prediction is not "there are many baryons" but the combinatorial quantitative claim "independent basis states are bounded at exactly 35."

Exotic baryons such as pentaquarks are also explained within this combination and do not require new basis DOF beyond 35.
