P-43: Quantum-gate Error Floor $\sim0.73\%$

$$\text{single-qubit gate error floor}\approx\frac{1}{137}\approx0.73\%,\qquad \frac{\alpha}{2\pi}=0.00116\ (\text{1-loop correction})$$

Prediction: single-qubit gate error floor $\approx1/137\approx0.73\%$. Test: IBM/Google quantum computers (2027~).

In the Banya formula $\delta^2=(\text{time}+\text{space})^2+(\text{observer}+\text{superposition})^2$, $\alpha=1/137$ is the CAS Compare selection cost (D-01), and a quantum gate is its physical implementation.

This card is the IBM/Google hardware-specific version of P-19 (gate error floor = α).

Each gate operation incurs the Compare selection cost, which cannot be 0 (Axiom 4: a cost beyond + exists).

Hence the single-qubit gate error rate cannot physically drop below $\alpha=1/137\approx0.73\%$ ==> a discrete cost floor.

Meanwhile the 1-loop correction term $\alpha/(2\pi)=0.00116=0.116\%$ marks a lower scale that more refined gates can approach.

So 0.73% is the floor for a plain gate and 0.116% the corrected limiting scale, both arising from $\alpha$.

The key is that this floor is a limit of the CAS cost structure, not of noise or control technology — a continuous theory should allow reaching 0.

Today's best single-qubit gate error rates are roughly 0.1–0.5%, showing signs of stalling between the 0.116% and 0.73% range.
