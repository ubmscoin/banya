Fractional-derivative Application Extension: CPE / Cole-Cole Generalization

Constant-Phase Element (CPE) $Z \propto (i\omega)^{-\alpha}$, $0<\alpha<1$

$$Z_{\text{CPE}} \propto (i\omega)^{-\alpha},\qquad \alpha=\tfrac{1}{2} \Rightarrow \text{Warburg}$$

Real interfaces and dielectrics show impedance $Z\propto(i\omega)^{-\alpha}$ (constant-phase element, CPE) and a Cole-Cole relaxation exponent $\alpha\in(0,1)$. $\alpha=1$ = ideal capacitor, $\alpha=0$ = ideal resistor, fractional in between. The Warburg case ($\alpha=1/2$) of this card is the ideal limit; general $\alpha$ is the dispersion of the category-crossing order away from $1/2$ caused by boundary disorder and roughness.

Thus the deviation of the CPE exponent $\alpha$ from $1/2$ is a measure of interfacial non-ideality (roughness, heterogeneity). Only the ideal limit $1/2$ is this card's discovery; general $\alpha$ is fit-dependent and remains at the structural-description (hypothesis) level.
