H-11: CAS is the operator outside time

CAS is the operator on the quantum bracket (observer + superposition) side and runs outside the time domain. R==>C==>S is not a temporal order but a logical dependency. Time's arrow appears only when CAS writes (juigi) to time.

In δ² = (t+s)² + (o+sp)², CAS is the operator on the (o+sp) bracket. The (t+s) bracket (DATA) is CAS's output, not its input. Compare is impossible without Read (no data to compare). Swap is impossible without Compare (no verdict to drive the exchange). This dependency is logical, not temporal.

Axiom 1 (Banya formula: DATA and OPERATOR separated) ==> Axiom 2 (CAS is OPERATOR) ==> Axiom 12 (classical bracket = ECS). CAS sits on the OPERATOR side, time sits on the DATA (ECS) side. The structure is: work in the workbench (OPERATOR space) and commit the result to DATA.

Because CAS lies outside time, CAS itself has no "before/after." Time's arrow appears when CAS executes a write (juigi) into DATA. The irreversibility of writing (Axiom 14, FSM) is the source of time's irreversibility. The direction in which the d-ring closes at the ring seam is the direction of time.

The problem of time vanishing in the WDW (Wheeler-DeWitt) equation of quantum gravity: this is exactly the same structure as CAS being outside time. The WDW equation is a description at the CAS (OPERATOR) level, and time appears only after being written into DATA.

Because this is a structural correspondence, the match is qualitative rather than numerical. The fact that time in quantum mechanics is a parameter (not an operator) is naturally explained by CAS being outside time.

In quantum mechanics, time is not an observable. The $t$ in the Schrödinger equation is a parameter. In the Banya Framework this is natural since CAS (OPERATOR) sits outside time (DATA).

The problem of time is a central difficulty of quantum gravity. In general relativity time is dynamical; in quantum mechanics it is a background. The Banya Framework resolves this gap as DATA (dynamical) vs OPERATOR (background-independent).

If CAS lies outside time, "simultaneity" in quantum entanglement is defined at the CAS level, so Bell-inequality violation is natural. Refutation condition: an experiment showing that quantum nonlocality requires a temporal order would break CAS's time-independence.

The mechanism by which CAS, while outside time, writes into time needs refining. The timing by which the utterance bit (Axiom 15, δ = bit7) triggers the CAS cycle must be tied explicitly to time in DATA.
