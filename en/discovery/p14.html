P-14: Singularities Do Not Exist (discrete maximum)

$$\ell_{\min}=1\ \Rightarrow\ 1/\ell^2\leq1\ (\text{no divergence});\quad \text{event horizon}=\text{accumulated-cost boundary, not singularity}$$

Prediction: no physical singularity — event horizon = cost boundary. Verify with GW ringdown spectrum

DATA is discrete (Axiom 3). Since d-ring cells are integers, the minimum distance $d$ is 1 and $d=0$ is impossible (Axiom 13 proposition).

At $d=1$ the per-pair cost is $1/d^2=1/1^2=1$, finite. Divergence (infinity) is structurally blocked in a discrete system.

A black hole is $N$ grasps packed at $d=1$. The total cost accumulates over $N(N-1)/2$ pairs as $N^2$, yet remains finite.

The event horizon is merely the boundary where accumulated cost exceeds escape cost — a cost boundary, not a zero-volume singularity (D-49).

So the "central singularity" is an artifact of the continuous-spacetime assumption; the discrete structure has no central point to diverge at.

This shares one root with P-08 and P-12 — discreteness ($\ell\geq1$) blocks divergence, and the interior is filled by a lattice.

A discrete internal structure produces fine deviations (mode splitting, frequency shift) from the smooth ringdown of a GR point singularity.

The deviation magnitude scales with d-ring resolution ($\Delta f/f\sim1/128$, linked to P-80).
