H-811: Dispersion Relations = Causality Constraint on CAS Cost

$$\text{Re}\,\mathcal{A}(s) = \frac{1}{\pi}\text{P}\!\int \frac{\text{Im}\,\mathcal{A}(s')}{s'-s}ds' \;\leftrightarrow\; \text{real and imaginary parts of CAS cost are linked by causality}$$

Grade: B

Dispersion relations link the real and imaginary parts of the scattering amplitude through causality. In Banya, CAS cost must be causal (cause ==> effect). The real part of CAS cost (elastic scattering) and the imaginary part (inelastic scattering) are bound by the Kramers-Kronig relation.

Axiom 2 (CAS cost), Axiom 8 (causality = δ ordering)

Axiom 2 (CAS = operation, cost incurred), Axiom 8 (8-bit ring buffer = time order = causality). CAS cost must respect the time order (the forward direction of the ring buffer), and is therefore analytic. Analyticity ==> dispersion relations.

Dispersion relations grant 'predictive power' to scattering amplitudes: knowing the imaginary part (total cross section) lets one compute the real part (forward scattering). This is a structural consequence of CAS cost satisfying causality. Directly connects to the optical theorem (H-815).

Froissart bound: $\sigma_{\text{tot}}(s) < c \cdot \ln^2 s$.
