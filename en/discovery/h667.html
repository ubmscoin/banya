5-Step Recursive Substitution: Deriving the Solar Luminosity from the FSM Fusion Cost Gain

Step 1. Banya Formula

$$\delta^2 = (\text{time} + \text{space})^2 + (\text{observer} + \text{superposition})^2$$

A star's light is the macroscopic emission of the cost gain recovered when the FSM fuses light nuclei into heavier ones (H-647). The norm released by fusion is radiated from the surface through RLU decay (Axiom 6), and generation balancing loss in cost equilibrium (Axiom 4) sets the luminosity.

Step 2. Norm Substitution

The norm being substituted is the FSM fusion-cost-gain norm. Luminosity is the total cost gain recovered per unit time, and that recovery reaches the surface as RLU decay, so this norm is chosen. At cost equilibrium the generation rate $\epsilon$ equals the loss, so the hydrogen-burning efficiency $\eta$ and the burned mass set the luminosity.

$L = \dfrac{\eta\, \Delta m\, c^2}{t}$
$\epsilon_{pp} \propto \rho\, T^4$
$L$: luminosity | $\eta$: hydrogen-burning efficiency = cost-gain ratio | $\Delta m$: burned mass | $\epsilon_{pp}$: pp generation rate | $\rho, T$: central density, temperature

Step 3. Constant Substitution

$\eta$ = 0.007 hydrogen-burning efficiency (0.7%)
c = $2.998\times10^{8}$ m/s
$T_{c}$ = $1.57\times10^{7}$ K solar central temperature
$L_{sun}$ = $3.828\times10^{26}$ W solar luminosity (observed)

Step 4. Domain Transformation

The transformation is into the macroscopic-radiation domain (surface RLU emission). What it reveals is the total cost gain emitted per unit time — the solar luminosity $L_\odot$. The norm released at the center via $\epsilon \propto \rho T^4$ is carried to the surface by RLU decay and emitted as $3.828\times10^{26}$ W.

$L_\odot = 3.828 \times 10^{26}\;\text{W}$
