The product of the cosmological constant and the Planck length squared, $\Lambda l_P^2$, is a dimensionless number expressing the absolute size of $\Lambda$ without a scale.

This prediction holds that this dimensionless number is fixed to $\Lambda l_P^2=\alpha^{57}$.

The exponent 57 is the CAS total cost budget ($21+35+1$, Axiom 9; e-folding $N_e=57$, P-62), and the base $\alpha$ is the CAS selection cost.

So the universe passes through 57 cost steps during inflation, each suppressing $\Lambda$ by a factor of $\alpha$, giving $\alpha^{57}$ ==> the cost budget enters the exponent directly.

$\alpha^{57}$ ≈ $137^{-57}\sim10^{-122}$, matching in order of magnitude the extremely small observed cosmological constant (the $10^{-122}$ of the cosmological-constant problem).

So this prediction is not "the cosmological constant is small" but the quantitative claim "exactly $\alpha^{57}$, with 57 explaining the $10^{-122}$ of the cosmological-constant problem."

This solves the cosmological-constant problem (why $10^{-122}$) not by fine-tuning but by the CAS cost budget 57.
