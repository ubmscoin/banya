Core Finding

P-126: RLU-decay Derivation of the Casimir-effect Temperature Dependence

$$F_\text{Casimir}(T)=F_0\left[1+\frac{2\pi k_B T d}{\hbar c}\cdot\frac{9}{13}\right]^{-1}\quad(\text{thermal-correction factor }9/13\approx0.692)$$

Prediction: a $9/13\approx0.692$ factor in the Casimir thermal correction (~30% difference from Lifshitz). Test: micro-nano Casimir force (2028~).

In the Banya formula $\delta^2=(\text{time}+\text{space})^2+(\text{observer}+\text{superposition})^2$, the Casimir effect (attraction between two plates due to vacuum fluctuations) is described by the polling structure of the RLU cache (Axiom 6, Axiom 8 δ polling).

This prediction holds that a $9/13\approx0.692$ factor attaches to the temperature-dependent correction of the Casimir force — $F_\text{Casimir}(T)=F_0[1+(2\pi k_B Td/\hbar c)(9/13)]^{-1}$.

9 is the RLU remainder (Axiom 6: the remainder after subtracting the maintenance cost 4 from the total 13), and 13 is the total cost of one interaction (Axiom 4).

So the thermal correction is attenuated by the ratio $9/13$ of the RLU remainder 9 to the total cost 13 ==> DATA discreteness (Axiom 3) shaves the thermal term.

Due to this $9/13$ factor, the Casimir thermal correction shows about a 30% difference from the standard Lifshitz theory.

So this prediction is not "the Casimir force has temperature dependence" but the quantitative claim "a $9/13$ factor exactly in the thermal correction, ~30% vs Lifshitz."

$9/13$ is in the same total-cost-13 distribution family as Schwinger $4/13$ (P-123) and vacuum energy $4/13$ (P-130) (4+9=13).

Standard Lifshitz theory predicts the thermal correction without this $9/13$ attenuation, so the difference is the discriminator.

Test: if micro-nano-scale Casimir-force precision measurements (T=100-600K, Yale, IUPUI, 2028~) show the thermal correction with $9/13$ attenuation, this prediction is supported.

Refutation: if the thermal correction agrees with Lifshitz to within 1% with no $9/13$ attenuation, this prediction is discarded.

5-Step Recursive Substitution: Deriving the Casimir thermal correction 9/13 from the RLU remainder 9 / total cost 13

Step 1. Banya Formula

$$\delta^2 = (\text{time} + \text{space})^2 + (\text{observer} + \text{superposition})^2$$

The Casimir thermal correction is the cost the RLU cache pays while polling the vacuum fluctuations between two plates. From the total cost $13$ of one interaction (Axiom 4), subtracting the maintenance cost $4$ leaves the RLU remainder $9$ (Axiom 6), which becomes the factor attached to the thermal term.

Step 2. Norm Substitution

The norm being substituted is the RLU-remainder norm — the ratio of the remainder $9$ after excluding the maintenance cost $4$ from the total cost $13$ (Axiom 6). This norm is chosen because the thermal correction is shaved by RLU polling (Axiom 8 δ polling), and that shaving closes through DATA discreteness (Axiom 3) into the ratio of the remainder $9$ to the total $13$. The Lifshitz thermal term is therefore multiplied by the $9/13$ factor.

$F_\text{Casimir}(T)=F_0\left[1+\dfrac{2\pi k_B T d}{\hbar c}\right]^{-1}$
$F_\text{Casimir}(T)=F_0\left[1+\dfrac{2\pi k_B T d}{\hbar c}\cdot\dfrac{9}{13}\right]^{-1}$
$F_\text{Casimir}(T)$: temperature-dependent Casimir force | $F_0$: zero-temperature Casimir force | $9$: RLU remainder (Axiom 6) | $13$: total cost of one interaction (Axiom 4) | $9/13\approx0.692$: thermal-correction attenuation factor

Step 3. Constant Substitution

13   = total cost of one interaction   Axiom 4

$F_\text{Casimir}(T)=F_0\left[1+\dfrac{2\pi k_B T d}{\hbar c}\cdot 0.692\right]^{-1}$
