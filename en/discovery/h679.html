Step 1. Banya Formula

The Eddington luminosity is the critical luminosity at which the outward radiation cost and the inward gravity cost balance exactly. Axiom 6 (RLU decay = radiation) and Axiom 11 (cost transfer = gravity) read this balance.

Step 2. Norm Substitution

The norm being substituted is the balance norm that sets the radiation cost equal to the gravity cost per unit norm. The radiation pressure acts on electrons through the Thomson-scattering cost $\sigma_T$ while gravity acts on the proton norm $m_p$ (Axiom 4 cost balance), so this norm is chosen. The critical luminosity is therefore substituted by the balance condition of the two costs. Why this norm: the Eddington Luminosity itself originates from the RLU Radiation-Gravity Cost Balance, so only a norm that carries that very cost structure maps onto it without slack. Why the substitution is valid: the standard physics form on the left and the Banya form on the right denote the same quantity, the latter merely rewriting it in CAS cost terms.

$\dfrac{L\sigma_T}{4\pi r^2 c} = \dfrac{G M m_p}{r^2}$
$L_\text{Edd} = \dfrac{4\pi G M m_p c}{\sigma_T}$
$L_\text{Edd}$: Eddington luminosity | $G$: cost-transfer constant | $M$: FSM norm | $m_p$: proton norm | $c$: radiation-propagation limit | $\sigma_T$: Thomson-scattering cost

Step 4. Domain Transformation

The transformation is into the cosmological accretion-luminosity domain. What it reveals is the critical luminosity per solar mass $L_\text{Edd}$. Dividing the four balance constants by the Thomson cost yields that value. Why this domain: the Eddington Luminosity itself is the observable measured in this domain, so the cost structure must be projected here to be compared directly with experiment.
