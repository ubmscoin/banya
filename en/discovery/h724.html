v1.8 interpretation of the γ factor: γ = ratio of system time to domain time. In an FSM in high-speed motion, per one tick of system time the domain time shrinks to 1/γ (direct corollary of H-723). The FSM itself does not change.

Within any finite domain time the system time must advance infinitely, so macroscopic acceleration becomes impossible — the v1.8 structural ground for the speed-of-light barrier. It is not the cost that diverges; it is the domain-time ratio that diverges.

This hypothesis is consistent with that measurement — the cost-divergence interpretation and the domain-time-conversion-ratio interpretation converge to the same γ value.

Why the library wording "kinetic cost is summed into the FSM norm" conflicts with v1.8: the FSM is a closed circuit (Axiom 14), so no cost can be added externally to enlarge the norm. Kinetic energy input is absorbed by adjusting the domain-time ratio, and the result is what a macroscopic observer measures as "mass increase." The phenomenon is something that happens in the time region, not in the mass region.
