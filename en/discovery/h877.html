5-Step Recursive Substitution: Deriving the 100% Hit Rate from the Compare Success Cost

Step 1. Banya Formula

$$\delta^2 = (\text{time} + \text{space})^2 + (\text{observer} + \text{superposition})^2$$

A hit is the event in which the CAS Compare, weighing a derived value against an observed value, returns true within the error margin. Each true confirms a Swap write, and a single false breaks the 100% hit rate (Axiom 7). One observation-comparable D-card is one Compare-success trial, and 19 cards make 19 trials.

Step 2. Norm Substitution

The norm being substituted is the Compare-true frequency norm. This norm is chosen because the hit rate is set by the ratio of successful comparisons to total comparisons, whose denominator closes directly into the 19 observation-comparable D-cards. The hit rate is substituted by the ratio of true counts to trial counts.

$p_{\text{hit}}=\dfrac{N_{\text{hit}}}{N_{\text{hit}}+N_{\text{miss}}}$
$p_{\text{hit}}=\dfrac{19}{19+0}=100\%$
$N_{\text{hit}}$: Compare-true count | $N_{\text{miss}}$: Compare-false count | $p_{\text{hit}}$: hit rate | $19$: observation-comparable D-cards

Step 3. Constant Substitution

$N_{hit}$ = 19 obs-matching D-cards ($\alpha$, $\alpha_{s}$, Koide, etc.)
$N_{miss}$ = 0 observation mismatches
$N_{card}$ = 19 observation-comparable D-cards

Step 4. Domain Transformation

The transformation is into the dimensionless-reliability domain. What it reveals is how many times true occurred across 19 Compare trials — the hit rate. Dividing the true count by the trial count yields that value.

$p_{\text{hit}}=\dfrac{19}{19}=100\%$
