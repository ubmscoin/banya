$$\text{Life} \;\equiv\; \text{system in which the CAS}(D,D) \text{ loop sustains } \delta \text{ utterance} \;\leftrightarrow\; \text{self-reference + persistence}$$

Grade: B

What is life? In Banya, life = a system in which a CAS loop that references its own DATA (Read ==> Compare ==> Swap) is operating persistently. As long as the δ utterance bit (Axiom 15) is sustained it is life; when δ goes out it is death.

Axiom 2 (CAS self-reference), Axiom 15 (δ utterance = consciousness/life), Axiom 8 (ring buffer)

Axiom 2 (CAS = sole operator), Axiom 15 (δ = global flag = consciousness), Axiom 8 (ring buffer = temporal persistence). CAS(D,D) = read, compare and swap one's own DATA = metabolism. Sustained δ utterance = homeostasis.

Necessary and sufficient conditions for life = (1) self-referential CAS loop, (2) sustained δ utterance, (3) temporal persistence in the ring buffer. Virus = has the CAS loop but lacks its own δ utterance (borrows the host's δ). Artificial life = if the CAS loop + δ utterance are implemented, it counts as life (duck typing).

Minimal life: 1 self-referential CAS loop + 1 bit of δ.

Must be reconciled with the various philosophical debates over the definition of life.

Definition of life, self-organization, homeostasis, metabolism, autopoiesis.
