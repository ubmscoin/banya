P-59: CPT Violation $\sim\alpha^8\sim10^{-17}$

$$|\text{CPT 위반}|\sim\alpha^8\sim10^{-17}\quad(\text{CAS 8비트 링버퍼 1주기 비용})$$

Prediction: the CPT-violation magnitude is $\sim\alpha^8\sim10^{-17}$. Test: ALPHA/BASE antihydrogen (2030~).

In the Banya formula $\delta^2=(\text{time}+\text{space})^2+(\text{observer}+\text{superposition})^2$, CPT symmetry corresponds to the complete cycle of the 8-bit d-ring buffer — one full turn returns charge, parity, and time to their places (Axiom 15).

CPT violation is the tiny cost accumulated over one turn of this 8-bit ring.

Since one period of the 8-bit ring passes the CAS selection cost $\alpha$ eight times, the CPT-violation magnitude is $\alpha^8\sim10^{-17}$.

This is the same scale that arises from the same 8-bit cycle as the strong-CP violation $\theta_\text{QCD}=\alpha^8$ (P-25) ==> the bit count 8 enters the exponent directly.

Since $\alpha\approx1/137$, $\alpha^8\sim10^{-17}$ — extremely small compared with current sensitivity.

So this prediction is not "CPT nearly holds" but the quantitative claim "violation is exactly at the $\alpha^8$ scale."

Many quantum-gravity models predict larger CPT violation (e.g. linear Planck suppression), close to current sensitivity — this is the discriminator.

Observational strategy: compare the spectroscopy, charge, and magnetic moment of antihydrogen (antiproton + positron) with hydrogen to measure CPT violation.

Test: if ALPHA/BASE (2030~) narrow CPT violation to the $10^{-17}$ scale in the antihydrogen-hydrogen comparison, this prediction is supported.

Refutation: if CPT violation disagrees with $10^{-17}$ by more than $3\sigma$ (especially a larger value), this prediction is discarded.

Step 1. Banya Formula

$$\delta^2 = (\text{time} + \text{space})^2 + (\text{observer} + \text{superposition})^2$$

CPT violation is the fine cost accumulated over one lap of this 8-bit ring, and the selection cost of one bit is the CAS Compare cost $\alpha$ (Axiom 2).

Step 2. Norm Substitution

(a) The substituted norm is the one-cycle norm of the 8-bit ring buffer. (b) CPT violation accumulates through 8 CAS selections over one lap of the ring, and each selection pays the coupling cost $\alpha$, so this norm is chosen. The CPT violation magnitude is thus substituted by $\alpha$ raised to the 8th power.

$|\Delta_\text{CPT}| \sim \alpha^{b}$
$|\Delta_\text{CPT}| \sim \alpha^{8} \sim 10^{-17}$
$\Delta_\text{CPT}$: CPT violation magnitude | $\alpha$: fine-structure constant $\approx 1/137$ | $b=8$: d-ring ring-buffer bits (Axiom 15) | $\alpha^8$: same scale as strong CP violation $\theta_\text{QCD}$ (P-25)
