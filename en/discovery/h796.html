Step 1. Banya Formula

Lepton mixing is the phase difference that arises when the three generations of observer utter δ at different instants. Since the observer is fixed by the δ utterance (Axiom 15), the inter-generation utterance-time difference becomes the PMNS mixing angle directly. Unlike quark mixing (H-795), which is a CAS Compare cost, lepton mixing is a phase difference of the observer itself — that is the starting premise.

Step 2. Norm Substitution

The norm being substituted is the observer phase norm. Because leptons couple directly to the observer (Axiom 15) and its utterance phase difference sets the mixing, this norm — not the CAS Compare norm — is chosen. The observer phase difference exceeds the CAS step cost, so the mixing angles appear larger than CKM. The PMNS mixing is therefore substituted by a sine function of the utterance phase difference $\Delta\phi_{ij}$.

$U_{\text{PMNS}} = R_{23}(\theta_{23})\,R_{13}(\theta_{13})\,R_{12}(\theta_{12})$
$\sin^2\theta_{ij} = \sin^2(\Delta\phi_{ij}),\qquad \theta_{23}\to\tfrac{\pi}{4}\ \text{(orthogonal utterance phase)}$
$U_{\text{PMNS}}$: lepton mixing matrix | $\theta_{ij}$: mixing angle of generations $i,j$ | $\Delta\phi_{ij}$: observer utterance phase difference (Axiom 15) | $R_{ij}$: rotation
