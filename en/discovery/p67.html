P-67: Landauer Limit = CAS Irreversibility

$$E_{\min}(\text{bit erasure})=kT\ln 2\quad(\text{Landauer limit}=\text{minimum dissipation of CAS irreversible operation})$$

Prediction: the minimum energy dissipation to erase 1 bit $=kT\ln 2$ (Landauer limit = CAS irreversibility). Test: nanoscale thermodynamics (2027~).

In the Banya formula $\delta^2=(\text{time}+\text{space})^2+(\text{observer}+\text{superposition})^2$, the Swap step of CAS is irreversible (Axiom 2 proposition: a completed CAS operation cannot be undone).

An irreversible operation erases information, and erasing information requires dissipating a minimum energy to the environment.

The minimum dissipation energy to erase 1 bit is the Landauer limit $kT\ln 2$ ==> the thermodynamic cost of CAS irreversibility.

$\ln 2$ is the information content of 1 bit (the two-way Compare branch, P-45), and $kT$ is the environment temperature scale.

So the Landauer limit is not a separate postulate of information theory but a direct consequence of CAS Swap irreversibility (Axiom 4 cost).

Reversible operations (computations that can be undone) do not erase information and can avoid this cost, but CAS Swap is intrinsically irreversible.

So this prediction is not "information erasure costs energy" but the quantitative claim "exactly $kT\ln 2$ is the floor, below which is impossible."

This is in the same CAS-irreversibility family as dark energy $w=-1$ (P-33: COLD irreversible) and the arrow of time (P-81).
