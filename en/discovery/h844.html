Core Finding

H-844: Transistor = Current Gating by CAS Compare

$$I_{DS} = \mu C_{ox}\frac{W}{L}\left[(V_{GS}-V_{th})V_{DS} - \frac{V_{DS}^2}{2}\right] \;\;\text{(CAS Compare ==> gate control)}$$

Grade: B

A transistor is a three-terminal switch in which CAS Compare, driven by the gate voltage, either permits or blocks the Swap between source and drain. Gate = the external control input to CAS Compare. Source-drain = the Swap path.

Axiom 2 (CAS Compare = gate verdict), Axiom 4 proposition (FSM norm), Axiom 4 (cost = current)

Axiom 5: CAS Compare compares $V_{GS}$ with $V_{th}$ ==> decides channel formation/dissolution. Compare result = ON/OFF. Axiom 3: the FSM norm fixes the carrier density inside the channel. Axiom 7: Swap cost = $I_{DS} \times V_{DS}$. The MOSFET gate oxide = the insulating barrier of CAS Compare.

Amplification: a small change in $V_{GS}$ ==> a large change in $I_{DS}$. Switching: $V_{GS} > V_{th}$ ==> ON, $V_{GS} < V_{th}$ ==> OFF. CMOS: an nMOS + pMOS complementary pair = duplexed CAS Compare. Scaling: shrinking the channel length $L$ ==> higher speed.

Modern MOSFET: $L \sim 3$~$5\;\text{nm}$. $V_{th} \sim 0.2$~$0.5\;\text{V}$. Switching speed: $\sim\;\text{GHz}$~$\text{THz}$. Leakage current: $\sim\;\text{nA}$~$\mu\text{A}$.

MOSFET I-V model agrees with measurement to $< 10\%$ (when short-channel effects are included).

Transistor switching is the decision cost by which the CAS Compare turns the channel Swap on and off, comparing the gate voltage to the threshold. Axiom 5 (TOCTOU lock register) lets the Compare decide ON/OFF by comparing $V_{GS}$ to $V_{th}$, and Axiom 7 (write and superposition-hold) splits that result into channel formation (write) and blocking (hold). This Compare cost is read in gate-voltage units.

Step 2. Norm Substitution

The norm being substituted is the CAS Compare cost norm. In the subthreshold region the channel occupancy follows a Boltzmann distribution in gate voltage, and that distribution closes into the thermal energy $k_BT$ of Axiom 4 (cost), so this norm is chosen. It is substituted by the gate voltage needed to raise the current by one decade -- the subthreshold slope. Why this norm: the MOSFET Subthreshold Slope itself originates from CAS Compare Cost, so only a norm that carries that very cost structure maps onto it without slack. Why the substitution is valid: the standard physics form on the left and the Banya form on the right denote the same quantity, the latter merely rewriting it in CAS cost terms.

$SS = \dfrac{k_B T}{q}\ln 10$
