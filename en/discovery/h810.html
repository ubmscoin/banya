Core Finding

H-810: OPE = Short-Distance Expansion of the CAS Operation

$$\mathcal{O}_A(x)\mathcal{O}_B(0) \sim \sum_n C_n(x)\mathcal{O}_n(0) \;\leftrightarrow\; \text{CAS}(A,B) = \sum_n \text{cost}_n \cdot \text{CAS}_n$$

Grade: C

The Operator Product Expansion (OPE) is a technique that expands the product of two local operators at short distance as a sum of other operators. In Banya, when CAS(A,B) is performed at short distance (high energy), the cost decomposes into multiple CAS modes.

Axiom 2 (CAS operation), Axiom 4 (cost decomposition)

Axiom 2 (CAS = operation), Axiom 4 (cost +1). When two CAS operations act on the same DATA simultaneously (short distance), the total cost decomposes into the costs of the individual CAS modes (Read, Compare, Swap). The Wilson coefficient $C_n(x)$ = cost weight of each mode.

OPE is a core tool in QCD sum rules, deep inelastic scattering, parton distribution functions, etc. If CAS cost decomposition is the structural origin of OPE, the Wilson coefficients must be computable from CAS costs.

The norm being substituted is the CAS cost-decomposition norm. It is chosen because the short-distance product is the weighted sum of the three mode costs, and those weights close through the per-mode bit costs. The operator-product expansion is therefore substituted by the sum of the three CAS mode costs. Why this norm: the OPE Wilson Coefficients itself originates from the CAS Cost Decomposition, so only a norm that carries that very cost structure maps onto it without slack.
