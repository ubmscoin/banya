Step 1. Banya Formula

$$\delta^2 = (\text{time} + \text{space})^2 + (\text{observer} + \text{superposition})^2$$

The cost of an operation is decided by whether the CAS executes a Swap. Read and Compare are reversible steps that do not change the state, and only Swap crosses the + ordering to incur cost $+1$ (Axiom 2, Axiom 4). Avoiding Swap costs 0; executing it dissipates heat (Axiom 6).

Step 2. Norm Substitution

(a) The norm being substituted is the Swap irreversibility norm. A Compare-only path is reversible at cost 0, while a path including Swap is irreversible with a lower bound of $k_BT\ln 2$ per bit. (b) Since reversible computation is a CAS that drops Swap and cycles Compare only, and irreversible erasure is precisely Swap execution, the presence of Swap divides the cost bound — which is why this norm is chosen. Computation cost is therefore substituted by the Swap irreversibility norm. Why this norm: the Zero-Cost Bound of Reversible Computation itself originates from the CAS Swap Cost, so only a norm that carries that very cost structure maps onto it without slack. Why the substitution is valid: the standard physics form on the left and the Banya form on the right denote the same quantity, the latter merely rewriting it in CAS cost terms.

$\text{Swap}\ \text{included}:\ E \geq k_BT\ln 2$
