H-709: Photoelastic Effect = Stress-Induced Domain Bit Deformation

The photoelastic effect is the phenomenon in which mechanical stress anisotropically alters the CAS cost propagation speed (refractive index) of a medium. An isotropic medium exhibits birefringence under stress.

5-Step Recursive Substitution: Deriving Photoelastic Birefringence from Stress-Induced Anisotropy of CAS Cost Propagation

Step 1. Banya Formula

$$\delta^2 = (\text{time} + \text{space})^2 + (\text{observer} + \text{superposition})^2$$

The photoelastic effect is the cost by which mechanical stress changes the CAS cost-propagation speed (refractive index) in a medium direction by direction (Axiom 4 cost-propagation speed = medium CAS-density dependence). Polarization is the domain-bit direction (H-696), and when the ECS lattice deforms under stress the cost propagation becomes anisotropic (Axiom 7).

Step 2. Norm Substitution

(a) The norm being substituted is the stress-index coupling norm, i.e. the photoelastic tensor $C_{ijkl}$. (b) An isotropic medium has the same cost-propagation speed for every domain-bit direction, but under a principal stress the domain bits split along the principal-stress directions into two refractive indices, so the index-change norm is substituted linearly in the stress tensor.

$\Delta n_{ij} = C_{ijkl}\,\sigma_{kl}$
$\Delta n = C\,(\sigma_1 - \sigma_2)$
$\Delta n_{ij}$: index-change tensor | $C_{ijkl}$: photoelastic tensor | $\sigma_{kl}$: stress tensor | $\sigma_1,\sigma_2$: principal stresses | $C$: stress-optic constant

Step 3. Constant Substitution

C(glass)  = 2.7e-12 Pa^-1        2.7 TPa^-1, photoelastic stress analysis
C(epoxy)  = 50e-12 Pa^-1         50 TPa^-1
sigma     = 10 MPa = 1e7 Pa      example stress

Step 4. Domain Transformation

(a) The transformation is into the photoelastic stress-analysis domain (fringe pattern). (b) What it reveals is the birefringence $\Delta n$ produced by a given stress — the quantity from which the stress distribution is reconstructed via the fringe pattern. A $10\,\text{MPa}$ stress on glass is substituted.

$\Delta n = 2.7\times10^{-12}\times10^{7} = 2.7\times10^{-5}$

Step 5. Discovery

Derived: $\Delta n = 2.7\times10^{-5}$ (glass, $10\,\text{MPa}$)
Measured: $C_{\text{glass}}\approx2.7\,\text{TPa}^{-1}$, photoelastic stress analysis
