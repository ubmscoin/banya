H-832: Entropy and Life = Cost Paid for Local Entropy Reduction

$$\Delta S_{\text{local}} < 0 \;\Rightarrow\; \Delta S_{\text{env}} > |\Delta S_{\text{local}}| \;\leftrightarrow\; \text{local order maintained by paying CAS cost}$$

Grade: B

A living organism reduces entropy locally (maintaining order), and in return increases the entropy of its environment. In Banya, by paying the CAS cost (Axiom 4) the local order of DATA is maintained, and the paid cost is released to the environment (RLU decay).

Axiom 4 (cost +1 = entropy cost), Axiom 11 (RLU decay), H-817 (life)

Axiom 4 (CAS cost = entropy generation), Axiom 11 (RLU = cost decay = released to environment). Life = continuously paying the CAS cost to maintain local DATA order. Schrodinger's "eating negative entropy" = the ability to pay the CAS cost.

5-Step Recursive Substitution: Deriving the human-body entropy release rate from the CAS cost payment

Step 1. Banya Formula

$$\delta^2 = (\text{time} + \text{space})^2 + (\text{observer} + \text{superposition})^2$$

Life is the irreversible cost (Axiom 4: cost $+1$) that the CAS pays to maintain the local order of the DATA. This cost, accumulating in the $(\text{time}+\text{space})$ term of the Banya formula, is read as the amount released to the environment through RLU decay (Axiom 11).

Step 2. Norm Substitution

The norm being substituted is the irreversible-cost norm. It is chosen because local entropy reduction is possible only by paying the CAS cost, and that cost is the closure released to the environment through RLU decay (Axiom 11), increasing the global entropy. The order maintenance of life is therefore substituted by the sum of the local negative entropy and the larger environmental positive entropy.

$\Delta S_{\text{local}} < 0,\quad \Delta S_{\text{env}} > |\Delta S_{\text{local}}|$
$\dfrac{dS_{\text{env}}}{dt} = \dfrac{P}{T}$
$\Delta S_{\text{local}}$: local order (maintained by CAS cost) | $\Delta S_{\text{env}}$: environmental release (RLU decay, Axiom 11) | $P$: metabolic rate = CAS cost payment rate | $T$: absolute temperature

Step 3. Constant Substitution

P = 80 W              human basal metabolic rate
T = 310 K             body temperature 37 C
dS_env/dt = P / T     entropy release rate

Step 4. Domain Transformation

The transformation is into the macroscopic-thermodynamic domain (the Banya structure itself, the first-order integer (4,3) combination). What it reveals is the entropy per unit time released to the environment while the body maintains local order — the macroscopic scale of the CAS cost payment. Dividing the metabolic rate by the body temperature yields that value.

$\dfrac{dS_{\text{env}}}{dt} = \dfrac{80}{310} \approx 0.26\ \text{W/K}$

Step 5. Discovery

Derived: $dS_{\text{env}}/dt \approx 0.26\ \text{W/K}$
Measured: $\sim 1\ \text{W/K}$ (human entropy release)
Error: same order of magnitude
