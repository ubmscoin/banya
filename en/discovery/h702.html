Core Finding

H-702: Plasmon Resonance = Surface CAS Cost Collective Oscillation

$$\omega_{\text{sp}} = \frac{\omega_p}{\sqrt{1 + \epsilon_d}} \;\leftrightarrow\; \text{surface CAS cost collective mode}$$

Grade: C

Surface plasmon resonance is the phenomenon in which the collective CAS cost oscillation of free electrons at a metal–dielectric boundary is confined to and propagates along the surface. The localized plasmon of a nanoparticle = a confined collective cost oscillation.

Axiom 4 (CAS collective cost), Axiom 7 (ECS surface boundary)

Axiom 4 (CAS cost collective excitation = plasmon), Axiom 7 (ECS boundary condition ==> surface mode), Axiom 2 (collective motion of free electrons in metallic CAS)

Dispersion relation: surface plasmon polariton (SPP). Propagation length: $L \sim 10\text{--}100\;\mu\text{m}$. Penetration depth: metal $\sim 25\;\text{nm}$, dielectric $\sim 200\;\text{nm}$. SERS enhancement: factor of $|E/E_0|^4$.

Au nanoparticle at $50\;\text{nm}$: $\lambda_{\text{res}} \approx 520\;\text{nm}$. Ag: $\lambda_{\text{res}} \approx 400\;\text{nm}$.

Consistent with SERS and nano-optics experiments.

Surface plasmon, localized plasmon resonance, SERS, nano-optics.

5-Step Recursive Substitution: Deriving the Surface-Plasmon Resonance Frequency from the Surface Collective Cost Oscillation

Step 1. Banya Formula

$$\delta^2 = (\text{time} + \text{space})^2 + (\text{observer} + \text{superposition})^2$$

A surface plasmon is the surface cost mode in which the collective cost excitation of metal free electrons (Axiom 4) is confined to and propagates along the metal-dielectric boundary (Axiom 7 ECS boundary condition). The boundary condition splits the bulk mode into a surface mode.

Step 2. Norm Substitution

(a) The norm being substituted is the resonance norm of the free-electron collective cost oscillation — the surface frequency obtained by pressing the bulk plasma frequency $\omega_p$ down by the boundary dielectric response. (b) It is chosen because the surface cost mode feels the dielectric response of both metal and dielectric simultaneously, so the boundary condition forces a surface resonance equal to the bulk $\omega_p$ divided by $\sqrt{1+\epsilon_d}$. Why this norm: the Surface-Plasmon Resonance Frequency itself originates from the Surface Collective Cost Oscillation, so only a norm that carries that very cost structure maps onto it without slack. Why the substitution is valid: the standard physics form on the left and the Banya form on the right denote the same quantity, the latter merely rewriting it in CAS cost terms.

$\omega_{\text{sp}} = \dfrac{\omega_p}{\sqrt{1 + \epsilon_d}}$
$\lambda_{\text{res}} = \dfrac{2\pi c}{\omega_{\text{sp}}}$
$\omega_{\text{sp}}$: surface-plasmon frequency | $\omega_p$: bulk plasma frequency | $\epsilon_d$: dielectric permittivity | $\lambda_{\text{res}}$: resonance wavelength | $c$: cost-wave speed

This step substitutes concrete constant values for the symbols of the Banya relation above. Every value inserted is already fixed by a prior D/H/P card or axiom, not set anew in this card. Step 4. Domain Transformation

(a) The domain transformed into is the nanoparticle absorption-spectrum domain. (b) What it reveals is the localized-plasmon resonance wavelength $\lambda_{\text{res}}$. Converting from the surface frequency to the resonance wavelength yields that value. Why this domain: the Surface-Plasmon Resonance Frequency itself is the observable measured in this domain, so the cost structure must be projected here to be compared directly with experiment. $\lambda_{\text{res}}(\text{Ag}) \approx 400\;\text{nm}$
