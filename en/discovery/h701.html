5-Step Recursive Substitution: Deriving the Optical-Lattice Potential Depth from Standing-Wave Cost Interference

Step 1. Banya Formula

$$\delta^2 = (\text{time} + \text{space})^2 + (\text{observer} + \text{superposition})^2$$

An optical lattice is the cost potential by which the standing wave formed from two counter-propagating cost waves (H-698) interfering (H-695) traps atoms at cost minima. This trapping is confinement by a cost potential (Axiom 4), and the spatial period of the cost wave becomes the lattice period.

Step 2. Norm Substitution

(a) The norm being substituted is the squared-amplitude norm of the cost wave — the standing-wave intensity $\sin^2(kx)$. (b) It is chosen because the node-antinode structure of the standing wave imprints a periodic cost potential in space and atoms are trapped at the cost minima (antinodes). The lattice potential is therefore substituted by a periodic function proportional to the cost-wave intensity.

$V(x) = V_0\sin^2(kx)$
$d = \dfrac{\lambda}{2}, \quad k = \dfrac{2\pi}{\lambda}$
$V(x)$: lattice potential | $V_0$: lattice depth (proportional to cost-wave intensity) | $k$: cost-wave wavenumber | $d$: lattice spacing | $\lambda$: cost-wave wavelength

Step 3. Constant Substitution

lambda = 800 nm          typical lattice laser wavelength
V_0    = 1--30 E_R        E_R = recoil energy
d      = lambda/2         lattice spacing

Step 4. Domain Transformation

(a) The domain transformed into is the cold-atom spatial-lattice domain. (b) What it reveals is the distance between adjacent cost minima — the lattice spacing $d$. Since the standing-wave period is half the cost-wave wavelength, this yields $d=\lambda/2$.

$d = \dfrac{\lambda}{2} = \dfrac{800\;\text{nm}}{2} = 400\;\text{nm}$

Step 5. Discovery

Derived lattice spacing: $d = 400\;\text{nm}$
Measured lattice spacing: $d \approx 400\;\text{nm}$ (Greiner 2002)
Lattice depth: $V_0 \sim 1\text{--}30\;E_R$ (consistent)

Verdict: Hypothesis. The lattice spacing and depth are consistent with the standing-wave cost structure, but the absolute scale of $V_0$ remains an external laser-intensity input, so the final step of the forward chain does not close.
