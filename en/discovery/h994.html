H-994. Koide phase-departure ratio: $d\theta/dQ = -15/16$

Key finding

H-994: in the Koide scale departure, the ratio in which the phase and $Q$ share one quantum matches the registered-number ratio $-15/16$

$$\frac{d\theta}{dQ}\Big|_{M_Z} = -0.93768 \pm 0.002 \quad\text{vs}\quad -\frac{15}{16} = -0.93750 \;(+0.02\%)$$

Hypothesis -- a 0.02% hit, but the lattice-density chance expectation is 0.30, so not decisive; the structural assignment is at the candidate stage

From the same input as the scale departure $dQ = \alpha/(2\pi)$ of H-992 (the Schwinger share), the phase departure is $d\theta(M_Z) = -1.1796\times10^{-3}$ against $dQ = +1.2580\times10^{-3}$: nearly equal in size, opposite in sign. The precise ratio is $-0.93768$ (XZZ $M_Z$ masses; $\pm0.002$ under tau $\pm0.15$ MeV). The naive candidate $-1$ (the full share reversed) is excluded at $-6.7\%$, while $-15/16$ hits at $+0.02\%$.

Structural reading (candidate): $16 = 2^4$ is the domain 4-axis combination count (structural registry), and $15 = 16 - 1$ excludes the single identity combination. That is, the phase receives one Schwinger share in the opposite direction, minus the $1/16$ share of the identity combination that does not participate in rotation.

Re-substitution use: the candidate that closes the phase component of the H-992 departure chain; an agenda item for refining the phase-antisymmetry paragraph in Paper IV v2.

Why it stops at Hypothesis: the lattice-density quantification (13 closed-list quotients in the 0.90-0.98 window; chance expectation 0.30 for a 0.1% window) does not clear the decision threshold, and the identity-combination exclusion has no axiomatic derivation.
