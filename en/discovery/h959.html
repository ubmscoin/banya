Core Finding

H-959: Viscoelasticity = Fractional-order Stress-Strain (between spring 0 and dashpot 1)

$$\sigma(t) = E\,\tau^{\alpha}\, D^{\alpha}\varepsilon(t),\qquad \alpha=0:\ \text{spring},\quad \alpha=1:\ \text{dashpot}$$

Structural description — interpolation between elastic (α=0) and viscous (α=1), half = α=1/2 (Scott-Blair). Polymer / soft-tissue form agreement

The stress-strain law of a viscoelastic material is the fractional-order $\sigma = E\tau^{\alpha}D^{\alpha}\varepsilon$. α=0 = perfectly elastic (spring, proportional to strain), α=1 = perfectly viscous (dashpot, proportional to strain rate). Banya: $D^{\alpha}$ = orthogonal rotation $\alpha\pi/2$ (H-953), so α is a rotation position between the elastic and viscous categories.

Half-order α=1/2 = 50:50 elastic-viscous = the Scott-Blair element. This is the mechanical version of the "between two categories" interpolation seen in H-953, the universal pattern by which fractional derivatives interpolate two poles (isomorphic to diffusion H-958 and statistics H-954).

Quantitatively unfinished: α is material-dependent (not an axiom integer) ==> hypothesis.
