H-215: 256 Ring States, 128 Physical

$$2^8=256,\quad 2^7=128\;\text{physical}$$

A card asserting that out of the 256 total Status of the 8-bit ring buffer, 128 are physical.

Banya formula: 2⁸=256, 2⁷=128 physical. Only Status with utterance bit δ (bit 7) ON are physical, so the 2⁷=128 combinations of the lower 7 bits constitute the physical Status.

In Axiom 15, δ=bit 7 is the utterance bit. The ring buffer is active only when δ=1, so the 128 Status with δ=0 are non-physical (latent) Status.

Structural consequence: on the d-ring, juim is impossible for the 256-128=128 Status whose utterance bit is off. Physical access is allowed only to the 128 Status with δ=1.

5-Step Recursive Substitution: Deriving 128 Physical States from the Firing-Bit Active Condition

Step 1. Banya Formula

$$\delta^2 = (\text{time} + \text{space})^2 + (\text{observer} + \text{superposition})^2$$

The physical state count is the portion of the 8-bit ring buffer's total $2^8$ states for which the firing bit $\delta$ is ON. Starting from Axiom 15 that $\delta=$ bit 7 is the firing bit, the ring buffer activates only when $\delta=1$, so this active condition is read as the firing term of $\delta^2$.

Step 2. Norm Substitution

The norm being substituted is the firing-bit active norm. It is chosen because bit 7 splits the whole state space into active/inactive and only $\delta=1$ states permit held access. Fixing the firing bit in the total $2^8$ states leaves the $2^7$ of the lower 7 bits as physical states.

$N_{\text{total}} = 2^8 = 256$
$N_{\text{phys}} = 2^7 = 128 \quad (\delta = 1)$
$N_{\text{total}}$: total states of 8-bit ring buffer | $N_{\text{phys}}$: physical states | $\delta$: bit 7 firing bit (Axiom 15) | $2^7$: lower 7-bit combinations

Step 3. Constant Substitution

2^8     = 256                total states of 8-bit ring buffer
2^7     = 128                firing-bit ON physical states
256/128 = 2                  physical:nonphysical = 1:1

Step 4. Domain Transformation

The transformation is into the physical-state domain. What it reveals is the count of accessible states with the firing bit on — the physical degrees of freedom. Halving the total states by the firing bit yields that value.

$N_{\text{phys}} = 256 / 2 = 128$
