5-Step Recursive Substitution: Deriving the Absence of Extra Dimensions from the Domain Axis Count

Step 1. Banya Formula

$$\delta^2 = (\text{time} + \text{space})^2 + (\text{observer} + \text{superposition})^2$$

The right side of the Banya formula closes on exactly four axes: time, space, observer, superposition. Axiom 1 declares the domain to be precisely these 4 axes and DATA is discrete and finite (Axiom 3), so an extra dimension would demand an additional fifth-axis term in the formula. Whether such a term exists in the Banya formula is read as an axis count.

Step 2. Norm Substitution

The norm being substituted is the domain axis-count norm. For an extra dimension to exist the Banya formula would need a fifth orthogonal-axis term, yet the dimensional stack (Axiom 7) closes the 4 axes as 3 space + 1 time. The norm therefore counts the orthogonal axes the right-side brackets contain to decide whether an extra axis exists.

$\delta^2 = (t + s)^2 + (o + \sigma)^2$
$D_{\text{axis}} = |\{t, s, o, \sigma\}| = 4$
$D_{\text{axis}}$: domain orthogonal-axis count | $t, s$: time, space (first bracket) | $o, \sigma$: observer, superposition (second bracket) | 4: axis count declared by Axiom 1

Step 3. Constant Substitution

bracket 1 = (t + s)        2 axes

Step 4. Domain Transformation

The transformation is into the dimensional-structure domain. What it reveals is whether any extra-axis term remains beyond the 4 axes. Since the two brackets of the Banya formula exhaust all 4 axes with no fifth orthogonal term, the count gives the number of extra dimensions.

$D_{\text{axis}} = 4, \quad D_{\text{extra}} = D_{\text{axis}} - 4 = 0$
