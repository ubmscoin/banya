H-993. Registry Decomposition of Quark Koide Phases: $\delta_U = (2/9)/3$, $\delta_D = (2/9)(2/3)$

Core Finding

H-993: both quark-sector Koide-type phases decompose exactly into registry-number combinations of the lepton Koide angle $2/9$

$$\delta_U = \frac{2}{27} = \frac{2/9}{3}, \qquad \delta_D = \frac{4}{27} = \frac{2}{9}\cdot\frac{2}{3} = 2\,\delta_U$$

Hypothesis -- the arithmetic decomposition is exact (as reported in the source), the structural assignment (why one third and why twice) is at candidate stage

Zenczykowski (arXiv 1301.4143, Eq. 9) reports that at the low-energy scale the up-type phase is $\delta_U \approx 2/27 = \delta_L/3$ and the down-type $\delta_D \approx 4/27 = 2\delta_L/3$. Both decompose using registry numbers only: up-type is the Koide angle $2/9$ (H-22, D-45) divided by the 3 CAS steps (Axiom 2); down-type is the Koide angle times the Koide ratio $2/3$, i.e. twice the up-type via the bracket count 2 (Axiom 1).

Library-internal consistency already exists: D-22 uses the same decomposition $4/27 = (2/9)(2/3)$ for PMNS $\theta_{13}$. The down-type quark phase and the reactor mixing angle share one registry combination, adding the fourth and fifth sites to the H-22 convergence list for $2/9$.

Why it stops at hypothesis: the arithmetic closes exactly on the reported values, but the structural assignment (why the CAS third for up-type, why the bracket doubling for down-type) is a candidate. The D-promotion path is deriving it from the cost asymmetry of Compare true/false paths.
