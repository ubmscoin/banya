Core Finding

H-337: Free-will illusion = the observer mistakes δ utterance for its own choice

$$\text{observer sees only filtered output}\;\Rightarrow\;\text{attributes }\delta\text{ fire to self}$$

The "illusion" of free will is derived from the structure in which the observer mistakes a δ utterance for its own choice. The observer sees only filter output (Axiom 10), and δ utterances themselves, being outside the FSM, are invisible.

Banya formula: the observer sees only filter output $\Rightarrow$ attributes δ utterances to self. Free will = self-attribution of filter output.

Axiomatic grounds: Axiom 10 (observer = filter, observes only filter output) defines the observation restriction. Axiom 15 (δ = outside FSM, invisible to the observer) ensures δ's invisibility.

Structural consequence: the observer interprets the output of its own filter as "my choice". The δ utterance is the actual source, but since δ is invisible, the filtered result feels as though one made it. This is the structural origin of the "feeling" of free will.

Numerics: information accessible to the observer: filter output ($k$ of 7 bits). Inaccessible information: δ utterance itself (bit 7). Information asymmetry = $k/(k+1)$.

Consistency: H-336 (will = δ==>observer asymmetric transmission) supplies the structure of will. H-340 (integrated information Φ) strengthens self-attribution via recursive loops.
