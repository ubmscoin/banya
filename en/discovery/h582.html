5-Step Recursive Substitution: Deriving the Landauer Minimum Erasure Energy from the CAS Irreversible Cost

Step 1. Banya Formula

$$\delta^2 = (\text{time} + \text{space})^2 + (\text{observer} + \text{superposition})^2$$

Erasing 1 bit is the irreversible CAS operation merging two states (0,1) into one. The TOCTOU lock register (Axiom 5) cannot un-lock a once-locked bit, so the cost $+1$ (Axiom 4) is unrecoverable and dissipated as heat. An operation that ends with reversible Swap alone pays no such cost.

Step 2. Norm Substitution

(a) The norm being substituted is the state-count log norm of one d-ring bit — the $\ln 2$ entropy norm. (b) The microstate count lost by irreversible erasure is $2\to1$, i.e. $\ln 2$, which multiplied by temperature closes as the minimum energy; that is why this norm is chosen. The cost $+1$ is substituted by $k_B T\ln 2$.

$E_{\min} = T\,\Delta S = T\,(k_B\ln 2)$
$E_{\min} = k_B T \ln 2$
$E_{\min}$: minimum bit-erasure energy | $\Delta S = k_B\ln 2$: $2\to1$ state-count loss of 1 bit | $T$: absolute temperature | $k_B$: cost-temperature conversion | $\ln 2$: one d-ring bit (Axiom 2)

Step 3. Constant Substitution

$k_{B}$ = $1.380649\times10^{-23}$ J/K
$\ln$ 2 = 0.6931471805599453
T = 300 K room temperature
$E_{min}$ = $k_{B}$ T $\ln$ 2 Landauer 1961

Step 4. Domain Transformation

(a) The transformation is into the information-thermodynamics domain ($\text{bit}\to\text{J}$). (b) What it reveals is the minimum heat an irreversible 1-bit erasure releases to the environment — the Landauer limit. Multiplying $\ln 2$ by $k_B T$ yields that value.

$E_{\min}=k_B T\ln 2=(1.380649\times10^{-23})(300)(0.693147)=2.871\times10^{-21}\ \text{J}$

Step 5. Discovery

Derived: $E_{\min}=2.871\times10^{-21}\ \text{J}$ ($T=300\ \text{K}$)
Measured: $(2.8\pm0.1)\times10^{-21}\ \text{J}$ (Bérut et al. 2012)
Error: $2.5\%$
